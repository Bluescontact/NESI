## A. Reread cluster E's own two laws, and they aren't procedural — they're claim-integrity rules

Law 11 (ONE MANIFEST PER TETRA) doesn't regulate *what* gets built. It regulates how much completion-credit a single structural unit may bank: *"a tetra completes at most one manifest, slice, or build order; a second manifest on the same tetra collides regardless of session or day."* Read plainly, that's a rule against double-claiming worth against the same substrate.

Law 25 (A BUILD SESSION MUST NOT RULE ITS OWN BASE FORK) doesn't regulate what gets decided either. It regulates *who* may price a decision's own legitimacy: *"logging a base-fork ruling carefully does not make it yours to make."* The session closest to the fork — the one with the most at stake in how it resolves — is exactly the one disqualified from ruling it.

Both laws share one shape once you strip the tetra/session vocabulary off them: **an actor may not be the sole assessor of its own claim's worth.** That's not "scope" in the sense of a fence around territory. It's an accounting discipline wearing scope's clothes.

## C. The edge, if it's real: self-pricing is what all six other clusters already forbid, and E is the one place it's forbidden *by name*

Every one of the found vocabularies above converges on one rule: **the party positioned to gain from a claim must not also be the party who prices it.** Brier ledger — reality prices the claim, not the claimant. Recognition chain — a third-person ledger prices the cost a direct letter structurally cannot. THE_TRUSS — load-bearing is computed from declared edges, never asserted by the node itself. `LOAD_BEARING.md` line 25 — the validator must be causally downstream of what it judges, never the same loop.

Law 25 is this rule stated at the level of *authority over a fork*: the build session that stands to benefit from ruling one way is disqualified from ruling at all. Law 11 is this rule stated at the level of *credit over a structural unit*: a tetra may not be double-billed for the same completion. Read this way, cluster E isn't missing an edge to debt/cost/value/worth/recognition — **it may be the cluster where that whole vocabulary gets applied reflexively, to the actor's own claim, rather than outward, to someone else's gift or someone else's cost.** Clusters B and F price *acts and returns*. E prices *who is allowed to do the pricing*. That's not adjacent territory — it's the same instrument pointed at itself, which is exactly the shape law 3 (verification is prestress, not identity) already names for a different pair of laws entirely, and exactly the shape the recognition chain found empirically today, hours before this pass started, without either exercise citing the other.

If this reading holds, the missing lateral edges are **E→B** (self-pricing forbidden is priced-act's own precondition: you can't attach a token to your own act's cost while also being the one who decides whether the token is honest) and **E→F** (a session ruling its own base fork is a world refusing to let anything return upstream of it — the closed loop law 15 exists to break).

## D. The competing read, held open rather than resolved in E's favor

The find in C is real, but it is not the only honest read, and law 17 (divergence is held, never averaged) says both stand rather than one winning by default.

Cost/debt/worth/recognition, everywhere they appear in this corpus (law 21, law 15, brier_ledger, the recognition chain), are **flow** concepts — they describe something moving between two parties across time, and they answer "what went where, and was it repaid." Law 11 and law 25 are not about flow at all. They are **fence** concepts — they answer "where does one unit's authority end and another's begin," a question that doesn't require anything to have moved yet. The five gates already carry this exact distinction in a different vocabulary: gate 2 (ecological accounting) is a flow gate; gate 4 (everyone is a designer, downstream agency) is a fence gate about who may touch what, not about what moved. Nobody reads gate 4 as a special case of gate 2, and nobody would draw an edge between them just because both involve people and consequences.

Under this reading, E's isolation is not a missing wire — it's the diagram correctly showing that **jurisdiction and value-accounting are different kinds of thing**, and forcing an edge between them because both use words like "cost" or "worth" in loose English would be exactly the failure the filters already name: one score, one resource bar, one currency standing in for everything that moves through a system. Cluster E might be tensegrity's compression member proper — independent, touching nothing, held in place only by the keystone's tension (law 20), which is the tensegrity instrument's own point: *"if the parts are structurally independent, the tension between them is the only thing making the whole one object."* An isolated cluster under a keystone is not a defect in that geometry. It may be the geometry.
