## What the second run actually tests

**1. Does the template hold on different material?** Yes, without
modification. Field 1 quoted cleanly from the source; fields 2–4 correctly
left open rather than inferred from context that's actually present (the
note's trust-handoff content is rich enough that a looser extraction could
have been tempted to read "capacity enabled" into it — the admission rule
held anyway).

**2. Is the register-note convention usable?** Yes, on this one entry — it
took one line to check and state ("unprompted, no concern found"), no
friction added. One data point only; still speculative at scale, per the
template's own deferral note.

**3. Does either held-open field-3 reading get evidence?** This is the real
finding of the second run. Change-composite's first-run reading (Pass 3 §5)
was specifically that naming a cost *inside* a letter addressed to the payer
would contradict *that letter's own stated no-debt clause* — a mechanism
that requires the letter to state a no-debt design in the first place (the
three Heartland letters each did: "no debt made of them," "complete on
giving," "nothing owed back"). **This note states no such clause anywhere.**
It is not a no-debt letter; it's a trust-handoff letter that happens to
close with thanks. Cost still comes back open. Cowan's reading (Pass 2 §2)
— that a letter's attention naturally centers on the gift and its landing,
not on what it cost the giver, regardless of whether a no-debt clause is
present — predicts exactly this outcome and doesn't require one. **This one
entry is more consistent with Cowan's reading than with change-composite's**
on the single point where they made genuinely different predictions. One
entry is not enough to close the fork — both readings still stand, per the
template's own rule against over-generalizing from a thin sample — but this
is the first real evidence differentiating them, and it should be carried
into the template's next revision as exactly that: evidence, not a verdict.
