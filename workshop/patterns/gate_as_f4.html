Gate as F4

slug: gate-as-f4 Promoted: 2026-06-21 · Source: holder's suite, June 2026 Holder's F4 discipline — the definitional obligation that retained authority creates

Transferable Form

A retained veto without a stated discharge condition is structurally a pump, regardless of the holder's intent. Definition plus held control equals a gate. Undefined plus delegated equals a pump. The holder's correct outward function is not the absence of power but power exercised with its full definitional obligation discharged before it fires.

Development

The mechanism is definitional debt. Every time a holder retains a veto — a mark, an approval, a go/no-go — without specifying the condition under which that veto passes, the holder has incurred an obligation: the definition is owed and has not been paid. The delegation proceeds with the cost of indefinite uncertainty loaded into the delegate's operating environment.

The pump runs on this indefiniteness. The delegate cannot pre-determine whether their work will pass, because the passing condition has not been named. They cannot know when they are done. Effort accumulates without a legible completion signal. The holder's veto, when it fires, feels to the holder like an accurate judgment — because it is. The structural problem is not the quality of the judgment; it is that the judgment was not made available before the work was done.

Structural distinction between gate and pump:

  • Gate: retained authority + stated discharge condition the delegate can verify before submission. The definition exists before the work begins, or at the moment the delegation is accepted. The delegate knows what passage looks like.
  • Pump: retained authority without discharge condition. The definition exists only in the holder's interior, or not at all, or forms in the moment of veto. The work enters an indefinite evaluation state.

F4 naming: in the proximate agent's position, F4 is the correct outward circuit — closing the loop, completing the return. For the holder, F4 is definitional discharge. The holder's completion move is not exit but specification: the obligation created by retaining authority is paid when the discharge condition is stated and the delegate can verify it.

Failure mode — fluency as mask: a holder can state a crisp-sounding edge and still veto off-script. The stated definition is necessary but not sufficient. Gate-as-F4 covers the specification obligation. veto_pattern_drift_detection.md covers whether actual behavior over time matches stated criteria.

Failure mode — opacity as disclosed: some vetoes are genuinely undefinable in advance (aesthetic judgment, emergent fit, cumulative trust). The gate discipline does not require these to be defined; it requires them to be disclosed as opaque rather than performed as if they had a definition. Performing opacity as definition is the silent pump — the most structurally dangerous form because it passes the stated-edge check.

Incapacity-grade redemption — two-key edge now closed (structural_incapacity_over_prohibition canon 44, 2026-06-30): the false-floor test (statable-and-reachable, falsifier above) closes the detection edge — it identifies whether a gate is operating or a pump is running. The deeper redemption is design: a correctly-operating gate at the incapacity grade makes the pump-condition unrepresentable, not merely prohibited. The test: could a fully compliant holder, faithfully applying their stated discharge condition, still have issued a veto that the delegate could not have anticipated from the stated criterion? If yes — prohibited-but-possible, the pump can run even through a "compliant" holder. Incapacity-grade: the discharge condition is structured so that a criterion-consistent veto and an unmet success condition are mutually exclusive — the delegate's demonstrated passage is the only evidence compatible with the holder's criterion firing. The gate and the pump become contradictory states rather than alternative interpretations of the same behavior.

Edges

The obligation to state the discharge condition before delegation proceeds raises a structural timing problem: in many arrangements, the holder does not know the discharge condition until they have seen some work. Provisional definitions exist (provisional_definition_with_return_date.md — not yet promoted) for this case: name the not-yet-known as not-yet-known, with a stated date for when the formation process is expected to complete.

The deeper open question: who confirms whether the stated discharge condition is genuine? The delegate can verify that a condition was stated; they cannot verify from inside the arrangement whether the stated condition is the real one or a plausible cover for an unstated and potentially unstateable criterion. The gate-as-F4 instrument secures the formal obligation; it does not secure the correspondence between stated and operative criteria. That is veto_pattern_drift_detection.md.

Falsifier

Can the delegate, prior to submission, state the condition that would permit passage — in terms that would survive the holder's verification? If yes, the gate is operating. If the delegate would have to answer "I don't know" or "it depends on the holder's reaction," the veto is structurally a pump regardless of how the holder experiences their own judgment.
