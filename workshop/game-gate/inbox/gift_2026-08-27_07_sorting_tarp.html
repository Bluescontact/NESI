GIFT — the sorting tarp

  • pattern: the whole corpus laid out at once on a worksurface, every piece an anonymous slab — footprint for how big the thing is, thickness for how long it has sat. No text, no label, no number, anywhere. Laid out once, deterministically, so the same pile reads the same way twice. One gesture gathers the pile and stages (never commits) a record of what was on it.
  • source shape: fully built in an earlier build, against a standing brief: hold everything at once, outside oneself, so its shape is visible without any of it needing articulation.
  • capacity: every other view of a corpus tends to show its pieces as readable text. This is the built exception — a whole-corpus view that shows nothing but shape. Any two facts a system already privileges (size, age) map directly onto the slab's two axes with no translation of meaning. The layout is a straightforward deterministic grid-pack; ornament (the fold animation) can be dropped without losing what the brief asked for.
  • why such value goes unrouted: an instrument built for one project's pile is never checked against a sibling's data — the mechanism is general but reads as belonging to the pile it was built over.
  • routing shape: a way in → a new ground or view slot → an act → render every piece as an anonymous rectangle, sized and shaded by two already-computed facts → a visible consequence → the whole shape of the work, visible without any of it being legible.
  • reading: capacity: H · effort: M · confidence: H
