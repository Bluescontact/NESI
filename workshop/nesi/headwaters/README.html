THE HEADWATERS — where new water enters

Built 2026-09-01 on the keeper's mark ("lets build upstream of the tributaries now"), from the corpus's own naming of what is upstream: nesi/spec/THE_CATCHMENT_2026-08-04.md. That naming is the law here, and it is mostly a law about what this place is not:

  • The system has no presence upstream of this folder. The catchment — everything that happens before anything is written — does not exist to the system at all: not as a gap, not as an absence, not as a thing to be inferred toward. By geography, not rule.
  • This folder is the furthest reach: the page itself. It receives; it does not go looking. The one checkable line: does it go anywhere it was not given?
  • Drop a file. That's the whole mechanism. (The trade-notes law, applied at the source.) No prompt, no practice, no streak, no schedule — a catchment cannot be tended, and anything that asks you to come back would put the system upstream of where it can be.

What happens to what lands here

spring.js (in this folder) is the surfacing organ — named for the naming's own §5: "a spring is not where water begins; it is where it becomes visible." Run by hand, it notices files that have arrived since the last run and appends one line each to SPRING.jsonl: filename, word count, byte count, arrival date. Volume facts only. Its response is identical for a thousand words of grief and a thousand words of grocery lists — the naming's own freshet test, and the first thing that would falsify this build if it ever stopped being true.

From the spring, the water is in the open: a surfaced file is a candidate The keeper can route to the gate (game2d/gate/admit.mjs) with a mark, and only then does it become a tributary of the deposit. Nothing crosses on its own. Nothing here is read for meaning by anything.

The raw files dropped here do not ship anywhere. Only what the keeper's mark admits ever crosses, and the crossing is the gate's, not this folder's.
