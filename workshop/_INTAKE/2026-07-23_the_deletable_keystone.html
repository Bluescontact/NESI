The Deletable Keystone

Extracted from: Outlast as extraction-lab · watched in parallel with the NESI build Pattern class: floor-setting under adversarial surround Intake status: raw pattern, ready to metabolize Dropped: 2026-07-23

Governing law

A floor is real only when it survives the removal of the one who set it. Installed as output, it is a cache and a leash. Transferred as capability, it is a floor. You set the floor by becoming unnecessary on purpose.

The mechanism, in sequence

Scarcity is a dial, not a condition. Extractive games manufacture scarcity to produce a gradient. The gradient is the thing defection scores on — you don't need to be fed, you need to be fed more than the node beside you. Remove the gradient and you remove the axis defection plays on. A theft that doesn't change relative position stops being strategy and becomes labor.

The floor attacks the gradient, not the defector. It doesn't moralize against taking. It flattens the mountain — there is nothing left to climb toward. This is why a distributed floor is theft-proof without a wall: robbing one node nets a day of supply and costs the gradient it was standing on.

The boundary is what makes the gift clean — not the generosity. Floor without boundary is a subsidy to the most ruthless participant at the table. The break in the game lives entirely in the boundary. Giving without it doesn't break the game; it loses, and the game absorbs the giver as content.

The two substances (the load-bearing distinction)

Output is rivalrous. Fire-as-coals, food-as-fish. A pile. Depletes, strip-mines, gets painted as the target. Sharing output is caching.

Capability is non-rivalrous. Fire-as-method, the trap, the knot. Cannot be un-taught, cannot be stolen back. Only capability resets the dial, because you cannot deplete a skill by transferring it.

But capability-sharing relocates rate limits — it does not dissolve them. It resets the dial only where the input is genuinely abundant. Teach every node to fish the same finite lake and you have converted a skill-scarcity into a commons-pressure — you drain the pool faster, not slower. So: floor-setting is true for renewable capability, false for extraction of a fixed pool. Most real fields are a mix, and the pattern must be applied per-substance, not per-node.

Boundary and trade are one object

Durable trade is the boundary seen from the other side. A capability handed off in isolation completes at delivery and ends. The same capability embedded in a standing trade relation keeps the giver structurally necessary without being owed-to — the no-reciprocity-handle expressed as infrastructure.

The theft-proofing is not a wall around the stock. It is that value stopped living in the pile and started living in being the node the topology routes through. You can't be robbed of that, because it isn't in a place.

The vulnerable window (why speed is a safety condition, not a flourish)

The danger is not after the floor is set. It is during.

A field with one fully-built node and five half-built ones is more extractive than the base game — there is now a visibly superior cache and no distributed floor yet to make robbing it pointless. Speed is not capacity-display. It is the act of crossing the replication threshold before the field reads you as the target. Build slow and you have painted a stockpile.

The propagation node is the load-bearing single point of failure until replication completes. The origin is redundant-safe once the skill is out; the second person absorbing-and-forwarding is not. Protect the transfer, not only the origin, until no single removal collapses the topology.

The test that separates the two floors

For any handoff, ask one question:

Can they rebuild it without me?

  • No → you installed infrastructure. Charity with a dependency tail. Six nodes that collapse when you're removed. A floor and a leash — and the leash is on you.
  • Yes → you transferred capability. A floor that survives your deletion. You made yourself unnecessary, which is the only version where "sets the floor for every participant" is actually true.

The fish trap you build for them is a leash. The fish trap they can now build because they watched you is the floor. Same object. Only one survives your removal from the game.

Transfer to NESI

The human gate is the per-transfer instrument that decides, each pass, whether the thing crossing the boundary installs dependency or transmits capability — whether it keeps the field routing through you or makes you deletable without loss.

The whole pattern reduces to one gate-question NESI already asks in its own vocabulary:

Does this pass leave the receiver able to regenerate it without the sender?

If yes, it's a gift that completed and a floor that holds. If no, it's a cache wearing a generous coat, and the sender is now the keystone whose removal breaks everything downstream.

The race NESI is actually running is the same one the sim makes visible: propagation speed vs. re-scarcification speed. Not against producers — against extraction and entropy. The sim just puts a clock on it.
