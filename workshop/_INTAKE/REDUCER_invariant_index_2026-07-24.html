THE FINDING — why the reducer refused to subtract

The reducer was set up to "mark each family's members as expressions and subtract the redundant." Run against the evidence, it refused, twice over:

  1. Family members are NOT redundant to their invariant — they are its facets. Gift Specificity — Receiver Known is not redundant to gift; Ratification by Live Hands is not redundant to proximity; Absence Has No Error Coordinate is not redundant to limit. The invariant is what they share; each pattern is what it adds beyond the invariant. Folding a facet loses the facet. The difference-preserving guard forbids it — and here it fires on essentially every family member, not a few.
  1. There is no true redundancy in the canon to cut. A near-duplicate scan (Jaccard over word-sets) finds 0 real duplicates: only 11 pairs above 0.35, the maximum is 0.50 (half-shared), the median pattern's closest neighbor is 0.19. Every pattern is a distinct recognition. There is nothing to subtract.

So "subtract the redundant" has no safe referent here. Honoring your gate on every subtraction means reporting that the gate has nothing to open: the canon is already differentiated; cutting it would destroy, not reduce.

WHAT REDUCTION ACTUALLY IS HERE — the index, not the knife

Your goal is load off the keeper: carry the few, not the many. That is achieved without deleting anything — by an index where the invariants are the handles and every pattern hangs off its invariant(s) as an expression:

  • You carry ~5–10 invariants, not 106 patterns.
  • Each pattern is derivable/navigable through its invariant(s) — you find it by the constant it expresses, instead of memorizing it discretely.
  • This is derive-don't-store applied to the canon: one authority (the invariants), everything derives (the patterns as expressions). The load drops in your head; the canon stays whole on disk.

The subtraction you wanted happens where it belongs — in what you have to hold, not in what exists.
