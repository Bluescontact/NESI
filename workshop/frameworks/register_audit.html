Register Audit

The instrument that keeps the framework's surfaces from drifting. Pulled back into the workspace 2026-06-06 from the deployed v4 page (library/register-audit.html), where it was stranded as a thing you could read but not run.

Register is structural, not stylistic. A surface in the wrong register reads as the wrong thing — even when the content is correct. The receiver metabolizes the register; the content is only what the writer thought they were transmitting. The audit catches drift before it hardens into the new normal.

The framework's register is specific: geometric, direct, no padding; third-person about the operator; demonstrate-don't-describe; trace not debris.

Step 0 — The surface-class gate (run this first)

Added 2026-06-06, from auditing the canon against itself. The six checks below assume a surface written for a receiver. Not every surface is. Grade the class first or the audit throws false positives on your own instruments.

The most common false positive: grading an instrument as a transmission and flagging your operating console for "second-person creep." The [THE KEEPER] slots in the_governor are the instrument working, not the register slipping.

The six checks (transmission-class)

  1. Trace, not debris. Does it describe what IS in the form, or how the form got built? Build-decision language, alternatives-considered, timing markers ("for now"), ratification status, self-referential frames ("this page demonstrates") are debris. Trace is what the metabolized substrate left visible.
  2. Demonstrate, don't describe. Does it enact what it claims, or describe what it should enact? A page about refusal that refuses cleanly demonstrates; one that explains refusal at length describes. The structure does the work.
  3. Third person about the operator. "the keeper built this infrastructure" holds architectural register; "I want to share why I built this" slips into personal brand. Structural, not coy.
  4. Geometric, not rhetorical. Does it rest on structural claims that hold geometrically, or argue toward a conclusion you're meant to be persuaded into? Geometric writing presents the form and lets the reader read it.
  5. No padding. Does each line carry load? Transition paragraphs, anti-objection sections, summary closers — cut what isn't load-bearing.
  6. The somatic check. Does it register in the body, or is it purely cognitive? Flat writing can be parsed but not felt — nothing catches in the chest or shifts the breath. When the body is absent from the writing, the writing has gone inert.

My working read, pending your word (not canon): camber is the load-bearing curve of a piece — whether the form arcs from its opening move up to its apex without sagging flat (collapsing into enumeration / a list that bears no weight) or over-arching (curving past its load into rhetoric or mysticism). A well-cambered piece carries its weight and then names where that weight could crush — the guard built into the curve. Test, draft: trace the arc from first move to apex; mark where it goes flat and where it bows past structure into persuasion.

The recognition-capacity check

Does the surface create recognition in a receiver — do they feel the pattern in their own life before they parse it? This is checks 2 + 6 working together: a recognition hook is a structural claim that lands somatically. ("A smoke detector with no battery test." "The cost lands at competence, not catastrophe.") Name the recognition target — a transmission for a wounded receiver and an instrument for a builder aim at different recognitions; a piece with high capacity for the wrong target still misses.

The negative-example registry (catch on sight)

  • "We" creep — first-person plural where third-person operator would hold.
  • CTA language — "click here," "sign up," "join us," "get started." Receivers are not funnel positions.
  • Aspirational adjectives — "powerful," "transformative," "groundbreaking." The work claiming for itself what the receiver should decide.
  • Pre-emptive justification — "you might be wondering why…," "some will object that…" Rhetorical defense.
  • Therapeutic register — "honor your truth," "trust the process," "wisdom of the body." Somatic-positivity that bypasses structural specificity. The framework's somatic claims are functional, not mystical.
  • Credentialing — qualifications, testimonials, "trusted by," logos. The work is the credential.
  • Closing-summary debris — "in summary," "to recap," "so what does this mean for you." Re-stating what the form already enacted.
  • Gift-label without gift-structure — the word "gift" (or "gift-based," "offering") stapled onto transactional exchange: exceed-spec-to-win-the-contract, value-add-to-close-the-deal. Gift-form is the non-transactional mechanism — no metered return expected — never the vocabulary. A surface that says "gift" while enacting exchange reads as the wrong thing, and contaminates the real pattern if mistaken for an instance of it.

The drift cascade (why catching it early is cheap)

Voice → Metrics → Performance → Productization → Gatekeeping → Extraction. Each coupling opens the next; marketing-voice makes metrics meaningful, metrics get performed, performance crystallizes into a product, products require gatekeeping, gatekeeping converts gift into extraction. Each coupling is harder to reverse than the one before it. The audit's purpose is to catch the first slip.

How to run it

  1. Class it (Step 0). Transmission, instrument, or substrate.
  2. Score the checks for that class. Mark each: holds / slight drift / drift, with the specific line.
  3. Trace the camber (once §[THE KEEPER: OPEN] is filled).
  4. Name the recognition target and rate capacity.
  5. Surfaces that fail one check usually fail several. The body registers slip before the cognitive system does — when it does, this is the language for what the slip is.

Run at three moments: composing a new surface, revising an existing one, and periodically across all surfaces to catch accumulated drift no single edit triggered. Not a perfectionist tool — a discipline for catching drift early.
