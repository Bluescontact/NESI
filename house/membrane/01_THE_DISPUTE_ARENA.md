# The Dispute Arena

*Membrane function HELD-1, built 2026-09-01 on the keeper's instruction
("build the three scoped membrane functions"). Scoped by the keeper's ruling
of the same day, after a ground pass ran the circuit's claims against the
commons record. Companion to [CIRCUIT_TOOL.md](../CIRCUIT_TOOL.md) and
[the open ledger](../open-ledger/THE_OPEN_LEDGER.md).*

---

## What this replaces

The circuit framing said: *"Disputes are out of scope by design... there is
nowhere to appeal, no one who holds the authoritative record."* The commons
record says that configuration is where such systems die: every enduring
commons carries rapid access to a low-cost, local arena for resolving
conflict (Ostrom's sixth design principle), and removing the formal arena
does not remove the disputes — it moves them into an informal one that
nobody can see or check (Freeman, 1970). The circuit already had a dispute
arena. It was the gathering, and it was undeclared. This document declares
it.

## The arena, named

**The arena is the gathering** — the same room the ledger already routes
people to. Not a court, not a committee, not a new institution: the standing
occasion where the people in a circuit are already face to face. What
changes is only that its dispute work is now on the table instead of under
it.

## Who convenes it

Anyone party to the dispute may bring it to the next gathering, and either
party may ask for a gathering sooner. Convening takes one sentence spoken or
written to the people involved: *"I want to bring something to the
gathering."* No one may block the convening — not the keeper, not the other
party. A dispute someone refuses to bring stays a private matter; the arena
never reaches out and takes one.

## What it can decide

- Whether a route between the parties **rests** — and any participant can
  rest their own end of any route unilaterally at any time; the arena only
  witnesses it.
- What each party **heard the other carry** — the arena can settle what was
  named, because naming is on the record; it is the reconciliation surface
  for "I said I could carry X" versus "I heard Y."
- Whether something that flowed **landed as gift or as extraction** — the
  felt-read, spoken in the room, by the person who bore it.

## What it cannot decide

- It cannot compute or assign **debt**. The schema has no field for it and
  the arena gets no power the schema refuses.
- It cannot **compel** a flow, a route, an apology, or a departure. The
  circuit has no enforcement mechanism; the arena is where disputes land,
  not where force comes from.
- It cannot **remove a person from the village**. Each person holds their
  own node; the arena can witness every other node resting its routes to
  someone, which is the mesh's real boundary — visible, distributed, and on
  each person's own authority — but there is no expulsion lever to pull.
- It cannot bind the absent. Nothing decided about a person who was not in
  the room binds them.

## How its record is kept

One line per dispute brought, in the gathering's own record, written or
spoken-and-noted by a person both parties accept for that entry: the date,
who brought it, what was decided or left open, in the hands' own words. No
category codes, no severity field, no count displayed anywhere. The record
exists so the next gathering can know what the last one held — it is a
memory, not a docket. A party may ask that an entry record disagreement
with the outcome; the disagreement goes in the same line.

## The limit, stated

This arena is informal and works at the scale where the people in a circuit
can actually gather. That is a real limit, not a flaw to engineer away: the
circuit's own framing says the mesh forms at the intersection of
individually-held nodes, and an arena that outgrew the room would need
authority the architecture refuses. If a circuit grows past what its
gathering can hold, that is a signal to split the circuit, not to grow the
arena.

---

*The falsifier this carries: if disputes in a running circuit are
observably being settled somewhere other than the gathering — in private
channels, by the keeper's sole word, or by silent exit — then the arena as
declared here is not the arena in fact, and this document is wrong and must
be rewritten from what actually happens.*
