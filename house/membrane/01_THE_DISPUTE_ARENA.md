# The Dispute Arena

Every gift circuit has disputes, whether or not its design admits it. In
this one, disputes land in **the gathering** — the standing occasion where
the people in a circuit are already face to face. That was always where
they landed; this document makes it official, so the arena can be seen and
checked instead of operating informally.

## Who convenes it

Anyone party to a dispute may bring it to the next gathering, and either
party may ask for a gathering sooner. Convening takes one sentence, spoken
or written to the people involved: *"I want to bring something to the
gathering."* No one may block the convening — not the circuit's owner, not
the other party. A dispute someone chooses not to bring stays a private
matter; the arena never reaches out and takes one.

## What it can decide

- **Whether a route between the parties rests.** (A route is a standing
  give-and-receive between two people; "resting" pauses it without ending
  it.) Anyone can rest their own end of any route at any time on their own
  — the arena only witnesses it.
- **What each party heard the other offer.** Offers are on the record, so
  the arena can settle "I said I could carry X" against "I heard Y."
- **Whether something that flowed landed as a gift or as extraction** —
  said in the room, by the person who bore the cost, in their own words.

## What it cannot decide

- It cannot compute or assign **debt**. The circuit's data model has no
  field for debt, and the arena gets no power the data model refuses.
- It cannot **compel** anything — a flow, an apology, a departure. The
  circuit has no enforcement mechanism; the arena is where disputes land,
  not where force comes from.
- It cannot **remove a person**. Each participant holds their own node;
  the real boundary is every other participant resting their routes to
  someone — visible, distributed, each on their own authority. There is no
  expulsion lever.
- It cannot bind the absent. Nothing decided about a person who was not
  in the room binds them.

## How its record is kept

One line per dispute, in the gathering's own record, written by a person
both parties accept for that entry: the date, who brought it, what was
decided or left open — in the participants' own words. No category codes,
no severity ratings, no counts displayed anywhere. The record is a memory
for the next gathering, not a docket. A party who disagrees with an
outcome can have the disagreement recorded in the same line.

## The limit

This arena works at the scale where people can actually gather, and that
limit is deliberate: an arena that outgrew the room would need central
authority the circuit refuses to have. A circuit that grows past what its
gathering can hold should split into two circuits, not grow the arena.

---

**How to show this wrong:** if disputes in a running circuit are
observably settled somewhere else — in private channels, by the owner's
sole word, by silent exit — then the arena declared here is not the arena
in fact, and this document must be rewritten from what actually happens.
