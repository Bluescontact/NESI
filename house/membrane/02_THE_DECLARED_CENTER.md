# The Declared Center

*Membrane function HELD-2, built 2026-09-01 on the keeper's instruction
("build the three scoped membrane functions"). The register's condition asked
for the declaration in the keeper's own words; this is the built form,
standing on the keeper's recorded decisions, and any line of it the keeper
re-words supersedes this text by layering, the corpus's own convention.*

---

## What this replaces

The circuit framing said *"no one coordinates it from the center"* and
*"a protocol with no central authority."* Freeman's finding (The Tyranny of
Structurelessness, 1970) is that there is no such thing as a structureless
group: removing the formal center makes the real one invisible and
unaccountable. The schematic already drew the truth — the keeper as central
bus. This document stops denying what the diagram shows and declares it
instead.

## What the keeper holds

1. **The gate.** Nothing enters the corpus or crosses into the public
   deposit except through the keeper's recorded mark. The gate's refusals
   are enforced in code; the marks are quoted verbatim in the record.
2. **The vocabulary.** The canonical terms (carry, hold, route, flow, rest,
   gap, refusal) and the refusal of the ledger frame at the language level
   are the keeper's design decisions.
3. **The mark.** Supersession, admission, crossing, and closure all happen
   by the keeper's explicitly recorded decision, and only by it.

That is a center. It is declared here rather than denied.

## What holding the center does NOT permit

- The keeper **cannot open, close, or force a route** between any two other
  nodes. Routes are bilateral; both ends consent or nothing flows.
- The keeper **cannot read, score, or aggregate** another node's gifts,
  needs, refusals, or Brake. Each person's node is self-authored and
  self-held.
- The keeper **cannot remove anyone from the village.** Retention is
  architectural: you hold your own node, so no one — including the center —
  can take it.
- The keeper **cannot decide another person's felt read**, in the arena or
  anywhere else. The instrument of judgment is the body of the person
  bearing the cost.
- The keeper's authority over the *corpus* does not extend to authority
  over *participants*. The gate governs what this project publishes and
  binds itself to — never what another node may do.

## What a participant can check this against

- **The gate's own record** — every admission and crossing carries a mark,
  quoted verbatim; a decision without a mark on the record did not happen.
- **The schema** ([SCHEMA.sql](../open-ledger/SCHEMA.sql)) — the declared
  incapacities (no counterparty, no price, no debt) are structural; a
  keeper-only backdoor would have to appear in this public file to exist.
- **The arena record** ([01_THE_DISPUTE_ARENA.md](01_THE_DISPUTE_ARENA.md))
  — if the keeper's word is settling disputes outside the gathering, the
  center has outgrown this declaration, and this document is being
  violated in a way the record will show.
- **This document's own history** — it is public and versioned; a silent
  change to what the center claims is itself a violation of the mark
  convention (supersession is a layer, never an erasure).

## Succession, honestly

The corpus currently has exactly one keeper, and this declaration does not
pretend otherwise. If the keeper stops keeping, the gate closes rather than
passing to an unnamed successor — fail closed is the corpus's own law
(silence defaults to stop). What survives without the keeper is what was
already given away: everything in this deposit works standalone, and that —
not an inherited center — is the designed continuity.

---

*The falsifier this carries: any observed keeper action inside the "does
NOT permit" list falsifies the declaration; the honest response is to
record the violation in the open, not to amend the list to fit it.*
