Written 2026-08-13, the day the institutional commitment landed, which means **this return is written from inside the phase it describes and cannot yet close it.**

---

## The definition, and why it is strict

> **A floor is a structure that works for people who cannot see it.**

Not a promise. Not goodwill. Not a person standing there to help. What persists when the person who built it has left.

The strictness is the content. Almost everything that gets called a floor is actually a favour with good intentions attached, and the difference only becomes visible under load — at which point the person relying on it has already fallen through. Five properties separate them, and a structure needs all five:

**It applies to everyone who enters, whether or not they read it.** The rider does not read the load rating on an elevator. They do not know the inspection code exists. They are protected by it anyway. This is the whole test in one image, and it is worth noticing that it inverts the usual moral logic: a floor that requires the protected party to be informed, vigilant, or assertive is not a floor, however well-drafted.

**It is written and verifiable, not verbal or discretionary.** A spoken commitment disappears with the person who made it.

**It has an enforcement mechanism, not just an expression of intent.** A policy reviewed once and then forgotten is not a floor. A policy reviewed by an external regulator, embedded in a contract everyone signs, or triggering an automatic process — that is.

**It is the same for everyone, not based on who asks or how they ask.** *"A floor that depends on a sympathetic person having capacity that week is not a floor. It is a favor."*

**It can be checked by someone other than the builder.** If only the original builder can verify it holds, it dies when they leave.

## The distinction this phase exists to enforce

After the intervention, three people inside the operating organisation talked, agreed, and made the refund right. That decision was real. That acknowledgment was genuine.

**A decision is not a structure.**

> The next time that organization sees a participant reaching the end of their carry, three different people may be in those rooms. They may not have capacity. They may not remember this conversation. They may make a different choice.

This is the sentence that separates phase six from phase five, and it is the one most likely to be skipped by an operator who has just received a good response. The written concession — *after discussing with the operator, we agree that your calculation of unearned tuition under the state education code is reasonable* — is a genuine institutional act and it protects exactly one person, retroactively, and that person had already waived their claim.

> **A floor exists when the decision is no longer required because the structure makes the right choice automatic.**

## The number this return will not settle

Two figures for the unearned proportion stand in the record and both cannot be the statutory one.

**57.1%** — derived 2026-08-05 from the implementing regulation's own language, which computes pro rata on days attended **or scheduled**: 84 of 196 calendar days.

**~35%** — the completion figure of the *instructional* period, used in the filing and reflected in the worked case's own description of the withdrawal point.

The memory record flags this plainly: *"Two numbers in this file are now contested and unresolved."* The concession that arrived agreed the participant's calculation was *reasonable*. Reasonable is not the same as statutory, and a concession that a number is reasonable does not determine which method the regulation requires.

This matters for the floor and not only for the accounting. **The floor is a refund policy that complies with the statute, and compliance requires knowing which computation the statute mandates.** A revised schedule built on the wrong method is a new document with the same class of defect. Anyone verifying the floor should verify the method first.

This return states the collision and does not resolve it. Resolution is a reading of 5 CCR § 71750(c) against the programme's actual calendar, and it is not this document's to make.

## The ten checks: a floor handed forward instead of built

The most transferable object the arc produced is not the revised policy. It is a checklist a stranger can run before signing anything, in an afternoon, with no expertise, on a document they already hold.

1. **Does any refund survive the first class?** Compare the refund schedule against the first *instructional* session — not orientation, not onboarding, not a welcome call. Fail if every tier closes on or before it.
2. **Is there a pro rata provision?** Search for *pro rata*, *prorated*, *proportional*, *unearned*. Fail if absent.
3. **Is there a right to cancel?** A refund schedule is not a cancellation right. Many agreements have the first and not the second.
4. **If you leave, do you still owe?** Fail if an unpaid balance survives withdrawal — that is being locked in by cost rather than by desire.
5. **Must a decision about you be given in writing, with a reason?** Fail if absent. A verbal decision cannot be proved, appealed, or corrected.
6. **Is there an appeal?** Fail if absent — otherwise every outcome depends on who is listening that week.
7. **Is there a written hardship procedure?** Not a person to email. A procedure with a defined institutional response.
8. **What is the load rating?** Hours per week, what is required versus optional, and whether you may be assigned an unpaid role — peer leadership, pod navigation, mentoring — disclosed before you sign. *Every elevator has a posted load rating. Programmes that ask for months of your life generally do not.*
9. **Who takes the money?** Compare the entity on the invoice with the entity making the promises. Different is often ordinary; you should be able to learn which, in writing, before paying.
10. **What is the total charge?** The list price, not your discounted price. In several states the list price determines whether the programme is regulated at all.

Reading the result: zero fails is rare and worth telling people about. One or two — ask in writing before paying, keep the answer. **Three or more means the agreement was assembled rather than designed** — common, not an accusation, and it does mean the document will not protect you and whatever protection exists depends on specific people continuing to have capacity.

This is the phase-six output that does not depend on any institution keeping a promise, and it is why it belongs here rather than in an appendix.

## The falsifier that was set in advance, and where it stands

Written on 5 August, before any of this resolved, as the test of whether the gift landed:

> - **If the money arrives and nothing settles**, the money was never the gift and never the point.
> - **If the money arrives and no floor gets written for the next person**, the gift did not land, whatever the number says.

The money did not arrive — it was declined and redirected as a restricted donation, which disposes of the first condition in the strongest available way.

The second is live and unresolved. A commitment to write a floor is not a floor. The test is a document, at a date: **the enrollment agreement the next cohort signs.** If its refund schedule has a tier surviving the first instructional session and a pro rata provision matching the statute's computation method, the gift landed. If it does not, the arc produced a written concession, a regulator's file, a policy review that happened or did not, and a person who paid for all of it.
