## The frame

It's the tetrahedron, wired as a working circuit. Four panels, one for each face. The circuit metaphor is load-bearing: it names what routes (conductors), what flows (gifts and needs), what regulates (the Brake), and what the whole looks like from outside (the schematic).

---

## The four panels

### Self — Differentiation
Name your gifts and your needs, tagged by domain. Plain language, because what you carry is what others can route to. Name your refusals too — what you won't carry is as structurally important as what you will. The gap between carrying and refusing is your ground.

### Village — Connection
Add people and record what each one carries and holds. This is the substrate; nothing routes until it's named. Each person in your village holds their own node — their own gifts, needs, refusals, Brake. You are recording your view of what they carry, and they are recording their own. The overlap between your node and theirs is where routes can form.

### Routes — Exchange
Conductors form where a need meets a gift. Two conductor types:
- **Copper** — domain match. Tight. The need and the gift are in the same domain.
- **Blue** — keyword reach. Inferential. The connection crosses domain lines.

You set a route flowing, then let it rest. It re-opens anytime. No "complete," no "paid," no settlement. Three states, all about circulation: **flowing**, **resting**, **gap**.

Routes are bilateral. For a route to exist, both nodes must be participating — yours and theirs. You cannot open a route to someone who isn't running the protocol from their own node. You cannot force a route to flow. The circuit only conducts where both ends are open.

### System — Architecture
The whole circuit at once:
- A **coverage meter** — how much of your named need is currently reachable
- A **gap list** — needs nobody in your current village can reach; the live growth vector
- The **Brake** — a capacity ceiling you set from the body, not from paper. It trips into warning when more is flowing than you can hold. You set it; your body confirms it.
- A **generated schematic** — you as the central bus, your village on both rails, conductors that animate when a route is live. Copper and blue visible in the diagram.

## Ownership architecture

Each person holds their own node and infrastructure. They own their own choices, shares, and refusals. The mesh forms naturally at the intersection of individually-held instances — no one coordinates it from the center.

**No one can kick you out of the village** because you hold your own node. The retention logic is architectural, not social. The village holds you because you hold the village.

**You cannot force compliance or exchange.** Routes flow only where both ends consent. The circuit has no enforcement mechanism and needs none.

**Disputes are out of scope by design.** A protocol with no central authority has no dispute mechanism possible — there is nowhere to appeal, no one who holds the authoritative record. This follows from the architecture, not from a policy choice.

## The vocabulary

The tool refuses the ledger frame at the language level. Canonical terms:

| Use | Don't use |
|---|---|
| carry | own |
| hold | have |
| route | send / give |
| flow | transact / exchange |
| rest | complete / close |
| gap | debt / shortage |
| refusal | rejection |

Every label and tooltip stays in this vocabulary. Any word that drifts toward transaction (earn, owe, receive, balance, complete) corrupts the frame at the root.

---

## The gap list as growth signal

The gap list is not just diagnostic. Gaps are open terminals — the voltage is there, nothing's connected. Every gap is a signal about where the mesh is thin and who to invite. The motion from gap → "who might carry this" → new village node is the primary growth mechanism. Make it easy.

---

## What the circuit runs on

The schematic shows the keeper as central bus, village on both rails. In a real circuit, the bus distributes supply from a source. The source here is the non-transactional mechanism: the off-grid setup, near-zero burn, the standing gift-form economy. It doesn't need to be prominent in the UI, but it belongs in the System view — what the whole circuit is running on.

---

## Persistence

Everything persists across sessions. The compounding substrate, not a transaction you redo each time. The circuit remembers — gifts named, village built, routes opened and rested, gaps identified — and the value compounds with each session.
