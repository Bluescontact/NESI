Eco · 1
Build the floor before anything else

The economic forms that sustain non-extractive life — gift exchange, labor-as-gift, near-zero burn — are mostly invisible to law. The law recognizes persons and property; a gift is neither. It falls through law, not against it: not illegal, unrepresentable. This makes these forms fragile unless the substrate they run on is owned. The floor is owned-not-rented: the ground, the tool, the node that cannot be revoked by someone else's decision. Secure the floor first. Everything built above it is tolerated-not-protected until the floor is in place.

Reading

Fires when evaluating any economic move or structure. Two readings on the floor question that are not reconciled: floor-first (the gift forms cannot reach beyond demonstration until the legal category exists — highest leverage is upstream) vs. demonstration-first (the legal category never gets written for something that doesn't yet visibly exist — the demonstration is the lobbying). Both may be true at different scales. Which one binds in a given situation is a body question, not a paper one.

Eco · 2
Need less, not earn more

Before adding income streams, subtract requirements. The manufactured-need loop generates scarcity that income cannot close — earning more funds the loop's next iteration without touching the structure. The test: what does enough look like, in specific terms you could verify? If the answer keeps moving, the loop is structural. Near-zero material burn changes what "enough" means at the foundation: the floor is lower, so the gap between current state and sufficient state shrinks without earning anything new.

Reading

Fires before any move to add income. The somatic check: when approaching the current floor's "enough," does the body register threat or completion? Completion-dread without a specifiable gap = manufactured scarcity. The manufactured-need version: the need keeps getting redefined upward as each previous threshold is approached. The structural version: one of the current needs is actually a dependency created by the economic structure, not by genuine requirement. Dissolving the dependency shrinks the floor rather than raising the income.

Eco · 3
Three columns, one forbidden

Every economic cost routes to one of three places.

Circulated
Paid forward as gift, investment in commons, substrate for the next agent
Refused
Felt and declined before incurring — the body said no at the gate
Consented-leak
Unavoidable, named, not hidden — the entropy that can't be stopped, acknowledged
Hidden-leak — forbidden
Always present; the discipline is moving its contents into consented as fast as surplus moves them back
Reading

The cross-resource falsifier: surplus in cash can hide depletion in time. Surplus in time can hide depletion in relational bandwidth. Run the ledger across all resource axes simultaneously, not just the visible one. Two failure modes: abundance hides the transfer (hidden column fills while you feel fine) and audit-treadmill (continuous accounting is itself a cost that reopens the ledger). The cutoff: close the ledger by decision, not by reconciliation. The spent body's judgment is the corrupted instrument — set the cutoff before depletion, not after.

Eco · 4
Name what the exit actually costs

Exit from a cost-generating economic structure is itself an economic move — often the highest-leverage one available. Before concluding that exit is too expensive, name specifically what is holding you inside: what is the actual cost of leaving, stated in concrete terms? Then name what it costs to stay, across all axes — economic, somatic, temporal, relational. Sometimes the hold is real. Sometimes the hold is a manufactured dependency that the exit itself dissolves. Relocation that creates distance from a scarcity-generating structure is structural release, not retreat.

Reading

Fires when the current structure feels like the only viable one. The manufactured-dependency version: the structure has produced dependencies (relationships, obligations, infrastructure) that feel unextractable but are actually downstream of the structure — they dissolve or transform when the structure changes. The real-dependency version: specific, nameable things would be genuinely lost that cannot be reconstituted from the outside. These are different. Name which one each element is. The mobile-node insight: a node that can relocate is not exposed to ejection from a fixed geography. Mobility itself changes the economic posture — the floor lowers when it doesn't require permission from a fixed location.
