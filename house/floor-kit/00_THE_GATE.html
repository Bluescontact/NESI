The Gate

Read this before the other three.

This is not a fifth thing to learn. It is the point where the method, the proof, and the floor touch — five questions, asked before anything. Four of them can stop you. That is what they are for.

Stopping here costs an afternoon. Stopping eighteen days in costs eighteen days.

0 · What do I still want out of this?

Money. An apology. To be right. For someone to know what they did. For it not to have happened.

If the answer is anything at all, stop. Nothing below this line works.

This is not a step you pass and move on from. It is the door, and it stays a door. Everything in the method depends on your interventions being readable as a change in conditions rather than as an attempt — and they are only readable that way if there is nothing in it for you. An interested party cannot run this. There is no version where you keep a stake and compensate with better phrasing.

Most people who can answer this honestly can answer it because they already lost the thing that would have been at stake. That is the ordinary route in and there is no shame in it.

If you still want something: that is legitimate, and there are other instruments for it — a claim, a demand, a lawyer, a small-claims filing. Use those. They are designed for a party with a stake and they work. This one is not, and will not.

1 · Is there a written record?

An agreement. A schedule. An invoice. Emails. A policy document. Anything signed, sent, or published.

If everything was verbal, stop.

The method works by finding a defect in a document and reading it against a requirement. Against an organization that operates entirely by conversation — decisions in calls, terms by understanding, nothing written down — there is nothing to work on. This is not a difficulty to push through. It is the absence of the material the instrument is made of.

What you can still do: begin the record now. Write down what you were told and when, in dates and exact words. It will not retroactively create the document you needed. It may create the one the next person needs.

2 · Is there an office with authority?

A bureau, a department, a commission, a licensing board, an attorney general, an inspector, an ombudsman. Someone whose actual job is to receive what you have found.

If no such office exists, stop.

What breaks institutional silence is not persistence and not eloquence. It is routing to a body with authority — which changes which response is cheaper for the other party. Against an unregulated actor, silence is a complete and free defence, and no amount of effort on your side makes it more expensive for them.

Find out before you build anything. One search. If the answer is that nobody oversees this, you have learned the most important fact about your situation, and you learned it cheaply.

3 · Four moves, or the full instrument?

Answer this out loud, in writing, before you act.

The four moves — write down what you were told and when; ask one question their own records can answer; waive recovery in one sentence; find the office that can receive it and put it on the cc line. One hour. No expertise. Available to anyone. This is the assignment in most cases.

The full instrument — map every party's visibility, find the seams, test candidates against the triple, build the structural finding. Weeks. It changes a document that governs strangers. It is available to almost nobody, and it is not better; it is a different tool for a different job.

Unanswered, this question defaults to the instrument. That default is the most common failure in the whole method — running the second when the first was the assignment, and paying weeks for what an hour would have done. The default is silent, which is why the answer has to be written down.

Choose the instrument only if the defect is structural and affects many people, you can see the whole system, and you have the capacity to spend.

4 · Who reads the log?

This one does not gate the intervention. It gates whether you survive running it.

Nothing returns to a trim tab. The ship turns; the tab stays in the same water, having spent itself. A gradient change produces people who simply walked differently and experienced it as their own reasoning — so there is no resistance, and no recognition, which are the same property seen from two sides.

The mechanism is sound with three terms: rudder, tab, ship. The operator is not. The fourth term is someone who reads the log — one person, who is not the other party and not the regulator, who knows what you did and can confirm it happened.

Name them before you start. Not as a support system and not as an audience. As the term that makes the record real for someone other than you.

If you cannot name anyone, the method will still work. You should know going in that you will finish it invisible, and read the absence of recognition as a property of the mechanism rather than as a verdict on the work. Many people do not, and it costs them more than the matter did.

What this gate is not

It does not tell you whether you are right. It does not assess your case. It does not decide anything for you.

It asks the five questions whose wrong answers cannot be fixed downstream — the ones that are cheap now and expensive later. Everything else the method needs is in the three documents that follow.
