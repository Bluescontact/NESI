The Proof: A Worked Case

Setting: A multi-month cohort program run by a small operating organization, taking tuition under a written enrollment agreement, operating under the fiscal sponsorship of a nonprofit, with payments handled by a third entity.

Elapsed time from first structural read to institutional commitment: under three weeks.

Outcome: Written statutory concession from the fiscal sponsor within twenty-four hours of routing. Policy review committed before the next cohort. Complaint filed with the state regulator and on record.

The Tetrahedron

Four vertices. Six relations. Each party sees three faces of the solid; none sees the complete structure until the fourth vertex is named.

V1: THE SCHEDULE

The refund clause of the enrollment agreement:

  • Full refund before an early date
  • Partial refund before a second date
  • No refund after that

The defect: Both dates fall inside the onboarding period, not the instructional period. Instruction began on the second date. The partial tier expired on the first day of class. No refund tier survives a single session of instruction at any point in a program running most of a year.

Why it matters: The state education code requires a pro rata refund for students completing 60% or less of the period of attendance. A schedule that closes on day one cannot produce a pro rata refund under any circumstances.

Who can see it: The fiscal sponsor can see the law. The operator can see the dates. Neither is reading them against each other.

V2: THE PARTICIPANT

Timeline:

  • Paid the tuition in full at enrollment — a figure reached after a partial waiver
  • Withdrew at roughly 35% of the instructional period
  • Directed the remaining balance to the program's scholarship fund, asking nothing

The calculation:

  • Earned: the completed fraction of the amount paid
  • Unearned: the remainder
  • Both figures sit below the 60% threshold that triggers statutory protection

What the participant carried:

  • Liquidated long-term savings to enroll
  • Financed living expenses with consumer debt
  • Reached the end of carry capacity mid-program
  • Could not continue

Who can see it: The participant sees their own payment and their own experience. They do not read the agreement closely until months in. They argue about an amount while the defect sits in the calendar.

V3: THE REGULATOR

The law:

  • An education code provision: pro rata refund required for students at or below 60% attendance, payable within a fixed window
  • An implementing regulation: the calculation method — (total charge ÷ program days) × days attended, subtracted from amount paid
  • A civil code provision: forfeiture terms are void unless the party enforcing them shows that actual damages were impracticable to determine

Jurisdiction: The state agency overseeing private postsecondary education reviews tuition contracts, refund schedules, and compliance with statutory minimums. Its jurisdiction attaches above a statutory price threshold — which means the program's actual charge decides whether the statute applies at all.

What it cannot see: A tuition contract between a participant and an operator is outside the agency's normal purview. The document showing the defect is the one it never receives — until someone files a complaint.

V4: THE INSTITUTION

The parties:

  • The operator — the organization that runs the program
  • The processor — the entity that takes and holds the money
  • The fiscal sponsor — the nonprofit under whose status the program operates

Their view: Agreement signed, dates applied, balance zero. Everything reconciles. Terms were followed as written.

What they cannot see: Whether those terms hold under law. The refund schedule and the statutory requirement are each accurate on their own side and are never read against each other. A decision made by three people in conversation can be right and still not be a structure.

The Six Relations

1. Schedule ↔ Participant

The relation: The schedule closes before instruction begins. The participant signs it without realizing they have no refund protection at any point if they attend even one class.

What the method found: The dates run in weeks from the onboarding start — onboarding weeks, not teaching weeks. This is checkable fact. The participant withdrew partway through instruction. This is verifiable record. The law requires pro rata protection at that point. This is statutory. No interpretation required.

2. Schedule ↔ Regulator

The relation: The law requires pro rata refunds at or below 60% attendance. A schedule ending on day one cannot produce a pro rata calculation under any mathematical approach.

What the method found: The defect is not in the judgment of either party. It is in the structure of the schedule itself. A regulator reading this can verify it without testimony. The numbers prove it.

3. Participant ↔ Institution

The relation: The participant is told the determination is zero — no refund due. No written reason. No receipt. No record.

What the method found: The determination fails three separate standards at once. It is not in writing, which the agreement's own written-determination clause requires. It has no stated basis, which the civil code requires of any forfeiture. And it cannot be audited by anyone else, because it was never recorded.

4. Participant ↔ Regulator

The relation: The participant paid under an agreement they believed carried a refund schedule. The law guarantees pro rata protection to anyone meeting the criteria. They meet the criteria. They receive nothing. This is invisible to the regulator unless the participant files.

What the method found: The participant is the only point in the system that can see both their own data and the law. They can connect them. They can file. They can put the regulator on record.

5. Institution ↔ Regulator

The relation: The institution operates on the assumption that its written agreement governs. The regulator operates under the law. Nobody ever told the institution the two conflict.

What the method found: Routing to the regulator makes the conflict visible. It is not an accusation. It is a structural fact. The regulator has standing to review. The institution has reason to revise.

6. Schedule ↔ Regulator — the seam

The relation: The schedule is a document. The law is a requirement. They live in different systems. The regulator sees the law. The operator sees its own agreement. Neither is looking at the other. This is the seam.

What the method found: Adding the regulator to the conversation pushes neither party. It changes which response is cheaper. Revising the schedule before the next cohort becomes cheaper than defending the current one in a formal proceeding.

The Method Applied

Step 1 · Map who can see what

  • Operator: agreement, dates, payment reconciliation
  • Participant: payment, experience, and the law once they go looking
  • Regulator: law, statutory requirements, compliance patterns
  • Fiscal sponsor: law, tuition policy, institutional obligations

Step 2 · Find the seams

  • Between onboarding and instruction — where the refund tiers expire
  • Between operator and regulator — where the schedule should have been reviewed and never was
  • Between private dispute and public record — where a filing lands

Step 3 · Test candidates against the triple

  • Cheap to execute? Yes — about an hour to identify, structure, and file
  • Irreversible? Yes — a filing cannot be un-filed; the law now applies to future cohorts
  • Changes the gradient, not the parties? Yes — the institution faces different conditions, not an accusation

Step 4 · Surrender the stake

  • Written waiver: I am not seeking money
  • Unconditional — waived before filing, not offered in exchange for a response
  • Bounded — limited to the matter, not to the person or the institution

Step 5 · Execute the smallest version

  • Not a lawsuit, not a demand, not a threat
  • A structural statement: four facts, six relations, what each party can and cannot see
  • A filing: one page, one request — review the schedule before the next cohort

Step 6 · Wait out the latency

  • Day 0 — statement sent
  • Day 4 — written concession received
  • Day 5 — filing submitted, with no follow-up and no pressure applied
  • Day 7 — institution commits to a policy review

Step 7 · Record what happened

  • The email archive: dated, signed, timestamped
  • The regulator's file: on official record
  • The written concession from the fiscal sponsor
  • The donation acknowledgment letter: a receipt for the gift

What the Method Produced

The entry: The unearned portion was properly accounted for as a statutory refund and transferred as a restricted donation.

The calculation: Verified in writing by the fiscal sponsor's executive director, against the state education code and its implementing regulation.

The receipt: A donation acknowledgment letter for tax purposes. Proof of transfer.

The acknowledgment: A written statement from the sponsor, issued after consulting the operator, agreeing that the participant's calculation of unearned tuition under the state education code was reasonable.

The floor: The operator committed to a full policy review before the next cohort. No participant in that cohort will face the same schedule. The defect is documented on the regulator's record. Future participants are protected not by goodwill but by a structure that has to hold.

Why This Case Proves the Method

It shows the method working across a complete cycle:

  • Defect identified in the structure, not in a person
  • Four parties shown with their limited visibility
  • Gradient inverted without pushing any party
  • Institutional response within twenty-four hours — the latency signature
  • Structural change committed — the floor built
  • Record preserved — seven-step documentation
  • Operator remains capable and willing — no wreckage, no resentment
  • Fiscal sponsor becomes a participant in the solution

It shows the economics:

  • Cost to the operator: a policy review, which was required and inevitable
  • Cost to the participant: none — recovery waived, reimbursement declined
  • Cost to the regulator: processing one complaint, which is normal work
  • Gain to the next participant: protection written into law

It proves the latency signature:

  • Months of direct requests: nothing
  • One routing to receiving capacity: written concession within twenty-four hours
  • Institutional commitment two days after that
  • Response faster than the effort was large — trim tab confirmed
