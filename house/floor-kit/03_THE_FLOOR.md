# The Floor

A floor is a structure that works for people who cannot see it.

A floor is not a promise. It is not goodwill. It is not a person standing there to help. It is what persists when the person who built it has left.

---

## What Makes a Floor Durable

A floor is durable when:

**It applies to everyone who enters the system, whether or not they read it.**  
The rider does not read the load rating on an elevator. They do not know the inspection code exists. They are protected by it anyway.

**It is written and verifiable, not verbal or discretionary.**  
A spoken commitment disappears with the person who made it. A written standard persists.

**It has an enforcement mechanism, not just an expression of intent.**  
A policy that is reviewed once and then forgotten is not a floor. A policy that is reviewed by an external regulator, or that is embedded in a contract everyone signs, or that triggers an automatic process — that is a floor.

**It is the same for everyone, not based on who asks or how they ask.**  
A floor that depends on a sympathetic person having capacity that week is not a floor. It is a favor.

**It can be checked by someone other than the builder.**  
If only the original builder can verify that the floor exists and is holding, then the floor dies when they leave. A durable floor can be audited, measured, and confirmed by a stranger.

---

## The Floor in the Worked Case

**Before:** Refund determinations were verbal. Decisions in calls. Nothing in writing. No stated basis. No way to audit whether it was fair. The next participant could not know what they were entitled to.

**After:** The refund policy will be reviewed before the next cohort. The review is documented. The commitment is in writing. The regulator has the complaint on file and will verify compliance. The policy that results will be in the enrollment agreement, visible to the next participant, and reviewable against the law.

That is a floor. The next person will walk into a different structure. They will not depend on whether the current director is reachable or having a good week.

## Ten Checks Before You Sign: A Floor as a Checklist

The following ten checks are a floor. They are a structure that protects you before you pay, while you are enrolled, and if something goes wrong. You can run them yourself. They require no expertise. You do not need to trust anyone. You just need to read the document and answer ten questions.

### 1 · Does any refund survive the first class?

Find the refund schedule. Find the date of the first *instructional* session — not orientation, not onboarding, not a welcome call. Compare them.

**FAIL if** every refund tier closes on or before the first day of instruction. If so, no participant who attends a single session can receive a refund at any point in the program.

**Why this matters:** If you attend one week and realize the program is not what you expected, you have no recourse. You are locked in for the full cost.

### 2 · Is there a pro rata provision?

Search the document for *pro rata*, *prorated*, *proportional*, *unearned*.

**FAIL if** absent. Without it, money is not returned in proportion to what was not delivered, no matter when you leave.

**Why this matters:** You are entitled to get back the cost of what you did not receive. This is in most state laws. The agreement should make it explicit.

### 3 · Is there a right to cancel?

Search for *cancel*.

**FAIL if** absent. A refund schedule is not a cancellation right. Many agreements have the first and not the second.

**Why this matters:** A refund schedule tells you what you get back at different points. A cancellation right tells you that you *can* leave at all. They are different instruments.

### 4 · If you leave, do you still owe?

Search for *balance*, *remaining*, *payment plan*, *continued contribution*.

**FAIL if** an unpaid balance survives your withdrawal. Under that structure, leaving costs money you have not yet paid, for instruction you will not receive.

**Why this matters:** Withdrawal should be free. If leaving triggers a debt for future instruction, you are locked in by cost, not by desire.

### 5 · Must a decision about you be given in writing, with a reason?

Refund determinations, scholarship denials, removal, suspension.

**FAIL if** absent. A determination that need never be communicated cannot be checked, appealed, or corrected.

**Why this matters:** A verbal decision is invisible. You cannot prove it was made. You cannot appeal it. You have no record. A written decision with a reason creates accountability.

### 6 · Is there an appeal?

From any denial, to anyone.

**FAIL if** absent. Without one, every outcome depends on who is listening that week.

**Why this matters:** Appeal procedures exist so that if one person makes a mistake or has a bad week, there is a second path. Without it, the first decision is final regardless of how it was made.

### 7 · Is there a written hardship procedure?

Not a person to email. A written procedure with a defined institutional response.

**FAIL if** absent. If the only route is a sympathetic individual, the support exists at their discretion and disappears with their capacity.

**Why this matters:** Hardship can happen to anyone. If the only help available is from a specific person who happens to be reachable and have bandwidth, you are depending on luck, not structure.

### 8 · What is the load rating?

Scheduled hours per week. Which are required and which are optional. What happens if you miss. Whether you may be assigned an unpaid role — peer leadership, pod navigation, mentoring other participants — and whether that is disclosed before you sign.

**FAIL if** the time and energy cost of participation is not stated anywhere in writing. Every elevator has a posted load rating. Programs that ask for months of your life generally do not.

**Why this matters:** You need to know whether you can carry what the program requires. You need to know before you pay, not three weeks in when you realize you cannot sustain it.

### 9 · Who takes the money?

Compare the entity named on the payment page or invoice with the entity making the promises in the marketing and the agreement.

**FLAG if** they are different. That is often ordinary — fiscal sponsorship, a contracted operator, a licensed program — and you should be able to learn which, in writing, before you pay.

**Why this matters:** If you have a problem, you need to know who to contact. If three different entities are involved, you need to know which one makes which decisions. You should not discover this after you have paid.

### 10 · What is the total charge?

Write down the list price, not your discounted price. Note whether any prerequisite program is required.

**Record it.** In several states, the list price determines whether the program is regulated at all.

**Why this matters:** Pricing determines regulatory jurisdiction in many states. A program priced just below the statutory threshold may fall outside the regulator's authority entirely; one priced just above it does not. You need to know which rules apply before you pay.

## Reading Your Result

**Zero fails.** Rare, and worth telling other people about.

**One or two fails.** Ask about them in writing before you pay. Keep the answer.

**Three or more.** The agreement was very likely assembled rather than designed. That is common and it is not an accusation. It does mean the document will not protect you, and that whatever protection exists depends on the goodwill of specific people continuing to have capacity.

Ask anyway. Ask in writing. Keep the reply.

## How to Build a Floor for Others

Once you have identified a defect using the method, the floor is what you build so the next person does not face it.

### The floor is specific to what is broken

In the worked case, the broken thing was the refund schedule. The floor is a refund policy that complies with the law and applies to all future participants.

In an elevator, the broken thing might be a cable that lacks a safety factor. The floor is an inspection code that tests every cable.

Do not build floors for problems that do not exist. Find the defect first.

### The floor is written

Verbal commitments end with the person who made them. A written floor persists. It can be audited. It can be verified by someone other than the builder.

### The floor is institutional, not personal

A floor that depends on a good director is not a floor. It is a favor. An institutional floor is built into the agreement, the policy, the procedure. It applies whether the original person is there or not.

### The floor protects people who cannot see it

The next participant will not read the new refund policy before they enroll. They will enroll anyway. The floor should protect them anyway. And it does — because the policy is now lawful, and the regulator will verify it.

### The floor is durable only when it has a maintenance mechanism

A floor that is built once and never reviewed will decay. A floor that is embedded in an agreement everyone signs, reviewed by a regulator, or audited by an external body — that persists.

### The floor is the gift

The floor is what you leave behind. It is what remains when you have left. It protects people you will never meet. It is the structure that makes the next person's arrival different from yours.

## When a Decision Is Not a Structure

In the worked case, after the method was applied, three people inside the operating organization talked, agreed, and made the refund right. That decision was real. That acknowledgment was genuine. But a decision is not a structure.

The next time that organization sees a participant reaching the end of their carry, three different people may be in those rooms. They may not have capacity. They may not remember this conversation. They may make a different choice.

A floor exists when the decision is no longer required because the structure makes the right choice automatic.

The floor is the enrollment agreement that clearly states the refund policy. It is the written determination that every refund gets a receipt. It is the appeal procedure that allows a participant to challenge a denial. It is the regulator's file that holds the complaint and verifies compliance.

These things work whether or not the original people are there, and whether or not they are having a good week.

That is the floor.
