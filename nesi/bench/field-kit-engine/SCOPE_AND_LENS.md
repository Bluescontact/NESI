# Field Kit Engine — Scope & Lens

*Founding document. Names the apparatus before the apparatus is fully built.*

**Version: v0.2** — adds cold-start vs compression mode distinction (Stage 1), names Communications Discipline as standing kit component (Stage 4), names Transmittal Packet as standing template. v0.1 changes from runs on TN Section 8 (cold-start, audience: tenant unions) and Sierra Electric defect dispute (compression, audience: practitioner-author Kevin Mears).

---

## What it is

An engine that takes a civic structural mess — a housing dispute, a surveillance proposal, a datacenter siting, a school closure, a water-infrastructure decision — and produces a **Field Kit**: a crystallized, distributable artifact set that a practitioner-ally (tenant union, opposition group, oversight body, advocacy org) can put to immediate use.

The form has already stabilized through three runs in 40 hours (data center activism, police camera opposition, TN Section 8). This document names what was running and gives it boundaries so the next civic substrate gets the structural lift the form is already providing, instead of being audited as another improvisation.

It is the **civic-substrate counterpart** to the Transmission Engine. Transmission Engine takes a finished article and produces RI-aesthetic transmission artifacts. Field Kit Engine takes a civic problem and produces practitioner-ready apparatus. They are siblings, not the same engine.

---

## What it produces

Every Field Kit contains:

1. **Structural audit** — the long doc. Federal floor cited, state/local layer mapped, four irreducible cases named, deadlines surfaced, leverage points enumerated, cross-cutting protections called out, organizing move named at the close.
2. **One-pager** — practitioner/citizen-facing. Locates the reader in under 60 seconds. First-48-hour moves. Who to call (jurisdiction-specific). What to demand. Points back to the deep doc.
3. **Recognition diagram** — the four shapes / vertices made visible. High-contrast organizing register (color-coded, accessible) as the default. RI register (dark field, luminous gold) optionally delegated to the Transmission Engine for the framework author's own channels.
4. **Sample letters & templates** — operational tools tied to the substrate's procedural openings (request-for-file, reasonable accommodation, defective-notice response, etc.).
5. **Crystallized field kit PDF** — single canonical file: cover + diagram + one-pager + audit. The distributable form.

## Scope

### In bounds

- Civic substrates where the dispute resolves into 3–5 irreducible cases (the tetrahedral compression test).
- Substrates with a federal / state / local statutory layer that can be mapped.
- Substrates where a practitioner-ally role exists or can be named (the audience).
- Jurisdictional grounding — at minimum state, ideally county or city.
- Substrates where the four-vertex geometry already wants to surface; the engine recognizes structure rather than imposing it.

### Out of bounds

- **Legal advice.** The kit names structural rights and procedural openings; an attorney walks them.
- **Specific case representation.** The kit is a map for a class of disputes, not a strategy for one named tenant or one named property.
- **Movement or political strategy beyond the structural level.** The kit names structural moves (organizing pattern across cases); it does not pick targets, design campaigns, or recommend tactics.
- **Substrates where the geometry refuses to compress.** If a civic mess is genuinely two-shape, write a Brief instead. If it's seven-shape, the geometry is wrong — recompress or refuse.
- **Multimedia.** Not a video engine. Not a podcast engine. PDF + SVG + markdown is the production surface.

## The Lens

What the engine looks for when it reads any civic substrate. Seven things, compressed to four vertices.

### Vertex 1 — WHO

- **Decision-maker map.** Who is moving against the harmed party? Who has authority to grant relief? Often more than one institution (e.g. Section 8: PHA + landlord + state court + HUD).
- **Audience role.** What practitioner-ally type sits between the individual case and the structural pattern for this substrate? (Tenant union. Opposition group. Oversight body. Watchdog org. Mutual aid network.)

### Vertex 2 — WHAT

- **Statutory floor.** Federal regs (CFR / USC), state code, local ordinances. Layered, not flat — the engine names which layer governs which question.
- **Deadlines already running.** The operational urgency map. Every adverse action generates writing; the dates on that writing govern everything.

### Vertex 3 — HOW

- **Leverage points.** Where structural rights exceed institutional defaults. Where the substrate gives the harmed party more than the institution is offering.
- **Cross-cutting protections.** The rear-vertex move. VAWA. Disability accommodation. Fair housing. Retaliation protection. Whistleblower protection. Protections that overlay every case in the substrate and are most often missed.

### Vertex 4 — AS PATTERN

- **The organizing move.** What can the practitioner-ally do that no individual lawyer can? Pattern-tracking across cases. Admin-plan collection. State preemption mapping. The structural defect that only shows up when N cases are stacked together.

These four vertices, edges between them, and the integrating center ("this isn't one fight, it's structured") form the engine's own tetrahedron. The engine has its own minimum system. It recognizes itself.

## Recognition tests

Each output must pass its own test. If it fails, regenerate.

| Output | Test |
|---|---|
| **One-pager** | A person reading it cold can locate their situation in under 60 seconds. |
| **Recognition diagram** | The four shapes can be grasped in 5 seconds at a glance. |
| **Structural audit** | A practitioner-ally can hand it to a lawyer and the lawyer says "this is half my work already done." |
| **Field kit PDF** | A tenant union receptionist can print it and hand it to a person in crisis without orientation. |

## Relationship to other engines

- **Transmission Engine** — invoked optionally for the RI-register visual layer (for the framework author's own channels). Field Kit Engine delegates that work; doesn't duplicate it.
- **Claude Dreaming layer** (planned) — feeds new civic substrates to the engine from monitored sources (news, policy changes, siting announcements). The engine drafts initial audits before the framework author is in the loop, leaves them as composted seeds for review.
- **Library substrate** — every kit feeds back into the Recognition Infrastructure library. Source-of-income preemption (TCA §66-35-102) entered the library through the Section 8 build. Social acupuncture and regulatory aikido entered through the datacenter build. The deployment is mutual recognition, not extraction.
- **Chief of Staff** — the orchestrator that decides which substrate gets a Field Kit and which gets a Brief, a transmission, or something else. The Field Kit Engine is one tool in the CoS's hand.

## Anti-register

What the engine refuses to produce, even when asked:

- A kit for a substrate that hasn't been pressure-tested by a real practitioner-ally relationship (no audience = no kit).
- A kit that talks about the practitioner-ally instead of being usable by them (every line in the one-pager must be operational; every line in the audit must be citable).
- A kit whose register reads as theory rather than apparatus (the engine produces tools, not arguments about tools).
- A kit that flatters the framework instead of disappearing into the substrate (the kit's audience should be able to use it without ever knowing Recognition Infrastructure exists).

## Production sequence (what's already running)

The pipeline runs in two modes. Mode selection happens at Stage 1.

### Stage 1 — Substrate read & mode selection

Confirm civic mess, jurisdiction, practitioner-ally audience, what's already on paper. Then determine the mode:

- **Cold-start mode.** No prior structural work exists in the substrate. The engine builds the four-shape geometry from raw material — paper, jurisdictional research, conversation. Stage 2 onward runs at full depth. TN Section 8 was cold-start: the four shapes did not pre-exist; the engine identified them from the federal regs + state code + practitioner-ally framing.

- **Compression mode.** The practitioner-ally has already done partial structural work — a competent demand letter, a draft brief, a prior audit. The geometry largely already exists in the seed material. Stage 2 onward runs as *compression and next-phase mapping* rather than from-scratch construction. The engine's job is to (a) verify the existing structure holds, (b) recompress where the seed is too dense or too loose, (c) add what the seed didn't address (typically: post-deadline phase, communications discipline, crystallization). Sierra Electric defect dispute was compression: the demand letter contained ~70% of the audit; the engine added the post-deadline map, communications discipline, and crystallized into a transmittable form.

**Mode-selection trigger:** does the substrate's seed material include accurate statutory citations and a defensible four-vertex (or near-tetrahedral) framing? If yes → compression. If no → cold-start.

**Failure mode at Stage 1:** projecting one substrate onto another. First action on receiving paper: explicitly verify *direction of harm* before naming shapes. In Sierra Electric the phrase "John hasn't coordinated to pick up his bike" was initially read as vendor-holding-customer-property (wrong substrate) before the paper revealed consumer-revocation (correct substrate). Recovery move: dump the projected substrate entirely; re-read from the paper.

### Stage 2 — Tetrahedral compression

Name the four irreducible cases. If geometry fails to compress to 3–5 vertices, recompress before continuing. In compression mode, the seed material's geometry is the starting hypothesis — verify or recompress, do not reinvent.

### Stage 3 — Structural audit drafted

Long doc with federal floor, state/local layer, deadlines, leverage, cross-cutting protections, organizing move. **Always include the Communications Discipline standing component** (see `templates/communications_discipline.md`). Templates appended where procedural openings exist.

### Stage 4 — One-pager extracted

Practitioner-facing summary, designed for cold reading in under 60 seconds.

### Stage 5 — Recognition diagram built

Organizing register (color-coded, high-contrast, accessible) as the default; RI register delegated to Transmission Engine if requested.

### Stage 6 — Field Kit crystallized

Cover + diagram + one-pager + audit assembled into single PDF via markdown → HTML → Edge headless print. **Always include the Transmittal Packet standing template** in the templates folder (see `templates/transmittal_packet.md`) — the cover sheet + multi-party CC list is where post-demand-letter leverage lives, regardless of substrate.

### Stage 7 — Status report

Geometry chosen, mode used, four shapes named, jurisdictional layer mapped, key deadlines surfaced, gaps flagged for the framework author to confirm before distribution.

This sequence runs once per substrate, in one of the two modes. It is the apparatus.

## Standing components (v0.2)

These two artifacts get pulled into *every* Field Kit, regardless of substrate. They live in this skill folder and are referenced (or copy-included) by the per-substrate kit at build time.

- **`templates/communications_discipline.md`** — the three doctrines (truth, opinion/fair comment, privilege) and three principles (conduct-not-character, watch legal-terms-of-art, hold public-facing reserve) that govern what the practitioner-ally can safely say in writing. Pasted into every audit. Per-substrate kit fills in the specific watch-words and example characterizations.
- **`templates/transmittal_packet.md`** — the cover-sheet form for shipping the kit's documentary record to the primary adverse party with multi-party CC to relevant agencies and stakeholders. The structural leverage move of any post-demand-letter phase. Pasted into every templates folder. Per-substrate kit fills in the adverse party, the CC recipients, and the enclosure inventory.
