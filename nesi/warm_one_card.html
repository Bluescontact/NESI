The Warm One
a game you play with pieces of your own life
1 ·Empty your pockets.
Put down a handful of things from your life lately — things you did, made, fixed, gave, or got through. Don't sort them. Don't rank them. Just lay them where you can see them.
2 ·Feel for the warm one.
Run your attention across them and wait. One will have a little heat on it — the one you keep coming back to, the one that means more than it should. You don't have to explain why. This part is only yours.
And if none of them is warm today — if nothing has heat — that's a real round too. Nothing is wrong. Let them be a pile, and let go.
3 ·Pull the thread.
Take the warm one and follow it. (Turn the card over.) Write one small true thing about yourself, in your own words. Not a score. Just something true.
4 ·Hand it over — or keep it.
That true thing is a small gift now. Give it to someone, or keep it. Once you give it, it's given — nobody owes you anything back.
5 ·Let go.
Put the round down. The pile goes back to being just a pile. Come back when you come back.
There's no score, no winning, no one keeping count. The game never tells you what you're worth. It only holds still while you do the seeing.

Pull the thread
Take the warm one. Answer in your own plain words — out loud, or here. There are no wrong answers, because no one is marking them.
What actually happened here?
Just the plain story of it.
What did it take from you?
What did it cost — time, nerve, effort, something given up?
What did you do that someone else couldn't have — or couldn't have, until you showed them how?
This is the one to sit with. Only if you want to.
