## RULED

### C1 · "spire" — the tetra edge. RULED.

`nesi.html:489` names it directly: *"spires: 0 = left edge (apex→left) · 1 =
base (left→right, head mid) · 2 = right edge (apex→right)."* Three descents,
built, running, and reachable — THE WATER TABLE station shows *"the three spires
— left · middle · right"* on screen (walked and screenshotted this session).

The competing reading — spire as exposed bedrock stack — came from
`world3d/scripts/terrain.gd`, which carries bedrock and outcrops. **There is no
bedrock object anywhere in the 2D build.** The fork had two bodies in the 3D
tree; in the active build it has one. Nothing renames, nothing moves.

*The cost the reconciliation named ("if that is wrong, everything in Part II §6
renames") does not apply here — Part II §6 is 3D-tree spec.*

### C2 · "membrane" — the tensioned routing surface. RULED, with a gated remainder.

The 2D build has already given the name a body. `nesi.html:1161`: *"THE MEMBRANE
— a station. Pull the sheet toward an anchor: a well forms and the water follows
it. Pull past its reach and it tears; it heals in its own time."* Built at 994,
registered as station st2 at 1123, with a tear-and-heal state at 1055. That is
precisely the "tensioned routing surface" reading, and it is walkable.

Kevin's original note said *"the second needs its own name."* The build answered
by giving the routing surface the name **membrane** outright. Ruled as built.

**The remainder is GATED, not ruled:** the *other* reading — membrane as the
lock across which a gift crosses to a second player — has no body in the active
build and is second-player/downstream-agency class. It is already filed at
`inbox/gift_2026-08-11_L4_second_player_recognition_log.md`, waiting on the
standing-open "second user is NOT upstream" fork. **No seat may name that
object; it needs a name that is not "membrane," and the naming is Kevin's.**

### C4 · the regeneration clock — neither words nor marks. RULED.

The fork as posed was *1000 words vs. mark count*. The build answered with a
third thing, and the third thing is better law than either option.

`nesi.html:279` — the water level is a continuous function of text mass:
`mass = sum of stone text lengths + unbanked field text`, then
`L = 1 - exp(-mass/1400)`. No threshold. No count. The light-cycle
(`S.cycles++`) advances separately, on the operator's own release at the dam
(line 789) — a hand-act, law 5.

**Why both original framings are retired rather than chosen between:** *"1000
words"* is a hidden counter with an activation at a boundary — structurally the
same shape this session ruled against in the three-lit-faces gate, and a law 2
risk the moment it becomes legible to the player. *"Mark count"* is the same
shape wearing a different unit. A continuous curve reaching an asymptote never
gives the player a number and never gives them a finish line to aim at.

**This closes the item flagged as most likely to be hit first** — the
reconciliation warned *"anything that makes water rise blocks here."* Nothing
blocks now; it is built and it is lawful.

### C5 · cross-session arrangement vs. the morning-pages guard — no conflict as built. RULED, with a live tripwire.

The 2D build does arrange across sessions: stones persist, they carry `run:
S.run` (line 224), and `S.run` increments when Enter on an empty line closes the
sitting (line 272). Stones from earlier runs remain in the tank and remain
placeable by hand.

**The guard is not violated, because the guard forbids the *system* reading the
writer — not the writer's own material persisting.** The morning-pages law bars
mood tracking, themes-across-mornings, sentiment, and reflection of inner state
back at Kevin. The build does none of these: it never reads, never summarizes,
never themes, never surfaces a pattern. It holds verbatim text where a hand put
it. `nesi.html:707` states the rendering rule: *"runs read as spacing — a gap
between sittings, no label, no number."*

**The tripwire, and it is the load-bearing half of this ruling:** the moment any
feature *reads across* sessions — a pattern finder, a theme surfacer, a "what
you've been circling" view, anything that looks at more than one sitting and
returns something the player did not put there — **C5 reopens immediately and is
gate-class, not mechanic-class.** This ruling licenses persistence and hand
arrangement. It licenses nothing that interprets.

## GATED — filed as cards, waiting on a naming that is Kevin's

### THE FIVE OUTPUTS · self recognition · capacity · amplification · grounding · support. GATED.

Unmapped to any place, organ, or move; nothing in the 2D build implements them.
This is **not** ruled, and deliberately so.

These five are descriptions of what a person receives — self-recognition,
capacity, amplification, grounding, support. Mapping them onto organs or moves
would make the mechanic decide what recognizes what in the person playing, which
is the exact inversion the corpus's own Recognition Law forbids: *the mechanic
never does the recognizing.* A seat ruling this would be a seat deciding what
counts as Kevin's self-recognition.

Filed: `inbox/gift_2026-08-11_the_five_outputs.md`.

**Not ruled by any seat, and deliberately not defaulted.** The cost of leaving it open is
visible; the cost of closing it by inference is a tally or a nominal site, and both are his

## RE-SEATED FROM A RETIRING GATE — the presence seam (2026-08-12)

**Status: OPEN. Kevin's.** Carried out of gate 15 of the nesi-other set before it closed.

Law 8 says *"the world runs while you are in it and stops when you leave"* — **"nothing moves
while you are away" is law.** What is NOT law, and is only where the build currently sits, is
the other half: **"nothing moves while you are here either."**

The one place the build already breaks it is the water table: the load falls out of suspension
*only while that panel is open and no key is pressed* — the single mechanic in either tree
where **withdrawal of the hand is itself an input.** Everything else waits for a click.

The fork: is stillness-with-you-present a world that is alive and unhurried, or a world that
is inert? Naming it because the answer decides whether anything else may ever move on its own
— and because the moment a second thing moves that the player did not cause, this stops being
a seam and becomes a policy.
