1 · The real material, checked before dreaming with it

solid.js already computes real volume — not a metaphor, a number. CELLS: 8 tetrahedra under the triangular faces (1 unit each), 6 pyramids under the square faces (2 units each), every one apexed at a real centre, using an actual tetrahedron-volume formula. It's checked by tools/solid_check.js and it's been correct since 2026-08-14. It populates twelve fixed, named organs — TANK, DAM, FILTER, and nine more — never sentences.

FACES.json already tried to site something onto that geometry once before, and it's honest about how far it got: 8 mechanics sited on the 8 triangles, each with a verb and a cost — but "the geometry gave the slots and not the tenants... scoring each mechanic against each triangle, the best total was 7 — achieved by 1872 of the 40320 possible matchings. The mechanical evidence does not pick one." Every siting there is still sited_by: "session", none marked, eleven days later.

That's the load-bearing fact this whole pass turns on: even with twelve fixed, well-understood, already-named organs, deciding what belongs where was genuinely underdetermined. Sentences are not fixed, not twelve, not named in advance, and arrive at whatever rate you write. A proposal that ignores this will fail the same way, worse.

3 · Diverge — five real shapes

A · The curated room strongest fit for "build a world"

You pick twelve sentences (or twelve stones — Dream B's grouping, still real, still unbuilt further) by hand. Those twelve become the seats of a real solid.js-derived shape: real edges, real faces, real volume, computed the same way the twelve organs' world already is. The world isn't "all your writing" — it's a room you built by choosing what belongs in it. Extends the already-proven four-pick pattern to the full twelve-seat geometry instead of stopping at four.

Cost: the biggest lift here — real UI for picking twelve, real layout math reused from a system that's never populated anything but its own organs before.

B · The eight lenses, sited in space smaller, but changes less than it adds

The eight grounds that already exist — relationship, time, weave, knowledge, categories, words, filter, garden — sited onto the eight triangular faces instead of a flat tab bar, reusing FACES.json's own precedent (site one thing per triangle). Walking the world would mean walking between your own lenses in real space.

Cost: real, but this mostly re-skins navigation that already works. It doesn't give the user anything new to build — the eight lenses exist regardless of where you stand to reach them.

C · The growing polyhedron most ambitious, biggest engineering risk

Instead of the fixed 12/14/24 cuboctahedron, a shape that grows a new cell every time you gather sentences into a stone — an open-ended solid whose form is a literal record of what you've built, not a container waiting to be filled. Most honestly matches "build their own world," most distant from anything that exists: solid.js is a pinned shape by design (THE_SOLID.md's own word), and nothing in this corpus has ever derived a solid that isn't fixed in advance.

Cost: real 3D geometry that grows correctly is a genuinely hard problem — worth a dream pass of its own if shape A proves out, not attempted blind.

D · The standing tetrahedron, made real cheapest, buildable today, reuses proven code

The weave ground already computes exactly this, flat: one anchor sentence (V0), three derived gifts (chain, near, declared — Dream E's own logic, already composted in). Give it real volume instead of a flat SVG diagram — the same _tet() tetrahedron-volume math solid.js already proved, run on four real points instead of twelve. Always exactly four vertices, never more, so it never meets the scaling wall §2 names.

Cost: smallest of the five. Mostly a rendering change to material that's already computed and already correct.

E · No new world the honest dissent

Every shape above either fights the scaling problem or reintroduces the exact underdetermination §1 already caught once. The counter-case: "world" might be better served by depth inside the flat lenses that already work — more grounds, richer connections — than by a 3D switch that costs real engineering to avoid the same trap that caught the organs. Kept as a real option, not dismissed for being the quiet one.

4 · Dream — the one idea worth building first

Shape D isn't the biggest dream, but it's the truest small step: it's the first place in this whole session where "where does volume start" and "the writing" would be the same sentence, not two separate systems living in the same repository. It costs almost nothing, because the hard part — deriving the three gifts from one anchor — was already built and audited on 2026-08-26. Giving it real volume instead of a flat readout is the smallest possible way to stop that answer being a metaphor.

Shape A is the actual destination your words are pointing at — build a world, not view one — and it's real and buildable, just bigger. The honest path is D first, proven, then A as what D's own room becomes once there's reason to build more than one.
