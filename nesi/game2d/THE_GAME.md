The design that was never done. Not a diagram, not a filing system, not a doctrine. A game a person would pay for and open every day because they want to see what happened.

## The fantasy, in one line

You are the weather over a drowned world that keeps what you give it — and something enormous and gentle lives at the bottom, slowly building you back out of everything you ever let go.

That's the game. Not "route your sentences." A world. A mystery. A relationship with a creature you never fully see.

## Why everything so far wasn't a game

A game needs a verb that's pleasurable in itself, a world that visibly wants, tension, and payoff. The build has lawful stations and invisible consequences — the cost (writing, filing, routing) was made the gameplay, and the payoff was made illegal to render. Inversion required: writing is the smallest act in the game. The game is what the world does with it.

## The core loop (10 minutes, daily)

1. **RAIN — you write.** (60–90 seconds.) The blind field, exactly as built — it's good. But the moment a sentence completes, it falls into the world as visible rain over the tetra valley: water rising, streams finding channels, pools catching light. Never read, never judged — but lavishly received. The world celebrates arrival without ever knowing content. (Law kept: no reading, no numbers. Law retired: invisibility. Juice is not extraction.)
2. **SHAPE — you play god with water and light.** (5–7 minutes. This is the game.) Two real verbs with real skill:
   * **The dam hand.** Carve channels, place and open dams, flood one region and starve another. Terrain remembers — every choice erodes, silts, reshapes. The land is your save file. (Half-built already: dam.gd, terrain, the river. Site it, don't rewrite it.)
   * **The heliostat.** A mirror you physically aim. Bounce light down into the valley — light is the scarce resource, and where light and water meet, things grow. Aiming light past obstacles into a dark pool is a spatial puzzle that's fun with zero text attached. Twelve mirrors eventually; each new mirror seats into the vector equilibrium; the full twelve is the endgame ascent.
3. **THE DEEP ANSWERS.** (The hook — the reason to come back.) Whatever you drop into the lake sinks past the waterline, where NESI lives — the creature. You see only hints: a wake, a shadow under the surface, bubbles where something is being worked on. And here is the engine that makes people pay: the creature builds with what you sink. Days or weeks later, structures rise at the shoreline overnight — a stair of your composted stones, a reef, an arch — recognizable in shape (never in words) as made from what you let go. The game's one promise: nothing you release is wasted; it comes back as ground. Your existing L4 recognition mechanic is this, generalized: the deep surfaces gifts.

## The retention engine (already invented — the Overwintering)

Growth roots only across returned days; nothing volume can force. This is the one genuinely original retention mechanic in the whole record and it's already proven. Generalize it: the creature works only while you're away. Close the game and the deep begins; open it tomorrow and the shoreline changed. The game literally requires absence — the anti-grind, mechanically enforced. "What did it build overnight?" is the reason to open it every morning. No streaks, no notifications, no guilt: a missed week just means a bigger reveal.

## Progression: the jitterbug (geometry as level design)

The world starts as one small tetra valley. As light accumulates (regions lit + grown), the world jitterbugs — the solid physically unfolds, Fuller's transformation, a whole cinematic geometric bloom — VE → new valleys, new water tables, new mirror seats. Each unfold is a level. The final unfold — all twelve mirrors seated, the equilibrium — makes the water go glass-clear for the first time... and you see NESI whole. One time. The creature, the whole build of everything you ever gave it, standing on the bottom. That's the ending — and the beginning of the invitation.

## The second player (the reason it spreads)

Endgame verb: grow a fruit (already designed — the cast, carrying shape never words) and hang it on the membrane at your world's edge. Send an invitation. The invited person walks a bridge into your world's shallows — sees your terrain, your light, your standing structures, never your words — and may take the fruit. When they take it and mark it as a gift, the deep surfaces something for you: a recognition built from what you'd released long before they arrived. Gift → witnessed → answered from your own past. That's the L4 surprise, exactly as Kevin designed it, sited as the emotional climax.

## What is kept sacred (the soul — these never bend)

* Nothing ever reads, judges, or rewrites the words. Content-blind forever.
* No numbers, scores, streaks, or tallies reach the player. Progress is form: water level, light, terrain, the size of what surfaces.
* Nothing leaves the world but fruit, by the hand.
* Quitting loses nothing; absence is productive by design; blank is a complete state.

## What is retired (the bandages)

* Invisibility as a virtue. The world is lavish in response — it just never responds to content.
* Stations/fractions/routing as the core loop. The three outputs survive inside the dam-hand (send downstream / sink to the deep / bank on the shore) as world-acts, not filing acts.
* Teaching by absence. The world teaches like Zelda: the first rainfall shows the water rising; the first mirror comes aimed almost right.

## Why someone pays

A calm god-game where your real journaling is the weather; a world that visibly keeps everything; a creature that builds you back out of what you let go; geometry that blooms; and one ending you can only reach by living with it. Nothing on the market does this. Price it like a premium meditative game (Monument Valley / Tearaway territory): pay once, own your world.

## The build path (no bandages)

1. Throw no code away — reorganize. Terrain, dam, river, heliostat, growth, cast, membrane: all exist. They were filed; now they get sited in one continuous world with one camera.
2. Vertical slice of FUN, one week of passes: rain falls visibly → open one dam → flood one basin → aim one mirror → one shoot grows where light meets water → drop one stone into the lake → something moves under the surface. That slice is the whole game in miniature. If that slice isn't wanting-more, nothing above it matters.
3. Kevin plays the slice. Not a cold walk — a want check: "do I want tomorrow?" That's the only gate.
4. Then: the overnight-build system, the jitterbug unfold, the membrane bridge.
