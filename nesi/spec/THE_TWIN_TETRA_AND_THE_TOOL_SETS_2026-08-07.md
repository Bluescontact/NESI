# PART I · THE COUNTING, WHICH IS THE WHOLE CONTENT

Kevin's line is *"6 relationships that define the faces."* A tetrahedron has **4 vertices, 6 edges, 4 faces.** Six and four are not the same number, and the mismatch is where the idea has its teeth.

## 1 · An edge is a relationship. A face is what three of them make when they close.

Each face is bounded by three edges. Each edge is shared by exactly two faces. `6 × 2 = 12 = 4 × 3` — the incidence is exact, and it is **not one-to-one**.

> **A face is not a fourth thing added to three edges. It is the closure of three relationships.**

That is the mechanism the phrase was reaching for. A tool is not *made from* relationships the way a table is made from wood. **A tool is three relationships holding at once.**

## 2 · Closure is scarce, and that is the constraint that makes it real

Three edges chosen from six can be combined `C(6,3) = 20` ways. **Only four of those twenty close into a face.** The other sixteen are triples of relationships that do not make anything — they wander off, or they share a vertex without enclosing.

> **Sixteen of twenty triples build nothing. A tool exists only where three relationships mutually hold, and most triples do not.**

This is the load-bearing consequence and it is why the idea is not decorative: **you cannot assemble a tool by picking three relationships you like.** The geometry refuses most combinations. A tool set is not a menu.

## 3 · Every tool excludes exactly one vertex — its pole

In a tetra each vertex is opposite exactly one face. So each face — each tool — has one vertex it does not contain.

> **A tool is defined as much by the pole it operates from as by the three it holds.**

Four tools, four excluded vertices, one each. Nothing is left over and nothing is doubled. *This is derived, not Kevin's, and it is the cleanest thing in the development: it says a tool always has a blind side, and the blind side is never arbitrary.*

## 4 · One tetra is a tool set of exactly four

> **ONE TETRA = FOUR TOOLS. The tool set is the tetra.**

Not "a tetra can hold tools." The four faces *are* the four tools, and there is no fifth. **That is what makes a tool set portable** — Kevin's *"they can be used in multiple places"* is satisfied by the shape itself, because anywhere there are four things in relation, the tool set is already there and only needs naming.

## 5 · Tools compose across a shared edge — and that is a function

Any two faces of a tetra share exactly one edge. So any two tools share exactly one relationship.

> **A function is a path across tools, and every step of the path crosses one shared relationship.**

Four faces, six edges: the adjacency graph of the four tools is **K₄ — every tool is adjacent to every other, and no tool is isolated.** Which means: *from any tool you can reach any other in one step, and the step is always through a relationship you already hold.* There is no tool you have to pass through to get somewhere else, and no tool that is a dead end.

**Derived, and offered as the reading, not ruled.**

# PART II · THE TWO JOINTS

The whole of the twin-tetra mechanic follows from one fact: **what two solids share when they touch.**

| | shares | dimension | rigid? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **JOINED AT A POINT** | 1 vertex · 0 edges · 0 faces | 0-dimensional | **no** — free to rotate |
| **SHARING A FACE** | 3 vertices · 3 edges · 1 face | 2-dimensional | **yes** — locked |

**That is the mechanic, stated once:** *a point-join is flexible and carries almost nothing. A face-join is rigid and carries a whole surface.* Everything below is that sentence with its parts named.

## 6 · The choke point

Two tetras touching at one vertex. **No edge is shared, so no relationship crosses. No face is shared, so no tool crosses.** Only the point is common.

> **A choke point transmits ONE THING AT A TIME, in sequence, and nothing as a surface.**

Three properties fall out without being added:

- **Bandwidth is minimal by construction.** There is no way to widen it that does not change the joint into a different joint.
- **Order is forced.** Two things cannot pass abreast through a point. Whatever crosses, crosses in a sequence, and the sequence is real information about what was let through first.
- **It is free to rotate.** Two tetras joined at a point can turn about that point without breaking. *The two worlds are attached and not aligned.*

**And it is already in the corpus under another name.** A lock's two gates are never open at once; nothing crosses by drifting; crossing is deliberate, one vessel at a time, and takes as long as it takes. **A lock is a choke point with a procedure written on it.**

### Which vertices are in contact — Kevin's question, three readings, none picked

**J1 · APEX TO APEX.** The two upper vertices touch. An hourglass: two worlds pointing at each other, each meeting the other at the place it opens from. Everything that crosses passes through both source rooms. *The strongest reading of a gift, and the most expensive: nothing reaches the other world without going up through its own headwaters first.*

**J2 · BASE VERTEX TO BASE VERTEX.** Two rim corners touch. The worlds are side by side at ground level, joined where deposit gathers. *The cheapest crossing and the one nearest to how two people actually meet — at the edges of what each has already built, not at the source.*

**J3 · APEX TO BASE.** One world's source touches another's ground. **Asymmetric, and the asymmetry is the point:** what is upstream for one is downstream for the other. This is the only reading with a direction built into the joint, and it is the shape of a gift that is not returned.

**Not picked. Each is a different claim about what two worlds meeting means, and that is Kevin's.**

*Note, derived: D-2 dreamt that the rim is where two worlds touch — but the rim is an EDGE, and an edge-join is a fourth joint neither of tonight's marks names. Two tetras sharing exactly one edge share two vertices and one relationship, and no tool. That sits between the point and the face, and it is unclaimed.*

## 7 · The membrane

Two tetras sharing a face. **Three vertices, three edges, and one whole tool are common to both.** Rigid — no relative rotation is possible.

> **A face-join means the two worlds SHARE A TOOL. Not a copy of one; the same one.**

That is the sharpest consequence in the development and it was not obvious before the counting: at a shared face, three relationships hold in both worlds simultaneously, and the tool they close into belongs to neither. **The membrane is a jointly held instrument.**

### Kevin's three variants, and each is already an object in this system

**M1 · THE SINGLE MEMBRANE.** One surface, both sides against it. What is on it is on it for both. **No inside, no gap, nothing between.** This is the tension membrane of the workshop — a surface whose shape a hand sets, and which both sides are standing on.
*Cost, named: there is nowhere to be in transit. You are on one side or the other and the change is instantaneous. No chamber, no held state, no "in neither water."*

**M2 · THE DOUBLE MEMBRANE WITH A SHARED VOLUME.** Two surfaces with a gap. **The gap is a chamber, and the chamber is the honest part.**
This is the lock, and the corpus already wrote its description: *"there is a moment in every crossing when you are in neither water — near gate shut, far gate not yet open, floating at a level that belongs to nobody."* **The shared volume is that moment given a body.**
*It is the only one of the three that can hold something mid-crossing. It is also the only one with an interior that neither world owns.*

**M3 · THE DECISION SURFACE / TREE.** The face is not passive. What arrives on it is sorted, and where it goes depends on what it is.
This is DS_v1 — the board, the rooms, the tiles — **and the fact that the widget chassis turns out to be one of the three legal states of a shared face is either the best thing in this development or a coincidence worth being suspicious of.** Named as both.
*Cost: a sorting surface has an author. M1 and M2 pass things without reading them; M3 cannot. That puts the recognition law directly on this variant and nowhere else — a decision surface that sorts by reading content is the one thing this whole system refuses. It survives only if the sorting is by declared grain or by hand.*

### The three are settings of one joint, not three joints

**M1 → M2 → M3 is an increasing order of what the boundary does.** M1 holds a shape. M2 holds a shape and an interior. M3 holds a shape, an interior, and a rule. **Nothing says a world must pick one for all its faces** — a tetra has four, and they could differ.

**The claim this makes, and it is checkable:** if a tool is a closed triple of relationships and every tetra has exactly four, then **the menu has four tools and nobody has named them.** Same for the stations. The vertices are named in both cases; **the faces are not named anywhere in this system.**

> **The gap this development opens: every tetra in NESI has its four vertices named and its four faces unnamed. Four instances, sixteen unnamed tools.**

*That is a finding, not a task list. Naming a face is naming what three relationships make when they close, and that is Kevin's work, not a generator's.*

**And the portability Kevin asked for is structural, not a feature.** A tool set moves between places because it is not attached to the places — it is the shape four things in relation always have. *Nothing has to be written to make a tool set portable. It has to be noticed.*

# PART IV · THE DIVERGENT PASS (clause 3, current per A6)

*One divergent pass, reaching past feasibility. Un-ranked. Not verified by its author. Enters as UNMARKED intake under clause 4.*

**t-1 · A tool you have not used is not a tool yet — it is three relationships that happen to close.** A face becomes a tool the first time something crosses it. Until then the tetra has four faces and no tools, which is the correct state of an unwritten world.

**t-2 · The pole is where you stand to use the tool.** If every tool excludes one vertex, then that vertex is not a blind spot — it is the handle. **You cannot be inside the tool you are using.**

**t-3 · A choke point is how one person gives; a shared face is how two people build.** The joint is not a design choice about topology. It is a statement about what the relationship is, and picking one has already answered a question about the other person.

**t-4 · The shared volume is the only place in the whole system that belongs to nobody.** Everywhere else has an owner: your catchment, your deposit, their world. The chamber between two membranes is the one room with no owner, and that may be what a commons actually is.

**t-5 · Sixteen triples build nothing, and that is the most honest thing here.** Most combinations of relationships do not close. A system that let you assemble any three into a tool would be lying about how thinking works.

**t-6 · Two tetras joined at a point can rotate, so the joint has a state nobody set.** The angle between two worlds is not authored and not computed — it is whatever it currently is. That is the first thing in NESI that has a value with no author at all.

**t-7 · A function is a walk, and a walk that returns to where it started has done something a straight path cannot.** Four tools in K₄ means there are closed circuits. A function that comes back is not the same as one that arrives.
