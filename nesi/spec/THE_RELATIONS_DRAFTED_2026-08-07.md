# THE RELATIONS — drafted

**Kevin's go, 2026-08-07: "fill the container agreement. fill the rim. develop the menu, and then the stations."**

**DRAFTED, NOT LAW.** All night the discipline has been that writing a relation is Kevin's. He has now asked for them written. So they are written **as proposals, every one unmarked and reversible**, in the one form this system has a model for: the Tetra Builders Agreement's *"relations that make it rigid."*

**The form, and it is strict.** A relation does not describe its two vertices. **It states what is true because both exist** — a constraint neither vertex carries alone. Anything that only describes an endpoint was cut.

# II · THE RIM — filled, and the geometry named it

**The three rim edges turned out not to be arbitrary.** Each corner of the world is a spire's outfall: 90° is where THE GROWN SPIRE lands, 210° THE GIVEN, 330° THE WOVEN. **So a rim edge is not a line between two places. It is the boundary between two waters' deposits.**

> **The rim is the three pairings of the three waters. There is no fourth pairing and no fifth rim edge.**

**The rim law, one sentence for all three:**

> *Two waters lay down different ground. They meet along the rim, they do not mix, and the line where they meet is the only place in the world you can see the difference between them.*

| | relation |
|---|---|
| **w1** | **GROWN–GIVEN** *(90°–210°)* · *Where your own work lies against what was given to you. Neither erodes the other. The line stays visible because the two waters carried different things, and it goes dry on the Given side until someone gives.* |
| **w2** | **GROWN–WOVEN** *(90°–330°)* · *Where your own work lies against what came back changed. The only rim edge whose two sides can be made of the same material at different times.* |
| **w3** | **GIVEN–WOVEN** *(210°–330°)* · *Where what they shared lies against what you made of it together. Both dry today, and two dry channels meeting is a complete configuration, not a fault.* |

**And this closes something that was open.** D-2 dreamt *"the rim is where two worlds touch."* Under this filling the rim is where **two waters** touch inside one world — which does not refute D-2 but says the rim already has a job. **If two worlds also touch there, the rim is doing two things, and that is C1's problem repeating with a different word.** Flagged, not resolved.
