## 1 · THE CONVERGENCE — the container law and the watershed are the same object

This was not aimed for and it is the largest thing in the document.

At the start of this session the containers were named as a state chain derived from the verbs: **raw → sorted → substrate → dispositioned → canon → released.** Six states, adopted, and two of them — `sorted` and `dispositioned` — had **no container anywhere on disk** and were flagged as possibly not real places (assumption a4).

Dreaming the states a thing can be in, in water, without reference to that work, produces **four**:

| In water | The container-law state | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **IN SUSPENSION** | raw | carried by the water, not yet set down anywhere |
| **SETTLED** | substrate | deposited; part of a bank you can stand on |
| **STANDING** | canon | a stack; it survived every washing |
| **ACROSS** | released | through the lock, in water that is not yours |

**`sorted` and `dispositioned` have no water state at all.** Sediment is either carried or it is settled; there is no third condition between them. And a disposition is not somewhere a grain of sand can *be* — it is a fact about it.

Two independent derivations, from opposite directions, on different days, agree: **the chain is four, not six.** The verbs said six and the water says four, and the two the water drops are exactly the two that never had a container.

**This is what "a whole ecosystem in convergence" would look like if it happened.** The infrastructure work and the dreamt world are not two projects — they are one structure in two registers, and they can now check each other. That check has fired once already, tonight, and it agreed with the doubt rather than with the adopted law.

**The other three container kinds land too, without forcing:** an EVENT ledger is the tally a lock keeps of what passed and which way — it never keeps the thing. A SURFACE is a chart: drawn from soundings, useful, and holding no water of its own. An ORGAN is the vessel and the gates — the things that act rather than the things acted on.

## 2 · THE PASSAGES — how you move between places

The four moves of `THE_SOUNDING` — drift, stand in and off, come about, sound — are what you do **within** a water. These are how you get from one place to another. Each is a real thing a person on water does and none of them needs explaining.

**RUNNING DOWN.** Going with the current. Effortless, fast, and it only goes one way — toward the lake, toward the middle, toward where everything gathers. This is the default motion of the whole watershed. Left alone, you end up in the middle.

**BEATING UP.** Working against the current toward the headwaters. It is slow and it is real work and **there is no other way back up.** You cannot drift upstream. The only way to be upstream again is to write again — which is not a rule anyone imposed, it is what a watershed is.

**STANDING OFF.** Holding position away from a thing without approaching it. This is a deliberate act, not an absence of one: you are keeping your distance and you are working to do it. **Standing off a deep is a complete way to play** — you know it is there, you are not going in, and staying out takes effort.

**WARPING.** Hauling yourself in by a line, hand over hand, with no engine and no way to hurry. This is how you come alongside: into the dry dock, up to a mooring. It is the slowest passage in the watershed and it is slow because precision needs it. **You cannot warp fast; that is the whole nature of the manoeuvre.**

**LOCKING THROUGH.** The passage that takes the time it takes. Near gate shuts, the level changes, the far gate opens from the other side. It cannot be rushed, and for part of it you are in neither water.

**FETCHING.** Making for something you can see but are not near — the stacks from the shoals, a mark on the far shore. You choose it and you go, and it takes a while, and it is visible the whole way. **Nothing is ever hidden behind a passage in this world.** You can always see where you are headed before you commit.

## 3 · THE ACTION STATES — what state you are in

Not modes and not settings. **Conditions**, and every one of them is a fact about your relation to the water rather than a judgment about you.

**AGROUND.** Not enough water under you. Nothing is wrong; the river is low. This is where every session starts and it is where a short one stays.

**AFLOAT.** Lifted. The draw is met and the water is carrying you. Everything else in this list happens from here.

**UNDERWAY.** Afloat and moving under your own choice — running down, beating up, fetching.

**ADRIFT.** Afloat, moving, not choosing. The current has you and you are not steering. **This is a legitimate state and not a failure**; the four faces re-express the ground whether or not you are steering, and some of the best looking happens while drifting.

**MADE FAST.** Tied off at the mooring. The line is taut, something is holding you, and you are not the one under load.

**HIGH AND DRY.** In the dry dock, water down, standing on the floor beside the thing you are working on. Nothing drifts here and nothing arrives.

**IN THE CHAMBER.** Inside the lock. Near gate shut, far gate not open, floating at a level that belongs to nobody. **The only state in the watershed that is not in any water**, and every crossing has one.

**AT THE END OF THE LINE.** Sounding, and the line ran out. You are over a deep. Nothing happens; you simply know.

## 4 · THE STATE OF A THING — what your material is doing

Four, as §1 found, plus the one condition that is not a state:

**IN SUSPENSION.** Just written. Carried, not set down. Everything you write starts here.

**SETTLED.** Deposited into a bank. Part of a shoal, standing above the waterline in your own words. Reachable, walkable, unchanged.

**STANDING.** A stack. It was there while everything softer went past it. Nothing puts a thing here; **outlasting does.**

**ACROSS.** Through the lock, in water that is not yours. The far gate opened from the other side, which is the only way anything gets here.

**And the condition that is not a state: IN THE CHAMBER.** A thing being crossed is in neither water. It has left yours and has not arrived. This is the honest name for what the membrane's transition record has always been recording — **not a state, a passage with a duration.**

## 5 · WHAT THIS SETTLES, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT

**Settled by the water:** the state chain is four. `sorted` and `dispositioned` have no condition a thing can be in. Two derivations agree.

**Settled by the passages:** you cannot get back upstream without working for it, and the only work that raises the water is writing. Nothing had to impose that.

**Not settled:**

- **What moves a thing from settled to standing.** Erosion is time and washing, and neither has a body yet. §7c's weather cycle is the obvious candidate and it is still unbuilt and unnamed in this register.
- **Whether a thing can go back into suspension.** Water re-suspends sediment all the time when the flow picks up. Whether your settled material can be lifted and carried again is unnamed, and it bears directly on whether compost is a place or a disposition — still open from earlier tonight.
- **Who else is on the water.** Every state above is single-vessel. The Range needs two and the Lock needs someone on the far side, and neither has a naming for how another person is present.
