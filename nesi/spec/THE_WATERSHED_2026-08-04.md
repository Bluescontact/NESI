# THE WATERSHED — the other six places, dreamt in the same medium

**A naming. Nothing built.** Companion to `THE_SOUNDING_2026-08-04.md`, which named one room. Kevin: *"dream the other six places in the same medium."*

**Method, unchanged:** every line is a stated default. One mark accepts the whole; any line amends in Kevin's own words and the rest stands. The constraint set while dreaming holds: **nothing here may need explaining.** Everyone already knows what a gate does, what happens when you tie up, what it means when two things line up.

---

## 0 · What came out of dreaming them together

I did not expect this and it is the largest thing in the document.

Named one at a time, the seven places are a list. Named in one medium, **they turn out to be a single watershed** — one water system with a direction, from the high ground down to the middle:

> **the Headwaters** → *the river* → **the Shoals** → **the Lake** *(the middle, where everything gathers)* → **the Dam** → **the Lock** → *water that is not yours*

The Spires stand off the shoals in deep water. The Dry Dock is cut into the bank. The Mooring is in sheltered water. The Range is two marks you sight across the whole thing.

**This lands exactly on §7e without being aimed at it.** The world is a tetrahedron, you enter at the apex, the ground is the face opposite, and *the lake is the center of the map*. A watershed inside that shape is what you would get anyway: **you come in at the top, and water runs downhill to the middle.** The geometry and the medium were named on separate days and they fit.

It also means the map has a direction, which a list of seven places did not. **Everything is downstream of writing.** Not as a metaphor — as terrain.

## 1 · THE SPIRES — what did not wash away

Three of them, already named by Kevin: **the Grown**, **the Given**, **the Woven**. In water, a spire is a stack — rock left standing when the sea took everything softer around it.

**That is what canon is.** Not what was built up. **What survived every washing.** The 176 patterns in the library are the things that did not erode, and a stack is the only structure in nature that is made entirely by subtraction.

They stand off the shoals in deep water, tall enough to be visible from anywhere in the shallows. You do not construct them and you cannot add to them by wanting to — **a thing becomes a stack by outlasting the water around it.** Which is what a crossing already is.

- **THE GROWN STACK** — rock from your own bed. It was always your ground; the water only revealed its shape.
- **THE GIVEN STACK** — carried here by a current that was not yours and set down. Geologists call a rock like that an erratic: it does not match the country it sits in, and that is how you know it travelled.
- **THE WOVEN STACK** — at a confluence, where two flows meet and both drop what they were carrying. It is made of two sediments and cannot be sorted back into them. This is why a return files here: work that went out and came back changed is, by nature, made of both.

§7b already said three spires, three indices, one memory. Three stacks, one bedrock, says the same thing without needing a database word.

## 2 · THE DRY DOCK — the workshop

The seventh organ, the private build space. Everywhere else in this world, things drift, currents act, and what is loose moves.

**A dry dock is where you get the water out.**

You bring something in, **close the gate behind it**, let the water down, and stand on the floor beside it in air. Nothing drifts. Nothing arrives while you are working. Nothing you are holding gets carried off. It is the only place in the watershed where the medium is deliberately absent, and that absence is the whole point — **you cannot work on a hull from a boat.**

The gate answers the naming exactly: *everything in a workspace is imported and approved for sharing before it enters.* A dry dock has one entrance, it is a gate, and **it is shut while you work.** Nothing gets in by drifting.

The tension table and the graft bench stand on the dock floor. So does the vessel, when she needs work — which is the only time you are not riding her.

## 3 · THE MOORING — the hearth

Continuation. The sitting-with place. The tavern is its social face.

You are still on the water. You are not underway. **You have tied off, and something is holding you.**

That is what continuation is, and it is not stopping — a mooring holds through the night while you are not steering, and it is doing real work the whole time. The line is taut. You are simply not the one under load.

**Its social face costs nothing to explain:** more than one boat ties to the same mooring. That is the tavern. Nobody is going anywhere, everyone is held by the same tackle, and the water underneath is shared.

Move 5 of the round — *put the round down, come back when you come back* — is tying off. Nothing is finished. You let go of the tiller and the ground keeps holding.

## 4 · THE RANGE — the heliostat

The convener. Kevin's own words: overlap computed *"by alignment of light ie yes marked"* — geometry, never recommendation.

**A range is two marks that line up.**

It is a real navigational object and it does nothing at all. Two beacons stand in fixed places. From most positions they appear apart. From one line of positions, **the near one sits exactly in front of the far one** — and if you can see that, you are on the range. Move off it and they separate.

Everything the heliostat needs is already true of a range and none of it had to be added:

- **It computes nothing.** The marks stand still. You are the one who moves and sees.
- **It cannot recommend.** It has no way to say a position is good. It can only be in line or not.
- **It requires two.** A single mark is not a range. This is the convener's whole reason to exist: *a table needs an invitation, not hands.*
- **Nobody is told.** Two people on the same water, each seeing their own alignment, are not sent a message. They look, and it either lines up or it does not.

The sun forge is what happens when it does: light that was travelling separately arrives on one line.

## 5 · THE LOCK — the membrane

Two workspaces divided, each explorable by the other. Only gifts cross. The receiver's mark makes the gift. Circulation is the record.

**A lock joins two waters that are not at the same level, and it never lets them mix.**

You come in. **The gate closes behind you.** The water in the chamber changes to meet the far side. Then the far gate opens and you go out into water that was not yours.

Every clause of the membrane is already a physical fact about a lock:

- **The two waters never touch.** Both gates are never open at once. That is not a policy; it is what a lock is.
- **Nothing crosses by flowing.** Drift gets you nowhere near it. Crossing is deliberate, one vessel's worth at a time, and it takes as long as it takes.
- **You cannot open the far gate.** This is the piece I did not expect the metaphor to carry, and it carries it exactly: **the gate onto the other water is opened from the other side.** A gift is made a gift by the receiver, and in a lock the receiver is the only one with a hand on that gate.
- **Each side can see through.** You can stand at the gate and look into the other water without entering it.
- **Circulation is the record.** A lock keeps a tally of what passed and which way. It does not keep what passed.

**The chamber itself is the honest part.** There is a moment when you are in neither water — the near gate shut, the far gate not yet open, floating in a level that belongs to nobody. That is a real place and every crossing has one.

## 6 · THE DAM — already named, placed here

§7d named this fully and it does not need renaming. What dreaming the others adds is **where it sits and what you do there.**

It sits between the Lake and the Lock: **everything gathers at the middle, and the dam is what stands between what has gathered and what leaves.**

Its three laws are already Kevin's: holding is not producing · power comes from the drop, not the water · restriction is legitimate and visible. What the watershed adds is that **a held gate now has a downstream.** Water held at the dam is water not arriving at the lock — so a closed gate is not only a normal state, it is legible as *what is not crossing yet*.

**Two acts, and they are not the four moves.** In the shoals you drift, stand in and off, come about, and sound — all attention, none of them touching anything. At the dam you **hold** or you **release**, and both change the world downstream. That is a different kind of act and it belongs to a different pair of hands: the four are how you look at your own water, these two are how you govern it.

**A dam and a lock are the same object put to opposite work.** One is a gate that stops water and passes nothing. The other is a gate that passes vessels and stops water. Both are how a person operates a boundary, and it is worth noticing they are the same technology.

## 7 · THE LAKE — why it is not on this list

The Lake is not dreamt here as a sixth place, and the reason matters.

**§7e already made it the center of the map**, and `THE_SOUNDING` made water the thing you are in rather than a location you visit. Put those together and the Lake is not somewhere you go — **it is what everywhere else is standing in.** The shoals are shallow lake. The stacks rise out of it. The mooring floats on it. The dam holds it. The lock meters it.

Naming it as one place among seven would make it smaller than it is. It is the middle, it is the medium, and **THE DEEP is inside it** — dark by construction, dark even to Kevin, where your line does not reach and only the vessel can go.

That is the whole of it, and it was named on 08-02 and again tonight. Adding a sixth entry would only have repeated it.

## 9 · WHAT THIS COSTS, AND WHAT IS STILL UNNAMED

**The seduction to be honest about.** A single medium that absorbs every place is a strong signal and also a strong temptation. Each of these fit *easily*, and easy fit is exactly when to be suspicious — a metaphor that explains everything has usually stopped being tested. **The dry dock is the one I would look at hardest**: the workshop is the seventh organ and the only place where the medium is deliberately absent, which either makes it the most honest name here or the one that had to be forced.

**Still unnamed:**

- **How you travel between them.** Drift, stand in and off, come about and sound are moves within a water. Getting from the shoals to the stacks, or into the lock, is a passage, and passages are unnamed.
- **What a gift looks like in a lock.** The crossing is named; the thing crossing is not. Stones, seeds, tools, lenses, devices — the kinds typology has been flagged open since 07-31 and nothing here closed it.
- **What the weather does.** §7c's fog → rain → wash → carved reliefs would act on every one of these, and hardest on the stacks — erosion is how they were made in the first place. Left alone rather than guessed at.
- **Where the Terminal went.** It was the seventh row of the organ registry and it has become the Headwaters, which is a place rather than a surface. Whether that is a promotion or a loss is not mine to say.
- **The Shore.** Still unreconciled with the Shoals, still flagged from `THE_SOUNDING`. It is the one seam this document did not close and the one that would most repay closing.
