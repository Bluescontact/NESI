The headline finding: two numbering systems share the words "law N" in this corpus. LEARNED.md's current 30-law sequence is one. A second, older sequence — which nesi/game2d/tools/refusal_check.js names explicitly at its own line 8 as "PROTOCOLS.md's twelve laws" — is the other. Every bare "law N" comment inside solid.js and most of them inside index.html and options.html cite the second system, correctly, within it. Two citations in index.html name LEARNED.md outright and match it exactly. Read as one flat sequence, several of these look like errors. Read as two real, coexisting systems, they aren't — but the collision itself is real and worth surfacing plainly, which is what the Relationship tab does.

This file does not canonize which numbering is "correct" going forward — that is not this edge's authority. It states what each citation actually points to, sourced to a file:line, so a later reader is never left guessing which of the two systems a given "law N" means.

Resolution 1 — solid.js:34's "Law 2"

Confirmed: not an error in the comment, but a real numbering collision. Read in context (solid.js:34, quoted above), the comment is about numbers never being shown to the player — nothing to do with stranger verification. It does not match LEARNED.md's actual law 2 ("VERIFICATION NEEDS A STRANGER"). It matches, exactly, the separate numbering refusal_check.js:151 defines in its own words: "law 2 — no number reaches the player." That file's own header (refusal_check.js:8) names its source as "PROTOCOLS.md's twelve laws", not LEARNED.md. So solid.js:34 is citing the older, still-real PROTOCOLS-native numbering correctly — the mismatch is only against LEARNED.md's newer 30-law sequence, which post-dates and renumbers the material solid.js's comment was actually written against.

Resolution 2 — index.html:1459's "law 3/11"

Confirmed: same category of finding as Resolution 1, not a separate problem. index.html:1459's comment attributes "law 3/11" to refusal_check directly, in its own text — and refusal_check.js:138 and its header (:15-16) do define exactly that pairing: "law 3 (no model call) and law 11 (nothing reaches outward) together." This citation is internally correct and traceable to its stated source. It does not describe LEARNED.md's law 3 (verification is prestress, not identity) or law 11 (one manifest per tetra) — neither is about network primitives. Both citations are accurate to the system they actually name (refusal_check's own "PROTOCOLS.md's twelve laws"); read against LEARNED.md alone, both look wrong. They aren't wrong — they're citing a different, still-live numbering that the corpus has never fully reconciled with LEARNED.md's current sequence.

What this confirms about the wider corpus

The preflight's cross-cutting findings already flagged one Law-4 collision (nesi.md vs. LEARNED.md). This edge finds the same shape recurring at much larger scale, inside the live build's own source: at minimum ten citations, across all three files, use the older PROTOCOLS-native numbering as their working system, and only two citations in the entire build (index.html:579 and :1043) name LEARNED.md directly. session-derived The two systems are not a single sequence with two typos in it — they are two real, separately-sourced numbering schemes that happen to share the word "law," and the build's own code comments are internally consistent within whichever one they're using.
