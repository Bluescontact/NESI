When Kevin hands over a long, dense piece of writing (morning pages, a naming session, a day's orientation) and it's functioning as a **recognition exchange** — he is giving the system his felt-read and expects something back that shows it was actually received — compressing the response to a handful of sentences fails him. He said directly: "4 sentences after handing you 750 words doesn't work for me... I can't orient based on 4 sentences."

**Why:** he had explicitly asked, at some earlier point, for ~750 words of recognition in return for a drop of that size. A one-line "where" statement in the widget chassis is not that — it reads as a receipt, not a mirror. He needs the length of the reflection to be proportionate to the length of what he gave, so he can re-see his own thinking laid back out and orient from it.

**How to apply:** when a message is long-form/generative (naming session, morning pages, a day's felt-read dump) rather than a short instruction, the widget's reflective body content (the WHERE-YOU-ARE section or equivalent) needs to carry real substantive length — reflecting back the specific moves he made, the language he coined, the tensions he named — not a compressed summary line. This is distinct from action cards, which should stay terse/click-driven. The recognition-mirror and the decision-surface are two different jobs inside the same widget; don't let the terseness convention for one collapse the other. See [[feedback_widget_gate_visibility]], [[feedback_pace_to_felt_read]] — this doesn't override pacing-to-felt-read (still one ripe decision, not a catalog of asks), it's specifically about the mirror/recognition portion needing real word-count when the input was long-form.
