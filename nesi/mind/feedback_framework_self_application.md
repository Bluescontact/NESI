When iterating on framework deployments (the kit, the audit, any artifact that embodies the four-vertex model), the highest-leverage move is to *run the framework on the artifact itself*. The audit is meant to be turned on its own deployments. The four-vertex diagnostic is meant to find the missing vertex in the kit, in the library, in the working session.

This was confirmed in the cycle that produced the `kit/` directory: applying the four-vertex diagnostic to the kit-in-development surfaced four real gaps (Differentiation: front-door preamble missing; Connection: gift-logic of closing not explicit; Boundaries: pre-audit gate, tie case, what-this-isn't all missing; Architecture: editorial content embedded in conversation rather than externalized to files). Each gap mapped to a concrete repair, and the Architecture repair (externalize to separable files) was load-bearing for the next cycle.

**Why:** The framework is recursive by design. A framework that cannot operate on itself fails the Architecture vertex of its own model. Kevin already knows this; treating it as a working method rather than a clever observation is what produces real progress.

**How to apply:** When stuck, when about to ship, when deciding what to do next on a framework artifact — run the four-vertex audit on the artifact. The missing vertex names the next move. Do not skip this step in favor of pushing forward; the gap surfaced will have to be addressed eventually, and earlier costs less.
