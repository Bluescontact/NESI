## The gifts — named narrowly, because narrow is what survived

Not "this corpus is general-purpose," and not "sharper than generic
hygiene" either — that comparative framing was in an earlier draft of this
section and was cut on verification for making the same unearned-superiority
move as the claim it replaced: calling something sharper than a rival
requires the rival be run, and no control methodology was ever tested
against these essays. What's left, stated plainly, without comparison:
three specific instruments, each stated as its own concrete test, each
independently checkable against the file it lives in.

**Gift 1 — the filesystem-fact test for mechanism.**
`nesi/mind/LEARNED.md` law 22: a mechanism claim is real only if it is
something a system *cannot talk past* — a missing parameter, a hash that
must match, a file that either exists or doesn't — never an instruction a
system reads and complies with. The test it gives: can the actor simply
decline? If yes, what's being called a mechanism is a request.
STANDING: UNCHECKED.

**Gift 2 — the re-runnability discriminator.**
`nesi/game2d/tools/standing.js`: a claim's strength is not its confidence or
its rigor, it's whether a second, independent hand can re-run it and get the
same thing (DERIVED) versus a reading only its own author can reproduce
(INFERRED). The operational question it gives: who else, running this
themselves, gets the same answer?
STANDING: UNCHECKED.

**Gift 3 — the answer-rate / close-rate falsifier.**
`nesi/mind/LEARNED.md` law 10, with its own measured numbers attached: 316
offered against 1 truly answered; separately, 58 of 83 "closed" items
carried no real resolution. A named, quantified instance of the trap,
reusable as a check against any system's self-reported success rate —
including this document's own match rate against the four essays, which
was never audited against it until the ground pass did so.
STANDING: UNCHECKED.

## Correction, Kevin's mark 2026-08-20: statistics was the wrong instrument

"we dont need statitics.. everything is derived geometry." The entire
control-experiment apparatus below — hit rates, percentages, blind
specificity grading — validates by frequency. This corpus validates by
derivation. Its own precedent for what a real anchor looks like is already
on record: "the one selection-immune anchor — the dual-math derivation of
the tetrahedron (Fuller tensegrity + algebraic-topology 2-simplex, two
independent traditions)" (`nesi/mind/project_aoc_v2_rebuild.md:13`) — proven
by two independent structural derivations converging on the same necessary
shape, not by matching often against a sample.

Re-read against that standard, not the statistical one:

- **The re-runnability discriminator** (DERIVED vs. INFERRED, standing.js)
  holds. It is not a frequency claim — it's the distinction between a claim
  that follows mechanically from its own supports, re-walkable by anyone,
  and one that doesn't. Structural, not statistical.
- **The filesystem-fact test for mechanism** (law 22) holds, and on
  firmer ground than first stated: "a gate the machine cannot talk past" is
  tensegrity's own principle — integrity held by structural constraint, not
  actor compliance. Same shape as compression/tension members that simply
  cannot occupy an invalid configuration.
- **The answer-rate falsifier** (law 10, 316-vs-1, 58-of-83) does not
  survive this standard. It is a measured ratio — the same statistical kind
  of thing the control experiment below is. It stays true as a fact; it is
  not the same *kind* of gift as the other two, and is demoted out of the
  gifts list.

The control-experiment section below is kept, unedited, as the record of
what was tried and why it was the wrong tool — layered under this
correction, not erased, per this corpus's own convention.

## The control experiment (run 2026-08-20, after this file's first pass) — SUPERSEDED, kept as record

Item 3 below is no longer open. Two independent agents, blind to each
other's results, graded on an identical 3-point rubric (GENERIC / MODERATE /
SPECIFIC — "specific" meaning a directly runnable test, not just a named
principle):

- A ten-item generic methodology checklist (Popper's falsifiability, control
  groups, peer review, preregistration, operationalization, SMART criteria,
  separation of powers, stakeholder analysis, root-cause tracing, Occam's
  razor — none drawn from this corpus) was matched against the same 20 gaps.
  **16/20 hit (80%). Specificity: 5 GENERIC, 11 MODERATE, 0 SPECIFIC.**
- This corpus's actual 17 matched passages were graded on the same scale by
  a separate, blind agent. **17/20 hit (85%). Specificity: 5 GENERIC,
  6 MODERATE, 6 SPECIFIC (35%).**

**Resolution.** Hit rate is not distinctive — 80% vs. 85% is within noise
for a generously-graded "plausibly speaks to" standard, exactly as the
ground pass predicted. The strong claim ("this corpus is uniquely
general-purpose") stays held, not asserted — the control confirms it does
not survive as stated. But a narrower, now-controlled claim does survive:
**a real minority of this corpus's matches (6 of 17, spanning check_all.js's
exit-code discipline, the container taxonomy's built-in falsifier,
decisions.py's ordering-enforcement command, selector-honesty's literal
check criterion, and the DERIVED/INFERRED reproducibility test) hand over an
actual runnable mechanism, where the generic checklist's matches — even
when equally numerous — topped out at naming the right category without
ever producing one.** That is the corrected, evidenced form of what this
thread was reaching for from the start.
