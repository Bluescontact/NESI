# Extraction — Cosmos Institute (funder calibration)

Run per `.claude/skills/external-extraction/SKILL.md`'s procedure:
Inventory → Audit → Cards → Routing Note. Source is an outside
organization's own published material (their blog, their grants page,
their essay-contest results), pulled to calibrate a pitch, not to absorb
as NESI content.

---

## 1 · Inventory

**Method:** live fetch of `blog.cosmos-institute.org`, `about`,
`cosmos-institute.org/grants`, and two result posts (`AI x Truth-Seeking
Grant Winners` — first cohort; `Tocqueville and Technology: the
winners`), plus web search for the general grants-program history.

**What the source actually contains:**
- A stated identity: "The Academy for Philosopher-Builders. Building AI
  for human flourishing." 23,000+ subscribers.
- Three named funding/recognition instruments: **Cosmos Grants** (fast
  prototype funding), the **AI x Truth-Seeking Grants** (a $1M program
  run jointly with FIRE), and the **Tocqueville and Technology essay
  contest**.
- Two active grant tracks, named directly: **AI and Human Autonomy** and
  **AI for Truth-Seeking** (the FIRE partnership track). A third,
  narrower track — **AI-accelerated Scholarship**, with IHS — is
  eligibility-gated to faculty/grad students/academic researchers.
- A first-cohort winners list (27 named projects, one-line descriptions
  each) and a stated selection rationale for that cohort.
- Two winning essays from the Tocqueville contest, both quoted with
  verbatim theses on AI, coercion, and centralized control.

**Where it was blocked / thin:** the grants page itself states no fixed
pitch-length or paragraph-count requirement — applications go through an
Airtable form, format otherwise unspecified on the current page. This
does not match the "3 short paragraphs via their form" detail in the
brief that opened this pass; that detail may describe a past cycle's
form fields, the essay contest's submission format, or a paraphrase —
it is not confirmed against the current live page and is flagged rather
than carded as fact (see Audit).

survive. The org's own branding language ("AI's greatest promise is
human flourishing") is real and repeated, but it's a mission slogan, not
evidence of what they actually fund or reward — noted as seen, not
carded as a judging-criteria nutrient. Kevin's own "3 paragraphs" detail
is treated the same way: seen, not confirmed, flagged in the routing
note rather than silently absorbed as fact.

**Cut (seen, not carded):** the "philosopher-builder" branding framing;

## 3 · Cards

Rating scale: **Capacity** (how much this line can carry unsupported) ·
**Effort** (stripping/context work before it's pitch-usable) ·
**Confidence** (how sure the source's own claim is).

### Judging shape

**J1 — The actual selection criteria, stated verbatim**
> "From hundreds of applications, this first cohort was selected for
> clarity of purpose, technical feasibility, and a commitment to
> truth-seeking."
Capacity: high · Effort: low · Confidence: high (direct quote, first-cohort
announcement post)
*Routing note: this is the load-bearing fact for pitch-shaping — three
named criteria, not "the idea over the CV" as a vague claim. A pitch
should hit all three explicitly: one clear purpose sentence, one
technical-feasibility sentence (what ships as a working prototype in 90
days), one truth-seeking-or-autonomy sentence.*

**J2 — The two live tracks, named**
> AI for Truth-Seeking: "Technology that supports the open contestation
> of ideas and human judgment in knowledge production." AI and Human
> Autonomy: "reducing excessive deference to AI systems, and systems
> that support our capacity for self-formation."
Capacity: high · Effort: low · Confidence: high (grants page, current)
*Routing note: the Human Autonomy track's own language —
"self-formation," "reducing excessive deference" — is close to a direct
match for the unilateral-closure discipline
(`nesi/_overnight_build/intake_copy/undefended_asker_completion_surface.md`):
a way of closing a gift/ask exchange that does not require external
(platform, AI, or third-party) certification that the exchange
completed. That's a structural instance of "reducing deference" to a
mediating system, not a metaphor stretched to fit.*

**J3 — Program mechanics, current**
> $1,000–$10,000 fast grants (larger considered for exceptional
> proposals), 90-day prototype window, applications via Airtable form,
> no stated pitch-length requirement.
Capacity: medium · Effort: low · Confidence: high (grants page, current,
directly fetched)
*Routing note: contradicts/does not confirm the brief's "3 short
paragraphs" detail — see Routing Note below. Whatever pitch gets
written should be built to the actual form fields, checked live before
submission, not assumed from memory of an older cycle.*

### Precedent — what actually got funded

**P1 — Protocol-level precedent, not tool-level**
> Index Network: "A discovery protocol for surfacing competing
> perspectives." Remarker: "An open-source Wikipedia for exploring
> truth-seeking in LLMs."
Capacity: high · Effort: medium · Confidence: high (first-cohort winners
list, verbatim one-line descriptions)
*Routing note: most of the 27 first-cohort winners are single-purpose
tools (a benchmark, a browser extension, a writing aid). These two are
the exceptions — infrastructure/protocol-shaped, closer in kind to a
relational-contract instrument like PFRC (`nesi/spec/REMNANTS_FOR_THE_GAME_2026-07-31.md`,
"pre-funded relational contract — the invite's paper form") than to a
single-user app. Worth naming PFRC explicitly as a protocol, not a tool,
if it goes in front of these judges — the precedent for funding
structural/protocol proposals over app-shaped ones is real and citable.*

**P2 — Essay-contest winning arguments name coercion and decentralization
directly**
> "Popular media and culture in authoritarian regimes are attempts to
> discipline society into compliance, something that is no longer
> necessary when every citizen can be offered their own customized cult
> leader." (Pang) / "It transforms that fear when it sustains plural
> associations in which people become capable of self-rule." (Snow)
Capacity: high · Effort: low · Confidence: high (Tocqueville contest
winners post, verbatim)
*Routing note: both winning theses argue from the same shape Kevin
flagged — AI's danger is individualized, decentralized coercion (a
"customized cult leader" per citizen, no mass discipline needed); its
promise is sustaining "plural associations" capable of self-rule. The
gift/mutual/exchange doctrine (`nesi/spec/REMNANTS_FOR_THE_GAME_2026-07-31.md`
DOCTRINE §13-15: "receiving never converts to a prompt to give") and the
unilateral-closure pattern are both structural refusals of exactly the
capture-through-dependency mechanism Pang's essay names. This is the
strongest direct philosophical match found in this pass — not a loose
thematic echo but the same specific failure mode (coercion that doesn't
need to announce itself) named on both sides independently.*

**P3 — Mission taxonomy, three named pillars**
> Autonomy, decentralization, truth-seeking — named as the institute's
> three backed areas, distinct from the flourishing/philosopher-builder
> branding language.
Capacity: medium · Effort: low · Confidence: medium (secondary source —
web summary of about page, not independently re-verified line by line)
*Routing note: if a pitch is built to name-check this taxonomy directly,
verify the exact current phrasing on `blog.cosmos-institute.org/about`
before submission — this card rests on a fetched summary, not a
re-read of the raw page HTML.*

## 4 · Routing Note

Three cards ready to shape a pitch now (J1, J2, P2); one names a real
precedent worth citing explicitly (P1); one needs a direct re-check
before use (P3); one flags a discrepancy that should be resolved before
anything is submitted (J3).

**What's ready to route into pitch language now:** J1 (write to the
three named criteria explicitly), J2 (the Human Autonomy track's own
"self-formation" / "reducing excessive deference" language is close
enough to unilateral closure to cite near-verbatim, not translate), P2
(the coercion/decentralization argument shape matches independently,
strongest single finding in this pass).

**What needs one more check before the pitch is finalized:** J3 — the
"3 short paragraphs" format in the opening brief does not match what
the current live grants page states (Airtable form, no stated pitch
length). Confirm the actual current form fields directly at submission
time rather than pitching to a remembered format that may be stale or
may belong to a different program (the essay contest, not Cosmos
Grants). P3 similarly wants a direct re-read of the about page before
any exact phrase from it is quoted in a pitch.

**Not routed, held as open question:** whether PFRC or the
unilateral-closure pattern is the stronger lead for a pitch — P1 argues
PFRC's protocol shape is the better precedent-match; P2 argues the
closure doctrine's coercion-refusal is the sharper philosophical match.
Both are real and not mutually exclusive within one ~2,000-word essay or
one grant pitch, but which one leads is a framing choice, not something
this pass resolves. That's Kevin's call.
