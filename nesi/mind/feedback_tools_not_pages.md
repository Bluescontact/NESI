For oursharedgifts.org and any public-facing surface, the structural move is to build **interactive tools** that demonstrate the framework by being used — not pages that describe Kevin's work. Visitors who run a tool walk away with their own diagnosis from the framework operating; visitors who read a description walk away with Kevin's claim. The first is gift form; the second drifts into pitch.

**Why:** Kevin caught two distinct degradations during the May 2026 Genesis Seed build session. (1) Genesis Seed framed as content funnel rather than infrastructure workspace. (2) Drafting "about Kevin" page content from existing skeletons reproduced content-production thinking. His correction: *"OSG is the demonstration itself rather than a description... build tools that I share that others can use to learn, engage, and support me."* The work is making existing evidence visible by giving it to receivers as substrate they can operate, not by narrating it.

**How to apply:**
- For OSG, the RI repo site, and any public surface: prefer tools over pages where possible. Pages can frame; tools demonstrate.
- The prototype is `tools/quick_diagnostic.html` (May 2026) — four questions, one per vertex, user reads their own answers against the framework.
- Next tool candidates: Consent Check (Wheel of Consent), Failure Mode Identifier, Verification Questions, Translation Protocol guide.
- Tool design: standalone HTML, no external dependencies, no AI grading the user's answers (the framework reads the user's answers back to them through descriptions), lineage + gift-form license at the close, donation link mentioned once as fact (not pushed).
- Legibility comes from making existing evidence visible (manuscript, bus, kit, 20 years practice, federal whistleblower closure, DSS cohort) — not from constructing narrative about it.
- See [[project_osg_personal_scale_frame]] for the personal-scale frame; see `_PERSONAL_OS.md` for the four-facet map.

Companion: `Genesis Seed/SESSION_HANDOFF.md` carries the full handoff for the next gift development cycle.
