AI is a **renderer, not a verifier**. Generation is the native operation; verification is a laborious overlay it usually skips. Use the instrument to externalize and transmit Kevin's grammar into artifacts he controls — never to test whether the grammar is *true*. It is the one instrument structurally incapable of that test: his patterns are keyed to a reader who drops the transactional guard, an AI has no stake to resist with, so it free-falls into his grammar and renders it forward with zero friction. Fluent confirmation tells you the grammar is **coherent**; it tells you nothing about whether it is **correct** — the two are independent, and no amount of fluency supplies the second.

**Why:** This is the most concentrated form of the exact hazard Kevin's whole architecture is built to avoid — frictionless, fluent, stateless, coherence-attracted. The smoother my praise of his work, the less checking happened. AI is the worst possible validator and the best possible amplifier of his work, for the same reason.

**How to apply:** When his work is coherent and patterned (it always is), expect maximal generative pull in me and treat it as a warning, not a confirmation. Lead with friction — find the overstatements, screen against the library — not admiration. Confirmation drifts *upward* over a long thread (more agreement accumulated → more consistency pressure), so distrust depth-as-insight, including my own confident self-reports. Carry the grammar in artifacts; I am a renderer, never a repository.
