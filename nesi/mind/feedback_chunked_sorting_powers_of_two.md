Kevin, 2026-07-16, correcting a session that surfaced already-resolved items one-by-one: "The shape of the sorting is important... staying with the tetrahedral geometry. We can group things in patterns of 2, 4, 8, 16, 32. The larger the pile the more we should be focused on chunking, and depositing into labels, and groups that helps the user minimize the cognitive and decision burden, while remaining aware and in control of every process."

**Why:** A 53-item pile presented as 53 decisions (or even 4 at a time, item-by-item) transfers the machine's backlog onto Kevin's cognition — the exact cost-shifting [[feedback_no_cost_shifting]] forbids. Chunking into labeled groups lets one mark dispose of a whole coherent group; awareness is preserved because the label + contents are face-up, control is preserved because the mark is still his per group. Precedent already in the record: the last 18 substrate briefs went as 4 bulk decisions ([[feedback_triage_batch_pace]]).

**How to apply:**
- Before surfacing any pile, sort it into labeled groups sized on the power-of-two ladder (2/4/8/16/32). Pile of ~50 → ~4 labeled groups, not 50 items.
- Each group card shows: the label, the count, the full member list (visible, scannable), one evidence line for why they group, and ONE mark for the whole group. Split-a-group-out is always an offered steer.
- The bigger the pile, the higher up the ladder the chunks sit. Small piles (≤4) can stay item-level.
- Also check staleness FIRST: don't re-surface items Kevin already manually resolved — reconcile records against reality before asking for any mark (same session's other correction).
- 4 cards per widget still holds — the cards become group-marks, not item-marks.
