## §12 · The two-map split — stable refusal, fast availability (E5 resolved)

The instrument is two maps, not one. They change on different clocks and never touch.

**The boundary layer (stable).** Standing edges — what I do not offer; what one `yes`
never implies; what kinds of contact cost me; where no negotiation occurs; what receiving
from me never obligates; how stopping affects the relationship. It changes **slowly, and
never in response to a live request** (a revision made while an ask is open is
indistinguishable from negotiation, §6.4).

**The availability layer (fast).** What is easy *today* — quantity or duration, place and
time, relevant limits, when the offer expires, and whether it is a gift, a mutual practice,
or an exchange (§13). It may change hourly.

The split's whole work: it **separates the material cost of helping from the relational
cost of becoming available.** A spare meal costs almost nothing materially; offering it
informally has historically carried the relational overhang ("does one meal make me a meal
source?"). Publishing the perimeter once, on the stable layer, lets the fast layer post a
bounded surplus without re-publishing general access to the self.

**E5 (revision vs. fixity) — resolved.** Fixity lives in the boundary layer; mutability
lives in the availability layer. Because they never touch, the availability layer can change
constantly without the refusal ever looking negotiable.
**Falsifier:** if a change to the availability layer ever reads as softening the refusal,
the two layers are not actually separate and the split has failed.

## §13 · Three instruments, never blended — gift · mutual practice · exchange

The architecture is trustworthy only when it holds all three without misnaming them. Every
offer on the availability layer **must self-tag** which it is (mandatory, never defaulted).

- **Gift** — nothing is owed. The receive path completes and stops (§14).
- **Mutual practice** — both parties consent to a bounded recurring structure, its edges
  published: either may leave before the next session; missed sessions do not accrue; there
  is no carried balance; one person's greater need does not silently expand the task; the
  agreement grants no access outside its stated scope. This is not a failed gift — it is a
  different instrument, *bounded mutuality*. The held-refusal layer makes it possible
  without months of interpersonal testing first.
- **Exchange** — one contribution is conditional on another. It can be clean and humane,
  but it is **not generosity**. The refusal map keeps the exchange from spilling into the
  rest of the relationship; it does not convert conditionality into a gift.

**Falsifier:** if an untagged offer defaults to "gift," exchange quietly borrows gift's
moral cover. The tag must be mandatory, not defaulted.

## §14 · The field design law — receiving never converts into a prompt to give

§6.2's leaf law, generalized to the whole commons. After someone accepts, the field must
**never** say: *offer something in return · pay it forward · "Kevin has given three times"
· complete your contribution profile · people who receive also help with… · would you like
to thank the giver?* Even a well-meaning **return button** reopens the ledger.

It also cannot rank people, count generosity, display reciprocal percentages, or reward
"active contributors." The moment the field records social standing through gifts, low-cost
surplus becomes reputation currency.

The receiving path must complete without delivering the receiver to an ask — exactly as the
leaf law requires. The person can **independently** enter the offering side later; nothing
delivers them there.

**Build hook:** extend `leaf_audit.py`'s ask-set to cover the receive path (banned
affordances: return button, pay-it-forward nudge, generosity count, reciprocal %,
contributor rank).
**Falsifier:** if any surface counts or ranks giving "just for visibility," it has already
made surplus into standing — visibility is the leak.

## §15 · Reciprocity as rhythm, not balance — the loop opens only on two independent marks

A loop must **never be inferred** from two people having helped each other; inference would
retroactively reprice prior gifts as deposits. A loop requires a separate mark.

1. Each independently publishes surplus.
2. Each receives from the other independently.
3. Nothing changes automatically.
4. Either can separately mark: *open to a recurring mutual practice.*
5. Only when **both** marks exist does a doorway appear.
6. They then set scope, term, exit, and the non-accrual rule.

The earlier gifts remain gifts — never counted toward the new agreement. This is what
informal calibration rarely does well: let mutuality become explicit without rewriting prior
generosity as negotiation.

**Falsifier:** if the doorway ever appears from a one-sided mark or from usage history, the
loop was inferred, not consented — and prior gifts got repriced.

## §16 · The living membrane + minimal disclosure — HELD (awaiting mark)

*Caught 2026-07-25, still in development. Not yet a marked crossing — parked here so it is not lost.*

The map is not a permanent declaration of who a person is. It is a **living membrane**:

- these are my standing edges;
- this is what moves easily through me today;
- these are burdens I am willing to let others carry;
- nothing received or given changes anyone's standing.

**The minimal-disclosure limit (the new guard).** A person must not have to make their whole
interior public to participate. You do not owe the world a complete account of your
capacities, costs, or refusals. The interface needs only enough information to make the
*immediate crossing* clean. Otherwise the boundary map becomes a new demand — *explain
yourself perfectly before you are allowed to belong* — which is the same extraction in
inverted form. The held refusal **protects the aperture; it does not close it.** Because the
`no` need not be defended, the `yes` can appear more often; because the `yes` creates no
debt, one can receive without preparing repayment.

What it enables: it removes `yes`/`no` from the social performance of the moment. A `no`
becomes information about the structure, not a judgment of the asker. A `yes` becomes
permission for this opening, not general access. Receiving becomes inflow, not debt.
Offering becomes movement of surplus, not enrollment in a role. Burden flows toward where it
is light. The question shrinks — not *"Will you care for me?"* but *"Is this particular
burden easy for you to carry?"*; not *"How do I repay?"* but *"What moves easily through me
now?"* Interdependence without surrendering differentiation first.

**Falsifier:** if participating requires publishing more of the interior than the immediate
crossing needs, the membrane has become a demand and the aperture has closed.

## §9 · Edge-ledger — updated

- **E2 · authorship asymmetry — RESOLVED.** The low-capacity form of the boundary is
  **negative space plus a marking instrument**, not a boundary published on the guest's
  behalf. The leaf law authors the *shape* (a property of the offer: "nothing here asks of
  you"); the space starts empty; the guest fills it at their own capacity, using the same
  mark grammar turned toward them. A guest who marks nothing has an **unfilled, respected
  space** — not an absent boundary. The *ventriloquized no* (giver publishing the guest's
  refusal as if guest-authored) is retired as designed-out: the builder may guarantee a
  property of the offer, never make a claim about the person.
- **E5 · revision vs. fixity — RESOLVED** by §12 (the two-map split).
- **E1 · fixed price — resolved** (convertibility, §2). **E3 · foreclosure of surprise —
  resolved** (occasion-not-purchase). **E4 · click as readiness — dissolved** (§6.3
