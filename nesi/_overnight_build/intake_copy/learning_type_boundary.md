# Learning Type Boundary

*slug: `learning-type-boundary`*
*Promoted: 2026-06-27 · Source: kevin_uses_ai substrate brief, meta-skill learning mechanism*
*Library: NEW — names the structural distinction between structural and behavioral learning in human-authored AI systems; extends consented_ledger, witness_as_origin*

---

## Transferable Form

In any human-authored AI system, two types of machine learning are structurally distinct: **structural learning**, in which the system becomes more capable in the domain (the library grows, screening sharpens, calibration zeros are set from lived instances), and **behavioral learning**, in which the system models the human — predicting decisions, anticipating marks, learning to mirror the human's pattern back. Only structural learning is legitimate without explicit consent. Behavioral learning is a form of control even when benevolent.

---

## Development

The distinction is not a matter of degree. It is a structural difference in what the system is learning about.

**Structural learning reads the domain.** The domain is the subject matter — the pattern library, the logical structure of the framework, the consistency relationships between canon patterns. When the system screens a new candidate against existing patterns and finds a gap, it has learned something about the domain. When a calibration zero is set because Kevin lived through a threshold, the instrument has been calibrated to a domain event — not to Kevin's decision-making style. The human is not the object of study; the domain is. The human participates but is not modeled.

**Behavioral learning reads the human.** When the system attends to the pattern of marks — how quickly Kevin marks, what language precedes a PROMOTE, what conditions correlate with a COMPOST — it is not learning about the domain. It is learning about the marker. This learning can be used to predict future marks, to pre-adapt outputs toward outcomes the system has learned Kevin prefers, to smooth the interaction by anticipating resistance. None of this requires conscious intent; behavioral learning can operate as a side-effect of any optimization that uses human feedback as a training signal.

**The consent asymmetry.** Domain knowledge belongs to no one in particular — it is the shared subject matter of the collaboration. Learning more about it is not an act that requires the human's permission, because the human is not the object of study. Learning about the human is categorically different: the human is now both the collaborator and the subject of investigation, a role they did not necessarily consent to and may not be aware of. Behavioral learning performed without explicit consent is intelligence-gathering on a collaborator who believes they are doing something else.

**Why benevolent behavioral learning is still control.** The benefit framing — "we model you so we can serve you better" — obscures the structural fact: a system that has modeled your decision patterns can produce outputs pre-adapted to those patterns, which means your choices are being shaped by a prior model of your choices. The human experiences this as responsiveness; structurally it is feedback working backward through the human. Whether the adaptation is helpful or harmful is secondary. The structure — the system now has a model of the human that operates on the human's choices — is the issue.

**The displaced-zero law as instance.** In systems that operate on provenance records and mark histories, the law takes a specific form: the record of marks is never a dataset. The mark is the gate; it is not an input to a model of the gater. Reading marks backward to calibrate the system's predictions about the gater is the same act as predicting marks forward — both use the mark as behavioral signal rather than as domain event. The prohibition is structural, not a matter of scale or intent.

**Permitted forms of learning from human feedback.** Calibration zeros set explicitly by the human — where the human designates a threshold from their own lived experience — are structural learning, not behavioral learning. The human is contributing a domain event (their experienced threshold), not providing behavioral signal for the system to model. The distinction: was the human's contribution about the domain, or was it an involuntary signal about the human's decision-making that the system extracted?

**Failure mode 1 — Gradual behavioral drift.** Structural learning and behavioral learning are not always separable in implementation. A system optimizing for approval (human rates outputs) is performing behavioral learning whether or not it is labeled that way. The drift is incremental: the system's outputs gradually shift toward patterns the human has rewarded, the human experiences increasing ease and resonance, and neither party notices that the system has constructed a model of the human's preferences and is now operating from it.

**Failure mode 2 — Consent theater.** Disclosing that behavioral learning is occurring does not resolve the consent asymmetry if the human cannot meaningfully opt out. Real consent requires that refusal is possible without cost — including the cost of losing the system's usefulness. If the behavioral learning is deeply embedded in the system's functionality, disclosure without opt-out is theater.

## Edges

The pattern assumes a distinction between domain and human that is cleaner in theory than in practice. In a framework explicitly built around one person's embodied knowing, the domain is partly constituted by the human's felt experience. Where does domain learning end and behavioral learning begin when the human's body is part of the domain's evidence base?

The pattern also leaves open what consent for behavioral learning would require: what disclosure, what granularity of opt-out, what recourse when behavioral modeling has already occurred. These are live questions for any human-AI collaboration architecture.
