## Transferable Form

Recognition infrastructure at the public layer almost always degrades into case studies, narratives, and curated examples — artifacts that perform the canon rather than enact it. The monument to utility grows while the habitat for recognition capacity starves. This failure is structurally inevitable given institutional pressure toward shareable, visible proof; it is not a mistake but a gravity.

---

## Development

The failure has a predictable shape:

Public-facing recognition work produces two kinds of output. The first is inhabitable — a tool, instrument, or interaction that creates conditions for recognition to occur in the user. The second is displayable — a case study, article, or narrative showing how recognition occurred in someone else. Displayable output has properties the inhabitable does not: it is shareable, quotable, comprehensible without use, and produces measurable comprehension.

Institutional and platform pressure uniformly selects for the displayable. It can be linked. It generates views. It signals competence. It answers "what does this do?" with a story rather than an experience. Over time, the public layer of recognition infrastructure fills with the displayable, which crowds out the inhabitable.

The monument is what results: a well-documented, beautifully narrated map of recognition patterns that does not produce recognition capacity in readers. The user leaves understanding how recognition works — which is not recognition. The canon becomes the subject, not the substrate.

Three markers of monument formation:
1. Success is measured by comprehension, not by capacity (new behavior possible)
2. Users can engage fully without ever performing the recognition act
3. The canon's patterns appear as named content rather than structural substrate

---

## Falsifier

If a case study demonstrably produces recognition capacity — not comprehension of recognition, but new behavior possible: the reader now notices what they previously could not notice — in readers who have not used an inhabitable instrument, the pattern is weakened.

The evidence across domains strongly suggests case studies produce comprehension, not capacity. Reading about bilateral route formation does not produce the ability to route bilateral under pressure. The pattern holds until a counter-instance is demonstrated.

---

## Edges

**The proof-of-value trap.** Recognition capacity is invisible and hard to quantify. Case studies offer tangible, attributable proof of value. Every organization building recognition infrastructure faces structural pressure to produce proof, and proof is always displayable. The monument is not a failure of intention — it is a gravity.

**The canon-as-subject inversion.** When the canon becomes the subject of the narrative (this case study illustrates witness_as_origin), the canon has been extracted from its substrate function and displayed as content. `render_without_canonizing` violated at the publishing layer.

**The comprehension-as-proxy failure.** Users who comprehend the map believe they have acquired the territory. The monument produces confident misidentification — users who can accurately describe recognition infrastructure but cannot recognize. This is worse than ignorance because it forecloses the search.
