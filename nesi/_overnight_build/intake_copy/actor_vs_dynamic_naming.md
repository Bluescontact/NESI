## Transferable form

A name is not a label but a pointing device: actor-pointing names aim at an entity inside the system, dynamic-pointing names aim at a condition outside any single entity. The pointing direction is set once, at founding, before the organ has a history — and at transmission time, when rules and context do not travel, the name's vector is the only thing that does. Judgment-drift is not misuse; it is an actor-name's original vector completing its trajectory.

## Mechanism

A name encodes a direction of attention. Actor-pointing names ("The Keeper," "The Coordinator") direct the system to read an entity — and eventually to evaluate whether that entity is performing correctly. That evaluation machinery is judgment; the drift is structural, not cultural. Dynamic-pointing names ("The Crossing," "The Metabolism") direct the system to read a condition; no entity is inside the frame to be evaluated. The difference is not grammatical (gerunds can be applied to actors) — it is whether the name is legible as a read of the form or as an assignment to an agent.

Transmission-time is the load-bearing moment. At design time, the author's intent, the rules, and the surrounding context can compensate for a name that points badly. At transmission time — when someone inherits the organ without the author, without the founding context — the name is the only carrier. A badly pointed name does not produce misuse; it produces a structurally coherent system organized around the wrong thing.

## Failure modes

1. **Dynamic name, actor logic** — an organ renamed to a gerund while its operational rules still assign specific agents to specific functions. The name travels clean; the actor-structure arrives hidden inside it. Naming alone cannot catch this because the mechanism is in the underlying form, not the word.
2. **Founding-window closes fast** — the pointing direction is set at founding and becomes load-bearing infrastructure fast. By the time drift is visible in the organ's behavior, renaming costs more than tolerating — the pattern is therefore only fully available once, in the founding moment, before the organ has a history. After that, it becomes a drift-diagnosis instrument with expensive correction.

## The claim, split (re-screen resolution, already made in-text 2026-07-15)

The pattern does **not** establish that non-extractability is a naming property. Structural truth: naming affects legibility and drift speed. It does not follow that non-extractability is a naming property — even an extractive actor cannot extract because the form does not permit it; that protection is topological, not lexical.

The pattern shrinks appropriately to: **actor-pointing names accelerate judgment-drift at transmission; dynamic-pointing names slow it.** Whether the underlying form is extractable is a different question, answered by the form's topology — a design discipline about drift speed, not a structural invariant about extractability.

## Edges

- If the form's topology is doing the real structural work — no handle for extraction regardless of what the organ is called — then this pattern governs a secondary effect (legibility, drift rate) rather than a primary structural property. It may be a design discipline rather than a structural invariant, and those require different homes in a pattern library.
- The falsifier shape is named but unbuilt: an actor-rename to dynamic should produce measurable reduction in drift-toward-judgment in external readers or adopters, with no change in rules and no change in who operates the organ. Without that shape tested, the pattern remains a design prescription.
- Where does a founding-moment discipline live in a system that develops iteratively? If the intervention is only available before history accumulates, the pattern needs an institution — a founding protocol, a design gate — not just a concept. What carries it into the room at the right moment is unnamed.
