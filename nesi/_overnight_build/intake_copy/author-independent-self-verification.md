## Transferable form

A grammar that encodes the generative path — not the outcome — allows any trained operator to reconstruct the identical form without the author's presence, reputation, or context. Trust transfers from person to procedure to the degree that the specification eliminates every degree of freedom in the output space, leaving nothing for the operator to interpret. Where interpretation remains, the grammar is not the trust infrastructure; the community running it is — and the author has not actually been made dispensable, only concealed.

## Mechanism

A generative grammar collapses a decision tree into compact notation. An operator who shares the expansion algorithm re-expands it identically. The trust property is not in the notation string but in the shared expansion algorithm — which means the grammar is never the sole load-bearer; the trained population that can run it is the hidden second component. Remove the school of heralds and the herald's dispensability vanishes.

**Specification completeness is the threshold condition.** Trust without authorial presence scales proportionally to how much of the output space the grammar constrains. Full constraint yields a single recoverable form — cold verification. Partial constraint yields a family of outputs; the gap is filled by the operator's judgment, which reintroduces authority at the point of interpretation.

Two trust mechanisms run in parallel: formal specification handles the determinate cases; community convention handles the interpretive remainder. Convention is distributed authorship — the backup author the pattern doesn't name.

**Cold verification** is the pattern's upper bound and hardest test: a stranger, fifty years later, no context, no community, no reputation — only the grammar and the object it should generate. A grammar that self-verifies across context-collapse must survive the death of shared language, the loss of community memory, and the drift of convention.

Dispensability is domain-scoped. The grammar makes the author dispensable for reproduction and verification inside the formal domain. It does not make the author dispensable for authorization, assignment, or origin — those boundary decisions require a living authority.

## Falsifier

Give the spec to two operators who have never seen the target, working independently. If both reconstruct the identical form — same output, no discretionary choice available anywhere in the process — it's a generative grammar (cold trust). If the two outputs differ in any dimension the spec was silent on, it was a rich description: the "identical form" was actually the author's tolerance range, filled in by each operator's judgment. The test is symmetric-operator divergence, not fidelity to the author's intent.

## Open edges

- Whether specification completeness and cold-verifiability are the same condition or two distinct thresholds.
- What it costs to achieve completeness in a given domain — whether the cost of eliminating every interpretive degree of freedom is ever paid in practice, or whether all real grammars are partial specifications sustained by living communities.
- The pattern names dispensability of the author but not transferability of the authority.
