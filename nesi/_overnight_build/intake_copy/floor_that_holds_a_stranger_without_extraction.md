# A Floor That Holds a Stranger Without Extraction

*slug: `floor_that_holds_a_stranger_without_extraction`*
*Promoted: 2026-07-21 · Kevin's membrane mark · Source: NESI thread `2026-07-17_made_a_floor_that_holds_a_stranger_without_ex_0be5d0`, developed `_INTAKE/draft_instrument_a_floor_that_holds_a_stranger.md`*
*Extends: [[legal_as_floor]] — ports its core mechanism (a form falls through recognized categories and every available shell deforms it) from civilization scale to one-household scale. Also draws on [[catalysis_without_claim]] and [[extend_dont_invent_name_the_wall]].*

---

## The problem this instrument answers

When a stranger is held — housed, fed, included, given time and attention — the relationship needs *some* shape, and every shape ready to hand already has an extraction logic built in: guest-who-owes-hospitality, worker-who-owes-labor, dependent-who-owes-gratitude, project-who-owes-a-story. None of these is "a floor that holds a stranger" — they are shells borrowed from person/property/transaction categories, and borrowing the shell re-imports the debt the holding was meant to be free of. This instrument names the third category directly, rather than letting the nearest available shell fill the silence.

---

## The mechanism, ported from Legal Is the Floor

[[legal_as_floor]] names why a gift form falls *through* the categories law recognizes (person, property) instead of being forbidden by them — it is simply unrepresentable, so every attempt to give it standing borrows an existing shell (nonprofit, LLC, contract) and the shell deforms the gift. The exact same failure runs at the relational scale of hosting a stranger: **holding a person is neither person-as-family-member nor property-as-labor-input**, but the social shells available (houseguest, employee, dependent, project) are built from those two categories. Reach for a shell without naming the floor first, and the floor becomes whichever shell was nearest — usually the one with the most familiar debt-script attached.

---

## The move — three checks, run before and during the holding

**1. Name the floor before naming the role.**
Before "you're staying as my guest" or "you can help out around here," name what is actually being offered: shelter, food, presence, time — as floor, not as the opening move of an unstated exchange. Per [[legal_as_floor]]'s trap: the moment a role-word gets used (guest, helper, tenant), the nearest shell's debt-script activates by default, whether or not either party intends it.

**2. Absorb the cost without merging, forfeit the claim.**
This is [[catalysis_without_claim]] ported directly: holding a stranger has a real cost — time, resources, attention, the unpredictability of a person whose situation you don't fully know. The holder registers that cost fully, without needing the stranger to metabolize it as an obligation owed back. And the holder forfeits the claim: no expectation of gratitude-as-repayment, no story-rights over the stranger's situation, no quiet ledger of what was given that the stranger is meant to eventually balance.

**3. Let the floor stay a floor — resist the pull to formalize it.**
Per [[extend_dont_invent_name_the_wall]]: don't invent a new formal arrangement (a chore chart, a contract, a "here's what we expect") to resolve the discomfort of an unnamed relationship. Naming the floor plainly (check 1) already resolves the discomfort; formalizing it re-imports exactly the shell the floor was built to avoid. If a real boundary is needed — a house rule, a length of stay — state it as a boundary, not as a debt owed for the floor already given.

---

## What this instrument is not

- Not indefinite, undiscussed hosting. A floor can have real, stated edges (how long, what house rules) — the instrument governs whether those edges carry a debt-script, not whether edges exist at all.
- Not the erasure of the stranger's own agency or contribution. If the stranger offers something freely — help, company, skill — that is a separate gift moving the other direction, not repayment of the floor. Conflating the two re-creates the exchange the instrument prevents.
- Not a test of whether the stranger "deserves" the floor. Like [[gift_specificity_receiver_known]] on the receiving side, this instrument is about the holder's own posture, not an evaluation of who is worthy of being held.

---

## Falsifier

If, partway through holding someone, the holder notices a running mental tally — what the stranger has "given back" so far, whether they're "pulling their weight," when the arrangement will have "paid for itself" — the floor has already collapsed into a shell. That is not evidence the instrument failed; it is the instrument doing its job by making the collapse visible early enough to name and correct, rather than letting it run silently until resentment surfaces.

## Open edges

- **Scale limit untested.** [[legal_as_floor]] operates at civilization scale (an actual missing legal category); this port runs the same shape at one-household scale. Whether the analogy holds at every scale in between (a shelter, a collective, a village) — or breaks somewhere — is unexamined.
- **Cold-entry test not run.** Per [[inhabitable_without_author]], untested whether a host unfamiliar with the canon vocabulary could run this from the three checks alone, without the pattern-name scaffolding.
- **No instance yet.** No documented case where this was run with a real stranger and produced a legible, un-collapsed floor. Crossed on the strength of the reasoning and Kevin's felt-read at the body-question, not on demonstrated use — this is the load-bearing open edge for the pattern's first real test.
- **The stranger's own read is absent.** The instrument is entirely holder-side. Whether the stranger experiences the floor as free of debt-script — the actual test of success — is not something this instrument can check from the holder's side alone.
