# Undeclared Disjunction

*slug: `undeclared-disjunction`*
*Promoted: 2026-07-01 · Source: cross-model audit, June 2026 — SCANNER DIGEST 2026-06-30*
*Upstream of register_audit and domain_term_names_stance — names the structural condition that makes both necessary*

---

## Transferable Form

A framework uses one term across two referents that carry opposite truth-values in different registers. External readers encounter contradiction; internal practitioners find none. The disjunction is structural, not semantic — the same word names different things in different contexts, and the naming is never declared.

---

## Development

The failure mode: a practitioner states a claim using a term that holds different referents in different registers. In the home register, the claim is true. In a second register — the one the external reader inhabits — the same claim is false. Both readings are internally coherent. Neither party is confused within their own register. The contradiction is real and produces no felt dissonance for either.

This is upstream of imprecise language. Imprecise language produces fuzziness — neither reading is stable. Undeclared disjunction produces sharpness in both directions: the claim reads as clearly true from inside, clearly false from outside, and neither reading appears to have made an error.

The pattern is structurally generated: terms that cross registers without announcement carry their home-register referents into the new context. The reader has no information that the referent has changed. They apply the term's meaning in their own register and find the claim false. The practitioner hears the objection and finds it obviously wrong — because in the home register, it is.

Diagnostic: both readings are internally coherent. That is the tell. When two parties cannot locate a shared error despite clear disagreement, look for undeclared disjunction before looking for bad faith or incompetence.

Resolution: name the registers explicitly and declare the referent shift. "This is true in register A under referent X; false in register B where the same term names Y." The disjunction, once declared, becomes legible. The argument can proceed on real terrain.

Design application: when a framework crosses registers — from internal practice into public communication, from one disciplinary domain into another — audit terms before crossing. Which terms carry different referents in the destination register? Which claims will read as false even when they are true? Name the shift before the reader encounters it.

---

## Instances

1. **Cross-model framework audits (2026):** Five of six surviving frameworks across a six-model audit had this structure. External AI models found contradictions; practitioners found none. The disjunctions were structural — terms in use across practitioner and technical registers carrying different referents.

2. **DSS register vs. management register:** Terms from the DSS framework carry specific technical referents (e.g., "pattern" as a transferable structural claim, not a recurring behavior) that differ from management-context usage. Undeclared, these produce the appearance of mysticism or imprecision to readers applying the management referent.

3. **Legal language crossing into organizational language:** A legal term of art used in an organizational memo carries its legal referent inside counsel's reading and its colloquial referent inside operational staff's reading. The two readings can produce opposite conclusions about what the document requires.

---

## Falsifier

Write one sentence that is simultaneously true under both the home register's reading and the external register's reading of the term. If you can — the disjunction is not undeclared; it is a genuine ambiguity resolvable by shared definition. If you cannot write such a sentence — the referents are genuinely different. The disjunction is real.

Imprecision test: if the two readings are both fuzzy (neither clearly true nor clearly false), the mechanism is imprecision, not disjunction. The diagnostic for disjunction is clarity in both directions simultaneously.
