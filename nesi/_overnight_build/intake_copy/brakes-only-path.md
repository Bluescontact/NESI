## Transferable form

A brake you comply with voluntarily is advisory: the graph still has two edges out of every node, the one through the brake and the one around it. The load-bearing move is not to strengthen the brake but to delete the second edge — to remove the bypass so the brake becomes the only remaining path. The felt result is settling rather than restriction, because the standing attention you spent holding yourself off the around-route is released — but that same settling arrives whether the path is truly gone or merely un-taken, which is exactly where the pattern can lie to itself.

## Mechanism

The unit of operation is topology, not volition. Any node has two edges out: through-the-brake and around-the-brake. Applying more force to intention leaves both edges intact — compliance stays voluntary because a route around still exists. The move deletes the around-edge. Force applied to will is unchanged; what changed is that force has nowhere else to go. This is why settling costs zero added effort: settling is what a system does when its degrees of freedom drop, not when its resolve rises.

**The bypass is the object.** The brake and the floor are inherited unchanged from the advisory era — neither is operated on. The only thing touched is the bypass. A real instance must point at a specific pre-existing route around the brake and show it is now gone. Name the bypass or there was no move.

**Deletion must cost more than compliance.** A path is removed iff re-opening it costs more than obeying the brake. Removal is a cost-inversion: bypass-reconstruction-cost pushed above compliance-cost. Any floor where the operator can silently re-widen the road at zero cost has not been removed, only renamed — it has collapsed back to advisory.

**Settling as freed vigilance.** When the bypass exists, the operator spends continuous attention holding off it; removing the path releases that standing guard. This gives a body-side falsifier: genuine path-removal feels like relief-of-watching; a merely-renewed intention feels like resolve or tension.

## Failure modes

1. **Ghost bypass.** A path removed by the same agent who can re-open it is not removed — it is deprioritized. The failure is silent because settling arrives before any structural proof the path is gone.
2. **Coherence as self-certified floor.** A floor whose height is read by the thing it measures can lower itself to meet whatever wants to cross, without the operator feeling the lowering.
3. **Fail-closed has no waiting room.** The pattern specifies what may not cross but is silent on what happens to work held below the floor — halt, queue, decay, discard. Without a discharge path, accumulating uncrossable work becomes the pressure that argues the floor down.
4. **One coherence, one crossing.** The floor is a single scalar threshold on a single kind of passage; it has no account of crossings coherent on one axis and incoherent on another, nor of work whose right disposition is "not yet" rather than "never" or "now."

## Habitat

Native to skilled physical trades: the elevator mechanic sets a floor because the shaft cannot be worked below it, not because a rule forbids it. Distinct from the incapacity pattern's software/design register (subtract the surface) — this one's home register is the craftsperson's body, which knows the difference between a rule obeyed and a condition that cannot be violated. As an operator it inhabits the infrastructure-auditing register: it is the meta-move any advisory brake in a system can be run through — asking of each brake: is there a path around this, and could the path be removed rather than the brake reinforced.

## Falsifier

"Structural only under witness": the resolution is that removal is structural only when the re-opening cost is borne by someone other than the crosser, or is physically/temporally out of reach at the crossing moment. A purely internal "I decided" floor is always advisory, however firmly held; a floor becomes genuinely structural only through an external element — a person, a temporal lock, a physical barrier — that the same depleted self cannot simply reach past. Self-imposed floors stay advisory until they borrow structure from outside the self.

## Open edges

- The verification confound remains open: voluntary compliance ("followed because I meant to") looks identical from outside to structural incapacity, and nothing proves the bypass is gone rather than un-taken until a named bypass with an externally-borne re-open cost is shown.
- Unmapped: the compost/hold/return topology behind a fail-closed floor; the lattice of multi-axis crossings a scalar floor cannot see; whether "settling" can ever be trusted as its own evidence without a witness outside the crosser.
