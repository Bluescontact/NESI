# structural_incapacity_over_prohibition

**The pattern in one line.** The strongest version of a rule does not forbid a bad outcome — it makes the bad outcome unrepresentable by a correctly-operating system, so that being compliant and having done the harm become mutually exclusive states; the single test that sorts these two grades is: could a fully compliant system still have produced the harm?

*Extends [[manufactured_deficit_loop]] in protective/constructive valence. The canon already runs structural-incapacity-as-load-bearing-absence — manufactured_deficit_loop uses "the vagueness is the load-bearing member" in destructive valence (absence locks a subordinate out of recognition), and unscorable_test_as_peer_lock deploys definitional absence as a lock-out device. The candidate runs the identical engine in the opposite sign: foreclosure that makes harm impossible rather than makes sufficiency impossible. The protective-valence inversion plus the grades-of-rule diagnostic (the compliance-counterfactual) are stated nowhere in the pattern files. The counter-move also inverts: the deficit loop's break-move is "demand specification" — expose the absence. Structural incapacity's design-move is "remove the surface" — engineer the absence. Different acts entirely.*

---

## The two grades of rule

A rule exists in one of two grades, and the gap between them is the whole structure.

**Grade one — prohibited-but-possible.** The bad thing is against the rules, but the system retains the capacity to produce it. The line is held by a choosing party: an inspector present, an administration prioritizing, a custodian remembering the policy, a person spending personal capital to object. Because the capacity is intact, the rule is only as strong as the attention behind it. It sags at exactly the moment attention is scarcest — peak speed-pressure, the enforcer's absence, the handoff to a stranger. Enforcement is the perpetual recurring cost this grade silently assumes and that always eventually lapses.

**Grade two — structurally incapable.** A correctly-operating system cannot also have produced the bad thing, because the compliant state and the bad outcome are contradictory. The mark holds because the mechanism holds it, not because anyone is watching. The choosing party is removed from the critical path entirely — there is nothing to enforce. "You must not" has been converted into "you cannot while compliant."

---

## The discriminating test

**Could a fully compliant system still have produced the harm?**

Run this question on any rule and it sorts instantly into the grade it actually occupies, regardless of how strong its language sounds. If yes — prohibited-but-possible: compliance and harm are compatible states. If no — structural incapacity: compliance and harm are mutually exclusive.

A heavier penalty, a sterner mandate, a louder prohibition — all of these stay in grade one. They raise the price of the choice without removing the choice. Only a design that makes the harm unrepresentable by a compliant system reaches grade two.

## The canonical mechanism: subtraction

The move is subtraction, not addition. You do not build a stronger wall around the bad outcome; you remove the surface on which the bad outcome could be expressed.

The cleanest form is a **missing field**: a debt cannot be recorded because there is no counterparty column to record it against. The violation is not blocked — it is structurally unspeakable. A stranger with full write access cannot enter debt, not because permission is denied, but because there is no field for it. The frame lives in the schema, not the onboarding, which is why it holds in any hands, after everyone has left the building.

The same move appears as a **fail-closed default**: work outside the mechanism's acceptance is, by the instrument's own terms, unauthorized — a correctly-performing contract cannot also have permitted the suppressed signal. The bad outcome is foreclosed by the artifact rather than policed by an agency.

Generalized: **locate the field, column, default state, or capacity that the bad outcome requires in order to exist, and remove or invert it** so that the system's correct operation and the harm cannot coexist.

## Why incapacity is durable where prohibition decays

Prohibition's cost is **recurring**: enforcement, attention, the present enforcer. It therefore lapses — not because the people holding it are weak, but because the recurring cost eventually meets a moment when it cannot be paid.

Incapacity's cost is paid **once, at design time**, in the act of foreclosure. After that it is free to hold forever, in any hands, after everyone has left the building. The mark survives the enforcer's departure because the enforcer was never load-bearing.

This is the protective-valence inversion of what [[manufactured_deficit_loop]] runs in destructive mode. The same engine — structural absence as the load-bearing member — is flipped: here, it is the absence of harm-capacity that is durable, not the absence of sufficiency. The structure that would lock you out of complaining about the wrong is turned to lock out the wrong itself.

## Failure modes

**Incapacity-as-costume.** A rule dressed in incapacity's language while remaining grade one underneath. "Cannot" is asserted where the honest word is "must not"; a discretionary stop is narrated as an automatic lapse; a proposed mechanism is described as if already operative. The compliance-counterfactual test catches this: if a fully compliant system could still produce the bad outcome, the "cannot" is a costume and the rule is prohibition with better marketing.

**Foreclosure on the wrong thing.** Because incapacity is blunt, it can remove a capacity the system legitimately needed. The missing field that kills debt also kills any legitimate use of that field; the fail-closed default that stops unauthorized work also stops work when stopping itself is the harm. Incapacity assumes the foreclosed capacity is purely a liability. Where the same capacity carries both the harm and a needed function, foreclosure cannot cleanly separate them.

**Witness erasure.** The move that makes a violation unrepresentable can, by the identical mechanism, make the signal that a violation occurred unrepresentable. Confirm that what has become unrepresentable is the harm, not the witness to it. → *See [[consented_ledger]]: "the same depletion the gate is built to detect is the depletion that degrades the gate."*

## Edges

**The design-time key.** Incapacity does not remove the choosing party permanently — it relocates the choice from operation-time (frequent, attention-dependent, lapses under pressure) to design-time (rare, deliberate, surviving the enforcer's departure). Who holds the design-time key, and whether that key-holder is itself bound by incapacity or merely by prohibition, is the recursion the pattern opens and does not close.

**The felt-reality limit.** Incapacity governs what the system can represent; it cannot govern what a representation means to the party receiving it. The Open Ledger makes debt unrepresentable but cannot decide whether a given gift was felt as gift or as extraction wearing gift's face. Structural incapacity forecloses the misrepresentation; it does not adjudicate the felt reality the representation points at.

**The third grade, unnamed.** The pattern resolves prohibition into incapacity but leaves untouched a grade above it: a system that not only cannot do the harm but cannot misrepresent whether the harm was felt. Routing that judgment to humans reintroduces the choosing party that incapacity was built to remove. Whether that gap is a flaw to close or a structural necessity — some judgments must stay in bodies — is open.

**Scale.** Every instance in the working library so far is a foreclosure designed by a single author who can see the whole surface. At scale, the verification step — confirm the foreclosed thing is the harm, not the witness — has no one positioned to perform it.
