## Transferable form

In any domain that encodes signal through a physical substrate, some combinations destroy the signal the medium was built to carry — not by violating a rule but by crossing the threshold at which the medium stops transmitting. A grammar that names these collapse points is not imposing prohibition; it is reading out a physics. The constraint is valid when crossing it produces incoherence, not when an authority declares it invalid.

## Mechanism

The medium pre-selects which combinations can carry differentiated signal before any practitioner or authority chooses. Parallel fifths collapse acoustic voices into unison; certain Effort factor combinations cannot be held in a body simultaneously; metal-on-metal in heraldry destroys the figure-ground contrast that makes the charge readable at distance. In each case the grammar names a collapse point, not an offense. The distinction the pattern turns on is unutterable versus undesirable — the grammar structurally forecloses the combination, does not merely discourage it.

## Failure modes

1. **Convention wearing physics' clothes.** Parallel fifths were grammatical in medieval polyphony; the listening community's coherence threshold shifted, not the acoustics. Any culturally stabilized prohibition eventually feels physical. Without a falsifier distinguishing physics-derived from convention-derived, the pattern redescribes whatever survived long enough to feel inevitable.
2. **Legibility for whom.** Legibility is not medium-absolute — it is receiver-indexed. The tincture rule fails for colorblind readers; Effort coherence fails for practitioners trained in different somatic grammars. The boundary condition is perceptual community, not medium.
3. **Grammar without a carrier.** Jazz re-admitted parallel fifths; Forsythe re-admitted Effort incoherence as expressive material. The physics persisted; the unutterability did not. Legibility physics is necessary but not sufficient to generate grammar constraints — something must translate the physics into explicit rule form and hold it in force, and when the carrier fractures, the same combination becomes utterable again.

## Falsifier

The test that separates physics from convention: does re-admission by a later tradition make the combination legible again? If yes, it was convention wearing physics' clothes (parallel fifths — jazz and Forsythe re-admitted it). If the combination stays permanently unreadable regardless of who tries it or when, it's physics (metal-on-metal in heraldry has no re-admission case in its history — two same-luminance fields adjacent destroy the figure-ground contrast a viewer needs at range, an optics fact true regardless of who's looking or what era).

## Open edges

- Can a practitioner read constraints out of a medium they have direct access to, or only inherit them from a tradition that already encoded them? If derivable, the pattern is a discovery instrument; if inheritable only, it is a legitimation story for established grammars.
- The unbuilt load-path: the central claim (constraints are derived from physical legibility conditions, not institutional agreement) remains asserted more than it is given a general derivation procedure — the source supplies the discriminating test, not a method for finding new instances from scratch.
- The unnamed carrier problem points toward a class of constraint-setters whose authority derives from demonstrated perceptual or somatic access to the medium — a distinct pattern this one implies but does not contain.
