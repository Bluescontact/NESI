## Transferable Form

When one party continuously supplies the structural frame, others operate inside it without developing their own capacity. Withdrawal of that provision — held without hostility — forces internal development that sustained supply actively prevents. The more generous structural act is sometimes the one that looks like absence.

## Development

**Mechanism.** A provided frame functions as load-bearing infrastructure. Occupants route through it rather than generating their own. The frame-holder's presence creates a structural dependency even when the intent is support. Dependency is not failure of character in the occupants — it is the rational response to available load-bearing capacity. As long as the frame holds, internal development is deferred; there is no developmental pressure.

Withdrawal removes the external load-path. Internal structure must now form or the system collapses. This is the generative condition. It is not created by instruction, encouragement, or explanation — only by the absence of the substitute.

**Structural requirement.** Withdrawal must be clean and without punitive signal. A punitive withdrawal reads as enforcement and triggers defensive contraction, not development. A withdrawal held with goodwill reads as trust — it signals that the demonstrated capacity is available to the other, not privately held by the withdrawing party. The faith component is load-bearing: the withdrawer must actually believe the other can develop structure, or the withdrawal becomes abandonment with a philosophical overlay.

Timing is structural, not emotional. The question is not "is this comfortable" but "has enough been demonstrated that the form is available." Comfort-based timing produces premature return, which is the most common failure mode.

The frame-holder's extended provision is often motivated by care, competence, or efficiency — all of which accelerate the dependency. High-capacity frame-holders create the deepest dependencies. The pattern is hardest to enact precisely where the frame-holder is most skilled.

Boundary held with goodwill is the mechanism, not the sentiment. The boundary is the generative structure. Goodwill is the signal that makes it readable as trust rather than rejection.

## Failure modes

1. **Partial withdrawal** — reducing provision without removing it; creates ambiguity, the dependency pattern searches for the residual frame and stabilizes there.
2. **Withdrawal as punishment** — hostility contaminates the signal; the developmental condition collapses into a relational rupture that must be repaired before structure can form.
3. **Premature withdrawal** — removing frame before minimum internal scaffolding exists; produces collapse rather than development. The pattern requires that enough has been demonstrated that the occupant has seen the form, not merely received its outputs.
4. **Rescuing the collapse** — returning frame at the first sign of difficulty; negates the generative condition and reinforces the dependency with a new data point (frame returns under pressure).

## Open edges

- The pattern assumes the occupant has sufficient exposure to the demonstrated form to internalize it — but the threshold for "enough demonstration" is not specified and may not be knowable in advance. There is no instrument for reading which condition obtains.
- The pattern cannot account for cases where the frame-holder's withdrawal is not genuinely available — where the withdrawing party is still structurally entangled (shared resources, ongoing obligation, positional authority). Partial structural entanglement may make clean withdrawal impossible even when correctly understood.
- The pattern positions the frame-holder as the one who determines readiness and acts — this preserves a kind of authority even in withdrawal. Whether there is a version where the occupant initiates their own developmental break, rather than having it structured for them, is unresolved. If not, the pattern may reproduce the asymmetry it attempts to dissolve.
- The witness-has-no-witness problem appears here: who holds the frame-holder when they withdraw? The act requires faith that is structurally unsupported — the very capacity being demonstrated (self-generated structure) is the same capacity the frame-holder must sustain alone during withdrawal.
