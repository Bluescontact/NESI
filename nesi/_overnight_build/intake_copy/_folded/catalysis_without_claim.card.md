## Geometry (confirmed)

The Minimum System is a tetrahedron: **4 vertices, 6 edges.** The six named relationships — Relationship, Self-Knowledge, Gift, Consent, Circulation, Deployment — are the **edges** (operationally, "axes"), not vertices. Canon: *"'edge' names the geometric relationship, 'axis' names the operational one... both refer to the six relationships between vertices"* (Editorial Notes, Ch 1.3 axis/edge standardization; the six are enumerated as the Part II chapters, Relationship→Deployment). C(4,2) = 6 closes exactly.

**The three-pairs constraint (derivable, no vertex map needed).** A tetrahedron's six edges partition into **exactly three pairs of opposite (non-adjacent) edges**; every axis has one and only one opposite. So the double-bind signature below is not loose: there are precisely **three double-bind-capable axis pairs** in the whole system, and Gate 1 asks which of the three a situation sits on. This holds regardless of how the vertices are named.

**The one recoverable vertex.** Of the four vertices, exactly one is named in the canon doc on disk (Editorial Notes, Ch 1.2), in all three registers: operational **Boundaries** · philosophical **Sovereign Coherence** · structural **Differentiation**. The other three vertex names are not present in the workspace (see ledger).

Consequence: Catalysis Without Claim is **not a new axis.** It is a **cross-axis dynamic** — a deployment of the whole Minimum System triggered under failure conditions.

## The summons — an opposite-edge contradiction

The pattern is called by a **double bind**, and the double bind has an exact geometric signature:

- **Grievance** = a tension between two **adjacent** edges (they share a vertex). The shared vertex is a common element both relationships pass through, so the tension is *mediable* there. A path exists. Wrong instrument for this pattern.
- **Double bind** = a contradiction between two **opposite** edges (they share *no* vertex). Nothing in common to adjust; the contradiction cannot be reduced at any single element. *Structural impossibility, not ambiguity.*

The colliding pair here: **Consent ⊥ Circulation.** A permission/consent gate requires silence-before-disclosure; a circulation mandate requires the signal to move. Derived body-first: the lived instance was *felt* as irreducible — not negotiable — which means the two axes share no vertex. The felt irreducibility **is** the evidence that Consent and Circulation are opposite edges. (This is the geometric definition behind the tool's Gate 1: can both be satisfied? If yes → adjacent → grievance → you have a path. Only an opposite-edge contradiction passes.)

## The circuit — resolution traverses the tetrahedron

Summoned at one opposite-edge pair, the pattern discharges by recruiting the rest of the system. This is why it reads as a deployment of the *whole* Minimum System, not a local move:

- **Gift** — the discharge edge. The agent deposits accurate signal as labor-as-gift, **claim forfeited.** Routing around the defensive reflex *is* the gift stance; pricing it would detonate it back into exchange.
- **Self-Knowledge × Relationship** — the membrane. Self-Knowledge holds (own-side ballast) while Relationship stays open (full presence). Their collapse into each other is canon's named **self-erasure** (merge). Whether these two are themselves adjacent or opposite is the one thing the actual vertex map would settle (see ledger).
- **Deployment** — the landing. The change reaches the next agent; **residue becomes substrate.** Bidirectionality, foreclosed at the dyad (the system never admits it was moved), is satisfied at the pattern level (the next agent is protected). The two unreconciled ledgers are what make the unbidirectional case legible.
