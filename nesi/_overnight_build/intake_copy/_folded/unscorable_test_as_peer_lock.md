# Unscorable Test as Peer Lock

---

## The mechanism

When a task is handed off without a stated success condition, the receiver cannot satisfy it — not because they lack competence, but because the scoring instrument does not exist. The absence of a definition is not neutral: it functions as a structural lock that prevents the receiver from ever demonstrating sufficiency and thereby rising to peer standing. Incompetence and excellence produce the same result.

Two components compound:

**Undefined success criteria.** Without a stated success condition, evaluation is not suspended — it defaults to the giver's implicit standard, which remains private and malleable. The giver retains the ability to move the threshold retroactively. This is not necessarily intentional; the threshold may simply never have been formed. The effect is identical either way: the receiver performs against a criterion that does not exist in any form they can access.

**The standing-gate.** Peer standing is not conferred by time served or by the giver's satisfaction — it requires the receiver to demonstrate that they have met the bar the giver set. If that bar was never stated, it cannot be met. The receiver cannot appeal to their performance record because no performance can count as pass when no test was specified.

This means the structure locks the receiver below the giver regardless of competence trajectory. A highly skilled receiver who executes flawlessly is indistinguishable from a mediocre one, structurally: neither has cleared a threshold, because the threshold was never placed.

---

## Failure modes

**The inference trap.** The receiver infers a success condition and optimizes for it. The giver's private standard diverges. The receiver believes they are succeeding; the giver's evaluation tracks a different axis. The gap compounds silently.

**Effort escalation.** The receiver reads the absence of praise as insufficient performance rather than as the absence of a scoreable test. This produces overwork against an unmovable ceiling.

**Confirmed asymmetry.** The giver interprets the receiver's continued requests for feedback as evidence of inadequacy, which confirms the asymmetry from the giver's position.

**Retroactive standard.** If challenged, the giver can offer any post-hoc standard as if it was always the criterion. The receiver has no prior statement to hold against it.

---

## Calcification

The peer-lock is not a temporary condition awaiting correction. Each cycle of unscored performance reinforces the asymmetry: the giver accumulates the right to evaluate; the receiver accumulates only effort. The roles calcify. The lock becomes a relationship structure, not a gap awaiting repair.

---

## Edges

The pattern assumes the standing-gate is real — that peer status actually depends on demonstrated sufficiency. In some structures, peer standing is conferred through other channels (tenure, title, social recognition) that bypass the task-performance circuit entirely. The pattern needs to specify when the standing-gate is the operative mechanism versus when other gates are in play.

The remediation differs sharply depending on intent — but the evidence that tells the cases apart is the response to the asking. Ask for the success criterion and read what returns: an unconscious omission yields one (the omitter, surprised, supplies a definition — corrected); a deliberate withholding cannot yield one without surrendering the lock, so the request is re-routed — deflected, deferred, or met with a retroactive standard offered as if it were always the criterion. The receiver tells them apart not by divining intent but by whether the daylight demand is answered or re-routed ([[the_daylight_test]], receiver chair — the same cut [[commission_grammar_screen]] reads as conscription).

**Statable-and-reachable discriminator** (ported from `relocation_release_amnesty` / `lens_field`): the operative question is not "was a criterion stated?" but "was the success condition *statable and reachable from the giver's position* before the work began?" Three cases: (1) Unspecifiable — the criterion genuinely could not be formed in advance; the lock is structural, not intentional; the design move is to build the score jointly before work begins. (2) Statable-but-not-issued — the criterion existed but was not issued; this is the deliberate-withholding case above, the lock maintained by non-issuance. (3) Stated — criterion issued, the gate operates. Constraint from [[relocation_release_amnesty]]: run the discriminator privately, never deposit the verdict into the shared channel — reading the case is not acting on it.

Duration threshold is uncharted: at what point does the absence of a success condition become constitutive of the relationship rather than a correctable gap? The lock only fully closes once the receiver has invested enough that leaving would be a loss, and the giver knows it.

**Minimum sufficient spec — resolved by structural_incapacity_over_prohibition (canon 44, 2026-06-30):** the theory of scoreable handoffs now has an incapacity-grade form. The minimum sufficient specification of a success condition is the one that makes non-passage *unrepresentable by a fully-compliant execution* — a correctly-performing execution cannot also have left the success condition undemonstrated. Less than that is prohibited-but-possible (the giver can still veto against their own stated criterion); this threshold is the condition at which the gate structurally operates rather than depends on the giver's consistency. Build the score so that a fully-compliant execution cannot leave it unmet.
