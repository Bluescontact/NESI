## Transferable form

When the same failure pattern repeats across every external receiver — the catcher misses, the handle slips, the offer doesn't land — the audit belongs on the lens, not the field. Keeping a handoff boundary undefined is an architectural move: it holds the work inside the body, the only place it has consistently felt safe, because a defined boundary makes the work separable and therefore available for external verdict. The protection is real and pre-rational — it formed before any calculation ran.

## Mechanism

The mechanism operates through boundary softness as containment. A clearly-defined handoff has a specific failure mode: once the line is drawn, someone else can meet it or fail to meet it, and both outcomes produce exposure. The work becomes a distinct object that exists outside the person and is now subject to evaluation on its own terms. Keeping the boundary undefined forecloses that exposure structurally — not by avoiding risk consciously, but by never creating the surface where risk could arrive.

The diagnostic signature is the universal receiver failure: if every direction of offering produces the same result (catcher inadequate, handle untrustworthy, landing incomplete), the common variable is the offer itself — specifically, the structural condition under which the offer is made. An offer with a soft boundary is not fully separable. An offer that cannot be fully received cannot be fully refused. The protection runs in both directions simultaneously.

The mechanism predates choice. The architecture formed under conditions where external verdict was genuinely dangerous — where being seen clearly and found wanting was not abstract risk but actual cost. The body learned: keep it inside, keep the boundary soft, keep the work yours. This is not indispensability as ego structure. It is a load-bearing wall that no longer needs to bear the load it was built for, but has not been identified as the wall yet.

**The audit move.** When the same failure shows up in every direction, screen the offer itself. Is the handoff defined? Is there a line that, once drawn, would make the work exist independently? If no such line can be found, or if every candidate line dissolves under examination, the boundary-softness is the structural feature to name — not the inadequacy of receivers.

## Failure modes

1. The pattern reads as external dysfunction — every partner is inadequate, every institution cannot hold it — while the generative source is internal boundary management. The diagnosis stays permanently external.
2. The person becomes the only vessel. The work's coherence is real but locked to the body that holds it. No transmission is possible without the body present.
3. The protection is metabolically expensive. Work held fully inside the body must be continuously maintained there. No rest from holding it.
4. The wall becomes invisible. Architecture formed before conscious choice cannot be seen by the same apparatus that formed it. External feedback is filtered through the same lens that built the wall: "they don't understand it" is both a possible truth and a structural defense against the moment of definition.

## Falsifier

If a receiver type appears that would actually hold the work, and the boundary-softness dissolves in response rather than perceiving new inadequacy in that receiver, the protection was waiting for a trustworthy enough external — not structurally unsatisfiable. If instead the pattern generates inadequate receivers regardless of who approaches the threshold, the protection cannot be satisfied by any external, ever, and the wall is load-bearing in a stronger sense.

## Edges

The pattern does not resolve when the protection is still appropriate. Some work genuinely cannot be handed off — the person is constitutive, not merely a vessel. The audit cannot distinguish from inside the protection whether the boundary is soft because the work is irreducibly embodied or because definition would produce exposure the body cannot yet bear. These look identical from inside the architecture.

The pattern points toward but does not reach: what it looks like to define a boundary without immediately subjecting the work to external verdict — whether there is a transitional state between "fully inside" and "fully exposed" that the body can inhabit without collapse. If that state exists, the work becomes transmissible without requiring the person to disappear from it first.
