# Defined-Version Diagnostic

*Provenance: unholdable_delegation meeting-prep pass, 2026-06-18 · PROMOTED 2026-07-28 (crossing1)*
*Library: EXTENDS `commission_grammar_screen.md` — Commission Grammar Screen asks whether the commission is open or pre-collapsed, but has no instrument for *producing* the diagnostic evidence. This pattern fills that gap: offer the explicitly-drawn version of a genuinely definable boundary and read the response. It is the detection instrument upstream of both `commission_grammar_screen` and `expertise_conscripted_as_rescue_fuel` — the move that generates the diagnostic signal those patterns interpret. ## Transferable form

When a boundary or standard is genuinely definable, offering the defined version — the explicit procedure, the written line — produces a diagnostic result: acceptance rules out the pattern, sustained decline confirms it. The refusals arrive one at a time, each plausible, which is precisely the signature. A boundary that can be drawn but isn't being drawn is not hard to define; it is being kept soft by the party who benefits from its softness.

## Mechanism

The mechanism is an asymmetry between stated difficulty and actual availability. The delegator claims the work is inherently ambiguous or judgment-dependent, while the defined version — if offered — sits unused. The test is not rhetorical: it is structural. Present the specification. If it gets adopted, the boundary was genuinely unclear and the pattern is absent. If it gets declined, the ambiguity was functional for someone.

Each individual refusal being sensible is not a coincidence — it is the failure mode. Sensible-sounding refusals accumulate into a pattern that no single refusal reveals. The diagnostic catches what the case-by-case reading cannot: the shape of sustained non-adoption across time.

The pattern requires that the defined version be genuinely available. Work that is undefinable in principle — where no crisp specification could exist regardless of effort — is not this. The test only has force when the specification was offerable. If the delegator could have drawn the line and did not, the softness is a choice.

The structural cost lands on whoever works inside the undefined boundary. Ambiguity concentrates judgment-burden downward or outward while the delegator retains interpretive authority without exercising it explicitly. The undrawn line is a form of control: reachable when convenient, deniable when not.

## Failure modes

1. **Premature diagnostic.** The diagnostic is offered pre-emptively before enough instances accumulate, removing the confirmatory pattern before it's readable.
2. **Poorly-drawn defined version.** The defined version is offered but poorly — allowing genuine quality objections to stand in for structural resistance. The test requires that the defined version be actually good, not a proxy for an argument.

## Falsifier

The diagnostic confirms presence or absence but does not locate motive — a delegator may keep a boundary soft from anxiety, from habit, from power-preservation, or from genuine unrecognized confusion about whether it's definable. If the defined version, once genuinely and well-drawn, is adopted, the pattern is absent for that instance regardless of any prior suspicion.

## Edges

The pattern assumes a single delegator. In distributed authority structures — committees, shared ownership, consensus bodies — the refusals may be structurally overdetermined: no individual benefits from the softness but the softness persists because no individual can close it alone. Whether the diagnostic applies to collective actors or only to individuals with unitary authority is unresolved.

The test also carries a cost: offering the defined version changes the situation. After the offer, the pattern either collapses (if adopted) or crystallizes (if declined). There is no neutral observation. The diagnostician becomes a participant, which means the instrument alters the system it reads. That alteration may be useful, but it must be named — this is not a passive diagnostic tool.
