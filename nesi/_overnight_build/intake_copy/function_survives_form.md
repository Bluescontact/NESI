## Transferable Form

When a form that requires external conditions to activate collapses, it does not automatically take its function with it. The function either re-embeds in something the person already maintains without external activation — practice, vocabulary, design principles — or it composts completely. There is no partial recovery: the cut is binary, not gradient.

## Development

**Mechanism.** A form's dependency on external conditions is its activation address — where the call originates from outside the person. While the form is active, this dependency is invisible; it reads as capability. Collapse is the first moment the dependency becomes legible. At that point, extraction is not content-transfer but re-addressing: can the call be rerouted through something the person already maintains? Re-embedding requires prior inhabitation — the function must have already touched a practice, vocabulary, or design principle the person keeps running by their own metabolism. If that contact never occurred, no extraction is possible. What the person maintains without external activation is the only substrate the function can move into. What has no such landing site composts completely.

**The binary.** Most loss-language operates on a gradient. This pattern does not: function either survives intact in a substrate-independent vehicle or is unrecoverable. Triage after form-death is sorting into two bins. This is structurally load-bearing, not rhetorical.

**The forensic register.** This pattern is native to the moment after collapse — sorting what a person carries forward from what died with the container. It is inoperable during a form's active life and inapplicable to hypothetical or future collapse. Its habitat is loss events: institutional endings, relationship closure, practice abandonment — anywhere a person must distinguish what they still hold from what required the form to exist.

## Failure modes

1. **Extraction looks like possession.** Articulating a function, naming it correctly, even teaching it — none of this is evidence of re-embedding. The pattern has no internal test to distinguish genuine transfer from fluent description of a composted function. False positives occur at the moment of highest loss.
2. **The external/internal boundary is unstable.** What reads as an internal vehicle from one register requires external conditions from another — uninterrupted time, a stable floor, a safe enough situation. If the line is drawn at the wrong altitude, the pattern misclassifies a vehicle as substrate-independent while the dependency has only moved to a less visible layer.
3. **Composting windows can close before the composting event is recognized.** A form in late-stage structural collapse may degrade its function before the collapse is formally identified. The extractable window may be narrow and prior to the composting event, not after it.

## Falsifier / pre-collapse test (resolved at re-screen 2026-07-15)

Mechanism: re-embedding requires prior inhabitation — the function must already have touched a practice, vocabulary, or design principle the person runs by their own metabolism, before collapse, not after. Test (runnable pre-collapse, no need to wait for the form to die): with the external condition still present but deliberately not invoked, ask the person to perform the function unprompted, from what they already carry. If they can produce it without reaching for the external trigger, prior inhabitation exists and the function will survive collapse. If they cannot produce it at all without the trigger, there is no landing site yet and it will compost. This converts the binary from a retrospective forensic call into a live, pre-collapse probe.

## Open edges

- Whether the binary is genuinely binary or whether the gradient is simply too fine to resolve at the forensic register.
- The external/internal line: at what altitude is a vehicle genuinely self-maintained, and how does the pattern distinguish moved dependency from eliminated dependency?
- The timing problem: if the extractable window closes before collapse is recognized, is there a pre-forensic signal — and does naming it require a different pattern in a different register?
- The person whose practice, vocabulary, and design principles are themselves externally conditioned has no substrate-independent vehicles at all — some losses are total, and the pattern currently cannot say so with mechanism, only with assertion.
