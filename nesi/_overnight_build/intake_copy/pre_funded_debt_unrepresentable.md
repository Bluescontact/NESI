## Transferable Form

Two parties discharge their entire contribution to each other at a single moment of agreement, so no giving or receiving remains to accrue afterward — the time axis a ledger needs has zero length inside the form. Score-keeping is not a breach of such an arrangement but an exit from it: the moment a tally appears, a different contract is already in force. Debt is not forbidden; it is made impossible to establish by accident, reachable only by the deliberate act of writing a new agreement.

## Development

**Mechanism.** Debt requires a time-gap between giving and receiving. The form compresses both parties' full contribution into one instant — signing. One party gives an entire container up front; the other gives presence and self-containment up front. There is no "after" inside the form for entries to be written on, so nothing accrues: no day is earned by behavior, no favor creates an IOU. Elapsed time itself is demonetized — the third week costs and merits exactly what the first did, because duration was priced once, at signing, never metered. This closes the channel most credit sneaks through ("after all these months...").

**Enforcement dissolves into recognition.** The form has no penalty clause because it needs none: keeping score does not violate the contract, it instantiates a different one. The moving part is a type-check — "is a ledger now present? then we are no longer under this form" — and detection of a tally IS the termination event. Debt is not absolutely unrepresentable: it is routed through the highest-cost channel, a deliberate rewrite. The claim is accident-proofing against ambient drift of favors, not abolition of credit.

**Taxonomic position.** Unrepresentability previously had two routes: subtract the field (schema without a debt slot) or complete the act unilaterally (gift). This is a third route — bilateral saturation: both parties zero the balance forever by discharging fully and simultaneously at t0. Unrepresentability by fullness rather than by absence. This adds a mechanism axis (absence / unilateral completion / bilateral saturation) rather than filing one more species under structural incapacity.

**Habitat.** The domestic interior — hosting, cohabitation, guest-rooms — where credit normally forms invisibly through micro-favors and "earning your keep," and where contracts are taboo. The form imports contractual saturation into that register, making the home legible without making it transactional.

## Distinction from neighboring patterns (resolved at re-screen)

Against the Open Ledger's Topology A (held-key subtraction): debt is unrepresentable there because the schema itself has no field for it — there is no column to write a balance into, regardless of behavior; a key-holder controls the schema and the representational slot simply doesn't exist. Bilateral saturation: the schema COULD carry a debt field, but nothing ever populates it, because both parties discharged their full contribution simultaneously at t0 — there is no elapsed "after" for a balance to accrue in. Test that separates them: **absence-of-representation** (Topology A — no slot exists) versus **absence-of-time** (saturation — a slot could exist but nothing has occurred to fill it). Against [[structural_incapacity_over_prohibition]]: that pattern already holds field-subtraction and unilateral-completion as two routes to unrepresentability; saturation is a genuinely third route (bilateral, simultaneous, time-axis-collapsing) neither of those two names. One library, one name per move: saturation keeps its own name because it fails by a different mechanism (no time) than Topology A (no slot) or unilateral completion (one-sided closure).

## Failure modes

1. **Asymmetric events reopen the ledger.** A post-signing shock — damage, emergency care, extra labor — has no slot in the contract, so accrual pressure restarts in the unwritten layer; the true boundary condition is "nothing accrues until something un-priced happens." Any drip of value after signing reopens the temporal axis; open-ended arrangements must therefore be re-signed, and the 72-hour written rule may be the re-signing clock.
2. **Unrepresentable is not unfelt.** The guest tiptoes, the host over-gives; a felt ledger can run precisely because there is no legitimate place to discharge it. The falsifier is a body-read, not a document-read.
3. **Definitional exit is not enforcement.** When one party defects, the other holds a dead form and a live grievance, with no observed exit, renegotiation, or repair path. Protection is only as strong as both parties' voluntary fluency in the form — which caps who it can be given to.

## Falsifier / evidentiary floor

This is a single untested instance carrying a genuinely distinct mechanism: the saturation route is real and library-worthy, but every claim about lived operation is definitional — contract text, 72-hour mechanics, and observed failure paths are missing. What is filed here is a mechanism classification with its honest edges, not yet a fully evidenced pattern.

## Open edges

- Who performs the type-check when a tally appears, and what happens to the guest's housing the instant it fails — the moment definition must become mechanism is unbuilt.
- The anti-field is unshown: the actual written terms that make debt unwritable rather than unwritten are missing from the source.
- The 72-hour rule's mechanics are unknown; if it is the re-signing clock that keeps the temporal axis closed, it is load-bearing and undocumented.
- Scope may shrink to short, low-variance arrangements if the form cannot metabolize asymmetric events.
- The felt ledger remains unmeasured: verification requires a body-read of both parties, and no such read exists.
- Whether saturation earns its own taxonomy axis or collapses back into structural incapacity awaits a second instance.
