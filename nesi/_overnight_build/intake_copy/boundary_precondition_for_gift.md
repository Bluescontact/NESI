## Statement

At a table where at least one participant is actively metabolizing others' output as fuel, giving *more* does not soften the table — it feeds the greediest mouth and converts the giver into feedstock. What makes an outflow land as a gift rather than as raw material is not the size of the offering but a **boundary**: a maintained edge that keeps the giver a distinct agent. Remove the edge and there is no counterparty for the gift to reach — only more terrain for the game to absorb.

## What "boundary" is — the operational definition

The word has been doing undefined work, and the whole pattern's false positive lives in the gap. Define it by a **two-part test**, both parts required:

1. **It refuses absorption.** The edge keeps the giver from being metabolized — there is something the giver will not let be taken, and that refusal is what makes the giver register as a *player* rather than as *feedstock*. Distinctness itself is the boundary; a giver with no refusal presents no membrane and is read by the game as terrain.
2. **It transmits.** The edge keeps an outflow live *across* it. Something crosses. The boundary is the shape of the channel, not the closing of it.

An edge that passes (1) but fails (2) is **a locked door, not a boundary** — a fortified refusal that transmits nothing. The pattern's own logic misreads it as a clean gift, because a naive boundary-check reads only cross / no-cross and a locked door satisfies "the no is intact." So the definition is load-bearing precisely as a *discriminator*: a keystone is an edge that transmits while refusing absorption; a wall is an edge that refuses absorption while transmitting nothing. The pattern is about keystones. Walls are out of scope, and calling a wall a clean gift is the characteristic error.

## Who enforces it

A boundary is not a structure sitting in place — it is **a continuously enforced act**, and an unheld boundary is not a boundary. This is the "it needs a body" problem: the instant the holder tires, sleeps, or leaves, the edge decays back into floor-without-boundary and the game resumes absorbing. Left there, the pattern's promise ("let the boundary do the work") is a lie — the labor reappears as permanent guard-duty.

The resolution routes to the `exclusion_before_offer` / `load_to_form` family: **the enforcer is either a live body or a load-time author.** A boundary can be paid for once, at loading time, so that no one holds it live at the encounter — the structure *remembers* the refusal rather than *guarding* it. Where the encounter space is roughly enumerable in advance, load-time payment dissolves the guard-duty; where it is not, a live holder is required and the guard-duty is real and must be named, not wished away. "Who enforces it" therefore has exactly two honest answers, and a boundary with *neither* a named body nor a load-time author is not a boundary — it is a claim.

## The absorption mechanism

The game is a **metabolizer**: it converts whatever enters into its own substrate. An unbounded giver presents no membrane, so the game never encounters a counterparty — it encounters more input — and the giver is re-served as the game's own output. This is why the failure mode is not exhaustion, debt, or binding the receiver (the registers the neighbor patterns hold) but **assimilation**: the giver ceases to exist as an independent actor. Game-theoretic annihilation, not wearing out. The boundary is the minimum condition for being a player at all: no membrane, no counterparty, no gift — only feedstock and the mouth it feeds.

## The trigger — bounded to an extractor actually present

The pattern must fire **only where a ruthless participant is actually at the table, keeping score.** This is the resolution of crystal 9's objection (that a maintained edge merely relocates the game one scale down rather than breaking it) — resolved by *scoping*, not by over-claiming:

- **Habitat:** an adversarial table — a contest with a scoreboard and a winner the giver can lose to. Here, "give more" stops being kindness and becomes a losing move, and the boundary is what converts a losing outflow into a gift.
- **Out of habitat:** trust, intimacy, care — any table with no ruthless participant. Run the pattern here and the boundary excludes the *vulnerable* in the exact motion meant to exclude the ruthless, because the edge reads only cross / no-cross and cannot read intent. Outside the extraction-lab, "clean" reads as cold. The pattern hardening a relationship against a threat that is not present is its characteristic out-of-habitat harm.
- **The gate:** before running the pattern, detect whether an extractor is present. The pattern *assumes* one but cannot itself sense one; that detection is a precondition the pattern depends on and does not supply. Absent the detection, do not run it.

**On crystal 9 directly.** A boundary does not break the game *globally and permanently* — it breaks the game **at this table, for this giver, this pass.** Drawing the edge mints a new inside, and inside it the same contest reruns with its own most-ruthless participant; the clean gift at one scale is the subsidy-to-the-ruthless at the next scale down. That is not the boundary failing — it is the recursion structure, and naming it is what keeps the pattern honest. The strong form ("the boundary breaks the game") is false. The true form: **a boundary is a temporary, holder-dependent (or load-time-paid) break at one scale, re-earned each pass.** It is a local move with no terminating condition, and claiming otherwise is the unearned headline the source could not pay for.

## The move

1. **Detect the table.** Is a ruthless participant actually keeping score? If no → do not run this pattern; a boundary here excludes the vulnerable. If yes → continue.
2. **Set the edge, both parts.** Does it refuse absorption (keep you a player) *and* transmit (keep an outflow live)? If it only refuses, you built a wall; if it only transmits, you built a subsidy pipe to the greediest mouth.
3. **Name the enforcer.** A live body (accept the guard-duty) or a load-time author (pay the no once, let the structure remember it). Neither → it is not yet a boundary.
4. **Scope the claim.** You broke the game at this table, this pass. A new inside was minted. Re-earn it next scale down; do not claim a permanent global break.

## Edges

- **Detection is the missing organ.** The whole pattern is gated on "is an extractor present," and the pattern cannot sense one — it can only assume. A mis-detection runs the cold-boundary harm on a warm table.
- **The learning adversary.** A boundary that breaks the game at t=0 is a published rule the game metabolizes at t=1; the extractor's specialty is turning your edge into their next attack vector. Holder-dependent re-earning partially covers this, but a sufficiently adaptive surround maps and routes around any fixed edge.
- **Load-time payment inherits the stale-no limit.** Paying the boundary at loading time only excludes what the giver could imagine when loading (the `exclusion_before_offer` failure mode). Novel encounters exceed the remembered refusal and the guard-duty returns.

## Falsifier

A verified instance at a genuinely adversarial table where an unbounded outflow was *not* absorbed as feedstock — where giving more, with no maintained edge, still landed as a gift and did not feed the scoring participant — weakens the core claim. Alternatively: a maintained two-part boundary (refuses + transmits) that still collapsed the giver into feedstock would show the definition does not capture what protects the giver, and the discriminator is mis-drawn.
