## Transferable form

Chronic friction at the survival layer — housing instability, physical safety, logistics that never resolve — operates as a continuous cognitive tax that suppresses bandwidth for everything upstream. Removing that friction is not an upgrade to comfort; it is restoration of the baseline capability that all other work requires. The capability floor must be stable before any other floor can bear weight.

## Mechanism

Cognitive bandwidth is finite and non-renewable within a given window. Survival-level problems do not queue behind other work — they preempt it. The body and nervous system treat unresolved housing, safety, and physical logistics as open loops that consume attentional resources in the background regardless of intent to set them aside. This is architectural, not motivational: the working memory available for any other task is the total minus whatever is allocated to the open survival loop.

The mechanism is load-bearing, not motivational. A floor that is unstable transfers no load upward reliably. You can place structure on an unstable floor, but the structure will absorb the instability rather than doing its own work. A person operating on unresolved survival conditions is spending structural capacity on stabilization that should be available for construction.

Work exchange, WWOOFing, or any arrangement that converts survival friction into resolved conditions functions as capability restoration — not retreat. The distinction is load, not luxury: does the intervention free up bandwidth that was previously consumed by the open loop? If yes, it is a capability move.

The pattern also interacts with time: survival friction is a recurring drain, not a one-time cost. The cumulative cost compounds — not just bandwidth lost today, but the work that did not happen across the full duration. Early resolution is disproportionately valuable; the return on closing the loop accrues forward across the entire remaining window.

## Failure modes

1. **Misclassification** — the cost is invisible because it is ambient. From outside, the person appears to underperform relative to evident capability. From inside, the person may normalize the drain and attribute the suppression to lack of discipline, wrong focus, or bad sequencing. Both readings miss the structural cause.
2. **Comfort-framing trap** — addressing survival friction gets coded as self-indulgence or avoidance, as if stability is a reward earned after the work is done. This inverts the actual dependency: stability is a precondition for the work to run at rated capacity, not downstream of it.

## Detection and sequencing (re-screen, 2026-07-15)

**Detection:** does resolving this specific friction free up bandwidth that shows up as increased output on unrelated tasks within days, not months? If yes, it was a capability floor. If resolution produces only local relief with nothing showing up elsewhere, it was ordinary friction.

**Sequencing when multiple floors are down:** stabilize in order of which floor threatens survival soonest (physical safety before housing before social-continuity), not in order of ease or emotional urgency — a floor fixed out of sequence can still collapse from an unaddressed floor beneath it, wasting the restored bandwidth on the next collapse.

## Edges

- The boundary between survival friction and ordinary discomfort is not fixed — individually calibrated, shifting with context, history, and nervous system state. The operative test (does this consume bandwidth otherwise available for actual work?) must remain rigorous or the logic inverts into rationalization for avoiding productive friction.
- Unresolved tension: some people demonstrate high output under severe survival friction — either falsifying the pattern or revealing the mechanism is more variable, possibly mediated by the degree to which the friction has been normalized or dissociated. May be most reliable for people whose nervous systems have not yet adapted to the chronic load.
- Points toward but does not reach a theory of the minimum viable stability floor — what precise conditions must be resolved before upstream capability is genuinely restored, versus which conditions produce no further gain once stabilized. Housing alone may not close the loop if safety, food, or relational continuity remain open. The floor may have multiple load-bearing points, not one.
