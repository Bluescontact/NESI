## Transferable form

When someone declines to hold a boundary that is theirs, the cost of that boundary does not disappear — it transfers by default to whoever stands nearest, with no handover ever occurring. The same person may hold other boundaries firmly (assets, property), which proves capacity: the boundary of the self is drawn selectively, closed for value, open for cost. Because nothing was ever explicitly delegated, the people absorbing the enforcement labor hold it invisibly, and no ledger exists to show the transfer happened.

## Mechanism

Two verified load-bearing elements, and only two.

**First: the transfer runs on default, not delegation.** No assignment event occurs; whatever the boundary-owner fails to enforce becomes ambient risk, and proximity substitutes for assignment — the nearest body absorbs the enforcement labor automatically. This is what distinguishes the pattern from `cost_externalized_through_delegation`: there is no act to point at, which is also why the transfer is invisible. Nothing was handed over, so nothing can be handed back.

**Second: the two-ledger asymmetry.** One boundary-drawing operation with opposite polarity — material claims (property) stay inside the self, affective claims (the wound) are pushed outside it. The wound functions as a jurisdiction-inverting token: responsibility for it is socialized while responsibility for assets is retained individually. The property proves the capacity to hold a boundary; the selective polarity explains why this one is not held. It is not incapacity — it is a boundary closed for value and open for cost.

**Habitat:** the pattern is visible only at the seam where two ledgers meet — where the wound is booked to the commons while the property stays private. Anywhere accounting is single-ledger (all shared, or all private), the asymmetry cannot be seen.

**Diagnosis is observer-entangled:** the invisibility that constitutes the pattern also blocks its detection — the only evidence of the circle's holding is the accounting the circle never kept. Naming the pattern retroactively creates the ledger, and the ledger itself changes the arrangement. The pattern cannot be verified without performing part of its remedy.

## Standing-rule mechanism (re-screen, 2026-07-15)

The two-ledger asymmetry stands alone (not a delegation special case) when it can be located prior to any transfer event: does the person's boundary-drawing rule already differentiate material claims from affective claims, independent of whether a transfer has ever happened? Test: what happens if a threat arises to property alone (defended) versus a boundary violation to selfhood alone (undefended) — if the split holds even absent any transfer, the asymmetry is a standing rule about which domain gets protected, not an after-the-fact artifact of delegation's cost-dumping. This makes "closed for value, open for cost" a testable personality-level discriminator rather than a description of one incident.

## Failure modes

1. **Mutual-aid false positive** — a circle voluntarily absorbing someone's load in acute crisis is structurally identical from the outside — same labor, same invisibility. The pattern exists only if the transfer is chronic and unrenewed; a snapshot can never confirm it. Time-axis criterion required: was it named, was it re-consented, did it become default? Without it the pattern misfires on every legitimate care arrangement.
2. **Circle complicity** — the one-way-transfer assumption breaks where circles pre-hold boundaries to purchase harmony or caretaker identity; the cost may be volunteered before it is externalized. At that edge the pattern inverts: the circle is externalizing its conflict-avoidance cost onto the boundary-owner's development. Boundary condition: would the circle actually stop holding if permitted to? If not, remedies aimed at the owner alone fail.

## Register placement

In the gift grammar this is the shadow twin of the handleless gift — complete-on-giving inverted into extraction complete on wounding. It likewise leaves no handle: nothing the circle can grasp, return, or invoice. The circle's holding is a gift that was never given, only taken.

## Edges

- Whether this is a distinct mechanism at all, or only the dark precursor state of the consented-ledger pattern — it cannot be verified without creating the ledger that remedies it.
- The gift typology may need a fourth quadrant for involuntary outflow: extraction wearing the topology of a gift; none of the neighboring patterns inhabit that space.
- The time-axis criterion (chronic, unrenewed, become-default) is named but not operationalized — no threshold says when care becomes externalization.
- Circle complicity remains an open inversion: if the holding is partly self-serving, the pattern has two authors and no current form assigns the split.
- The mechanism layer stays underdetermined — how the transfer actually runs in a given circle is unverified; only that it runs by default, and that the ledgers split, has floor.
