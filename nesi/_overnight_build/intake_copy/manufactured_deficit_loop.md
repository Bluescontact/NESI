# Manufactured Deficit Loop

---

## The mechanism

A responsibility is delegated in deliberately undefined terms, ensuring it cannot be fulfilled — and its chronic failure is the mechanism by which the delegator maintains positional necessity. The vagueness is not accidental; it is the load-bearing member. Any structure in which "no one can hold this" is quietly the point, not the problem, runs this loop.

Four elements interlock:

**Intentional underdefinition.** The boundary, task, or standard is passed downward without the specification required to satisfy it. Not ambiguous through oversight — ambiguous by design, because definition would create a testable threshold.

**The unpayable debt.** A defined boundary, once met, obligates the delegator to acknowledge sufficiency. That acknowledgment is a form of payment — transfer of credit, cession of indispensability. Keeping the boundary undefined keeps the debt permanently in arrears. No performance can discharge what was never specified.

**The proof circuit.** The loop needs the failure to close. The delegator does not discover the deficit; the delegator harvests it. Each failure replenishes the claim "only I can hold this." The failure is not the cost of the arrangement; it is the yield. Remove the failure and the delegator loses their structural position.

**Structural invisibility.** Because the mechanism runs on absence — undefined terms, unspoken thresholds — it is almost impossible to name from inside it. Recipients typically attribute failure to their own inadequacy, which is exactly the attribution the loop depends on. The loop is self-concealing by the same mechanism that makes it self-renewing.

---

## How the loop breaks

The loop collapses if a recipient names the underdefinition aloud and demands specification — this forces the delegator to either define (closing the escape) or visibly refuse (exposing the structure). It also collapses if a third-party observer maps the pattern, removing the private character of the proof.

Escalating specificity from below is the structural counter-move.

---

## Variants

The loop runs in institutions (performance standards that shift on contact), in relationships (emotional needs that cannot be named clearly enough to meet), and in political structures (compliance requirements calibrated past the point of achievability). In each case the diagnostic is: who benefits positionally from the ongoing failure?

---

## Edges

The pattern assumes a single-direction power gradient — delegator above, recipient below. It is less clear how the loop runs in lateral or peer structures, where positional necessity is maintained differently.

The question of intent is no longer the blocker it was. Structural analysis still does not require reading intent — but the counter-move no longer waits on it, because the loop's own break condition doubles as the discriminator. Demand specification and read the response: a delegator merely *attached* to indispensability can supply a definition once it is demanded (they define, the escape closes — reasoned with); a *strategic* one cannot define without surrendering the position, so the demand is re-routed — reframed, deferred, answered with a retroactive standard — which is [[commission_grammar_screen]]'s conscription tell. The evidence is not the intent but whether the daylight demand is answered or re-routed ([[the_daylight_test]], receiver chair). What remains genuinely open is the deeper question below.

The deeper open question is what the delegator is actually holding: if the loop closes on "only I can hold this," what is the delegated responsibility actually protecting them from knowing? The loop may be not only a power structure but an anxiety-management structure — the underdefinition prevents a test that the delegator themselves fears. That reading points toward psychological substrate the current articulation does not reach.
