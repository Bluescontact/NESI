# extend_dont_invent_name_the_wall

**The pattern in one line.** To close a gap, extend an already-authorized mechanism across the place where coverage goes dark — it inherits the standing, funding, and durability of what it rides — and explicitly decline to invent the part that would need authority you don't hold, naming that refusal as design, because the load-bearing hazard is not under-reaching but pitching a claim at a false height: floor painted where no structure was built, which the next party falls through.

*Extends [[register_audit]] on the authority axis. register_audit treats altitude as **stylistic-structural surface** — a surface in the wrong register "reads as the wrong *thing*," and its drift-cascade catches claims pitched wrong as voice/performance slip. This carries the same word — height, altitude, pitch — into a register the audit never touches: **authority-altitude as the load-bearing hazard**, with a downstream-victim mechanism the audit has no concept of (the false floor the next party falls through). The genuinely-new claim no canon file holds: a claim's apparent height can sit below its true height, and the gap externalizes onto whoever steps on it next. Developed 2026-06-24; the two halves fold into one design grammar and the false-height detector (below) is the addition that makes it runnable.*

*Not [[the_silent_close]] — adjacent and slightly in tension. Silent-close says do **not** narrate the limit; this says name the wall **explicitly**, because the unnamed wall is the hazard. The audit (2026-06-23) resolves the tension with a forward-vs-backward discriminator: name the **wall** — what you decline and what authority it would need to enact — never the **why-you-stopped**. Naming forward sets honest height; narrating backward is labor-dressed-as-restraint and reopens the close. Not [[catalysis_without_claim]] — CWC's axis is who-pays / who-gets-credit / preserve the institution's deniability so it changes without admitting it was moved. This axis is what-survives-because-it-didn't-require-new-authorization. Not [[consented_ledger]] — it forbids externalizing **cost** across bodies and resources; this forbids externalizing an **authority-gap** onto the next party. The shape rhymes (never push the unfelt thing downstream where it accrues unfelt) but the resource is authorization/standing, not cost. A later development could argue this as an inheritor of "never let abundance hide which is which" (here: never let polish hide that you are claiming authority you do not hold) — a development, not a fold.*

## The mechanism — two halves, one design grammar

A gap has two candidate closures and they are not symmetric.

**Invention** stands up a new actor — an office, authority, intake, or mandate that did not previously exist. It inherits nothing. It must acquire standing (who recognizes it), funding (what pays for it), and durability (what keeps it alive past the founding attention) from zero, against the prevailing grain, each acquisition a separate fight.

**Extension** takes a mechanism that is already authorized, funded, and running, and carries its *form* across the dark stretch where coverage stops. It inherits all three from what it rides, because it is the same mechanism pointed at a new target. Cheaper at construction and — the load-bearing part — durable: durability is borrowed, not built.

**The constructive half: ride what is already real.** The structural precondition is that the mechanism's *form* must already be proven somewhere, even if never aimed here. The move is *point a tool that already works at a target it has never been aimed at* — the proof of concept is the tool's existing operation in an adjacent domain, and the only novel claim is the re-aiming. This makes it an analogy of **mechanism** (the form of a flowdown condition, the form of a named-authority pre-work sign-off) and not an analogy of **content** (the substance it currently carries). The form travels; the content is local. Confusing the two is how the move gets misread as either trivial ("you're just copying X") or illegitimate ("X has nothing to do with this").

**The disciplined half: decline to invent, and name the decline.** Most of a gap closes by extension; some residual cannot, because closing it would in fact require authority that does not exist or runs against the prevailing grain. The discipline is to decline that part **explicitly** and name the decline as part of the design. This is counter to the reflex that treats a design's value as proportional to its reach — name more, claim more, build more. The reflex is wrong here. Naming the wall — stating precisely what you will not build and what authority it would need — is the operation that sets the claim at the correct height. The wall is not a gap in the design; it is the design's truthful edge, and stating it is what makes the rest of the claim trustworthy enough to stand on.

## "Height" is the load-bearing variable

A claim has a **true height**: the level of authority it would actually require to be enacted. It also has an **apparent height**: the level it reads as requiring.

- When they match, the next party who steps onto the claim lands on real floor — they know what authority they need to bring.
- When apparent height sits below true height, the claim **appears already-available** while in fact needing new authority. This is the specific failure. Not that the claim reaches too short — that it reaches into territory requiring new authority while presenting as though it rides existing authority. The next party steps on expecting solid floor and falls through, because the floor was painted at a height where no structure was built.

The cost of false height lands not on the author but on whoever relies on the claim downstream — the same externalization shape as a load-bearing element drawn at a height it cannot actually support. (This is the discipline [[domain_term_names_stance]] already gestures at from its own trade: *a floor set wrong is a hazard the next person falls through* — there as craft constraint, here as the general law.)

So the two halves are one grammar. Extension says: ride what is already real, inherit its standing. Refusal says: where you cannot ride something real, say so out loud and stop — do not paint floor at a height you have not built to. Naming the wall is what keeps apparent height honest to true height.

## How you'd know it's operating / how to run it

**The extension/invention test** — separates legitimate extension from disguised invention:
> Can the mechanism's *form* be enacted at the new target using only authority that already exists and already applies to this domain? If yes — extension. If the re-aiming requires standing up a recognizer, funder, or mandate not already present, the part that requires it is invention, and the discipline is to name it as the wall, not launder it as extension.

**The false-height detector** — run on each load-bearing element of the claim:
> What authority does this element actually require to be enacted, and does that authority currently exist and apply here? Any element where the honest answer is "it would need new authority" but the claim reads as "already available" is a false-height segment.

**The next party is the falsifier.** Trace who steps onto this claim next and what they assume the floor is. If a downstream party would proceed expecting authority that isn't there — bringing none because the claim read as already-covered — the height was false and the floor is unbuilt under them. A claim is only as honest as the floor it leaves for whoever stands on it next.

## Failure modes

1. **Invention wearing extension's costume.** The line between extend and invent is contestable, and an aggressive extension can smuggle in new authority while calling itself composition. The form is stretched until it is functionally a new actor, but the rhetoric of "we're just extending what already works" suppresses the recognition that new authority is being claimed. This is the dominant failure, because it is the move's own success-language turned into camouflage. The discipline: flag the seam honestly — mark which parts are genuine extension (riding proven, authorized form) and which would be invention (requiring authority that does not exist). The move fails the moment "extend" becomes a costume for "invent."

2. **False-height claim — the floor that isn't there.** The central hazard. Pitching a claim at apparent height below true height. Detected by the false-height detector above; falsified by the next party. A performed opacity that reads as a defined criterion is one instance of this (see [[gate_as_f4]]: opacity narrated as definition passes the stated-edge check while painting floor that isn't there — a false-height segment in veto grammar).

3. **Over-refusal / under-reaching.** Declining to extend where extension is in fact legitimate, out of excessive caution about the seam. The refusal half misapplied as general timidity rather than precise naming of the specific wall. The corrective: the wall must be **specific** — "this part needs authority X, which does not exist" — not a vague reluctance. A named wall is a coordinate; a diffuse one is just absence.

## Edges (remaining open)

- **The seam is contestable and has no neutral arbiter.** The pattern does not dissolve the extend/invent contest — it only demands the seam be flagged honestly. But "honestly" is judged by whom? The author marks the seam; the receiver decides whether the mark is faithful. A sufficiently motivated author can believe their own costume — invention can feel like extension from inside, especially when the form-analogy is strong. The pattern manages the author's discipline; it cannot guarantee the receiver's verdict, and the two can diverge. Same author-controls-the-showing / receiver-controls-the-reading split that limits the cost-signature move.
- **Height assumes a legible authority gradient.** In well-mapped domains (statute, contract, regulation) true height is a real, checkable coordinate. Where authority itself is contested or emergent, "true height" is not fixed but itself a claim, and the false-height test loses its footing — you cannot measure apparent height against a true height no one agrees on. The pattern is sharpest exactly where authority is already mapped, which may be where gaps are least common.
- **Inheriting standing inherits blind spots.** The move privileges survival-by-inheritance, but riding a mechanism inherits its constraints, its blind spots, and its politics — including whatever made the host fail to cover the gap in the first place. When is a gap a signal that the host is the *wrong vehicle* — the dark stretch dark for a structural reason — rather than merely unaimed? The pattern assumes an aiming failure; it gives no test to distinguish "never aimed here" from "structurally cannot reach here." That distinction is the unbuilt floor under the whole move.
- **A named wall is also a published map of where the structure stops** — exactly the information an adversary needs to route around or exploit the boundary. The refusal that protects the next good-faith party also instructs the next bad-faith one. The pattern treats naming-the-wall as unambiguously load-bearing; it does not address when the wall should be named *to whom*, or whether some walls are better held than published.
- **Honesty is also incompleteness.** When honest extension closes only part of the gap and the residual genuinely needs new authority, naming that residual as wall is correct — and then the gap stays open. The pattern can leave a truthfully-named gap that no extension reaches, and it offers nothing about who, if anyone, is then authorized to invent.

**Why a section, not a new file:** naming-the-wall operates in doctrine and design conversation; this enacts it inside the execution path — the declaration and the would-be deception happen at the same site, in the same invocation, and the stub doubles as an executable spec. A point-of-use register the parent pattern does not inhabit; same genus.

**Transferable form:** When part of a system is not yet built, install a stub that states, at the point of invocation and in the register the fake would have occupied, exactly what the real operation would do — then does nothing. The gap carries its own spec in the execution path, so it cannot be routed around silently or drift from its description. This holds only mid-build, and only where a wrong output would cost more than no output.

**Mechanism:** co-location of intent and absence. The stub is the spec relocated into the execution path: it declares the real operation's behavior at the exact site where that behavior will eventually live, then performs none of it. Description and future behavior share an address, so the gap cannot drift from its own account of itself. This separates it from TODO comments and design docs — the un-built capability announces itself at runtime, at the moment a simulated capability would have deceived. A stub that fails to describe its real behavior is a weaker artifact of a different class.

**Failure modes (structural):**
- *Painted floor* — an eloquent stub becomes simulated capability at one remove: the description of the operation gets mistaken for having designed it, and the gate relaxes because the plan sounds finished. The pattern eats itself here; this boundary with the parent is undefended unless named.
- *Stub rot* — a stub with no named un-gating condition has no expiry: honesty launders permanent absence into apparent architecture. A stub without a conversion condition is not architecture; it is deferral with good posture.
- *Receiver-relativity* — loudness exists only at the layer where the stub announces itself; one composition level up, its output-shaped silence is indistinguishable from a real operation that found nothing. The confession must pass through the interface, not just sit in the source.
- *Outer edge* — where a crude, wrong-sometimes implementation would teach more than a perfect stub, the stub starves the feedback loop. Stub-over-simulation is correct only where wrong-output cost exceeds no-output cost.

**Habitat:** the half-built bench — one real floor, many planned rooms. In finished software the pattern disappears; in pure vaporware it cannot exist. It governs the long middle of a build, at runtime rather than in documentation, so it survives the reader who never reads the spec.

**Open edges (carried honestly):** no general gate-clock mechanism for stub expiry/conversion · no measurement procedure for the wrong-output/no-output cost threshold · interface-level loudness named but unverified · no detector for the painted-floor mode beyond a reader's suspicion.
