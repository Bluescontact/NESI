## Transferable Form

When a system assigns need as personal characteristic rather than receiving it as diagnostic information, it misclassifies the data and loses the reporter simultaneously. The person who has fallen through a gap carries a precise map of that gap — a credential no one who hasn't fallen through it holds. Separating the person from the signal degrades both the intelligence and the person's standing in the room where repair decisions get made.

## Development

The mechanism turns on where need gets located. In the individual-identity frame, need is a property of the person — something they have, something that marks them. In the infrastructure-signal frame, need is a property of the system — something the system is producing, something the person is reporting. The two frames are not different interpretations of the same situation. They produce different information flows, different authority assignments, and different repair architectures.

When need is located in the person, the response is management: stabilize the individual, reduce the symptom, return them to functional status. The gap that produced the need is not the problem the system addresses. The system addresses the person. This is not malice — it is the logical consequence of where need has been assigned.

When need is located in the system, the response is diagnosis: what is the gap, where is it, what conditions produce it, who else is at risk of falling through it. The person who fell through is now the primary source. They have direct experiential data about gap geometry — where the floor gives, how fast, what the fall feels like, what the landing surface is. No one who has not fallen through that gap has that data at that resolution.

The failure mode of the individual-identity frame is compounded: the system loses the most credentialed witness at the exact moment it most needs that witness. The person who carries the most precise map of the gap is assigned liability status and excluded from the repair conversation. The gap remains unmapped by anyone with direct credential. The next person falls through it.

The standing inversion is structural, not rhetorical. It is not that the person "deserves respect" — that framing reinstates the individual-identity frame with a moral overlay. The structural claim is simpler: the person who has fallen through the floor has information the system needs, and the system cannot get that information from any other source at the same resolution. Their standing is not granted; it is produced by the gap itself.

Capacity and need arriving together is not coincidence or consolation. It is the mechanism. The gap produces both the wound and the witness. Any system that separates them on intake — routing need to services and capacity to contribution — has structurally severed the intelligence circuit. It now runs two degraded loops where one functional loop ran before.

## Failure mode under scale

As systems professionalize, gap-diagnosis is delegated to specialists who study gaps without falling through them. The specialist has methodology; the person has the map. These are not equivalent. The specialist can model the floor; the person knows the specific location where it gives. Professionalization that excludes the fell-through as non-credentialed is precisely the mechanism that degrades the repair signal as systems grow.

## Open edges

- The pattern assumes the community has a functional reception apparatus — that there is a circuit to close, a repair process that can receive the signal. Many actual systems have no such apparatus. What is the structural move when the diagnostic infrastructure itself is absent?
- The person who has fallen through the gap is also in the gap — the moment of maximum credential is often the moment of minimum capacity to translate that credential into legible input. A system that requires the person to have stabilized before their testimony is received has already missed the sharpest signal.
- The pattern holds cleanly for gaps that are local and addressable. It strains when the gap is systemic and the repair is outside the community's control — when the floor is made of policy, economy, or climate. Does the pattern hold when "receiving the signal" produces no repair pathway?
- The credential claim is strongest when the gap is specific and the witness is singular. It weakens when many people have fallen through the same gap simultaneously — at scale, the individual credential diffuses into aggregate data, and the diagnostic function shifts to institutions rather than persons. The pattern may describe pre-scale community more precisely than it describes post-scale systems.
