# Symmetrical Blindness From Asymmetrical Visibility

---

## The mechanism

Two parties each carry a real cost that is vivid to themselves and invisible to the other; each, reasoning honestly from their own half of the data, independently concludes they are the one bearing more. Neither is lying and neither is wrong about their own experience — the belief in being the net carrier is stable regardless of whether the underlying costs are actually equal, because it is driven by visibility, not magnitude.

Mechanism, precisely: two-party structure, each holds complete, honest, partial data — own cost is vivid, the other's cost is invisible. Both independently run the same inference ("my cost is what I can see; I see mine, not theirs; therefore I carry more") and both land on "I am the net carrier." The conclusions are opposite in direction but identical in form — this formal identity is the pattern's diagnostic signature, not a coincidence of the instance.

Test: ask both parties independently who carries more. Both saying "me" marks this pattern. Both saying "them," or only one being wrong, marks a different mechanism entirely — the blindness has to be mutual and the conclusions mirror images for this pattern to apply.

---

## Belief decoupled from ground truth

The pattern's stability does not depend on real cost asymmetry existing. Vividness — not magnitude — drives the belief. Costs could be objectively equal, or objectively unequal in either direction, and the phenomenology (each feels like the net carrier) stays the same in the equal case and can persist even in the unequal case. This means the pattern is fundamentally about a visibility architecture, not a resource-distribution fact. Any remedy aimed at "redistributing the real cost" is misaimed if the costs are already balanced — the actual intervention surface is the visibility/attention regime, not the ledger.

The situation cannot be resolved from inside it, because the missing data is exactly what the norm suppresses. It is not that the parties fail to communicate incidentally; the informational suppression is structural — a designed or default feature (quiet-giving) of the relationship, not an accident. Fix is therefore a regime change (alter what the norm makes visible) rather than a fact-check (determine who is more right) — determining who is right is a category error, since both are right from where they stand.

---

## Two failure modes

**False positive — concealment wearing this pattern's clothes.** The diagnostic requires that if each party were handed the other's missing half, they would update in good faith. If either party would instead say "I knew, I just didn't say," the situation is ordinary concealment or extraction, not symmetrical blindness. "Neither is lying" is load-bearing — it is what makes the pattern structural and forgivable rather than a trust violation, and any application that fails this counterfactual test is misdiagnosis.

**Symmetry without a commensurability check.** The pattern only verifies that visibility is mutual (each is blind to the other), never that the costs being misjudged are actually comparable in kind or magnitude. A structurally advantaged party can invoke "we're both blind, it's just the norm" to flatten a real, objective asymmetry into an apparent symmetric one. The felt mutuality of invisibility can co-exist with a real, non-mutual imbalance underneath — the pattern as stated has no built-in check for this, which is exactly where power differences hide inside apparent structural fairness.

---

## Edges

What the norm-level fix (regime change) actually looks like in practice is unspecified — the target is named (alter the information regime) but not the mechanism (ritual disclosure? third-party witnessing? periodic audit?) — any of those is an addition, not yet crossed.

Unresolved tension between two live readings: the belief may be a pure perceptual artifact with zero real asymmetry underneath, or costs may be genuinely, objectively unequal underneath the felt symmetry. The pattern holds both possibilities open simultaneously and does not adjudicate which is live in a given case — a user must run both the good-faith-update test and a separate commensurability check, with no guidance for what to do when the two disagree.

Whether repetition or duration is required is also unresolved — a single instance of mutual partial-data honest misjudgment and a sustained norm are treated as the same thing here, and that compression may not survive contact with a worked example.
