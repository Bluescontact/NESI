## Transferable Form

When a structural conflict is transmitted as diagnostic information rather than adversarial claim, its doctrinal status inside the institution shifts from grievance to data — and the institution's subsequent actions become operations-with-knowledge rather than operations-without. The form of the offering determines whether the recipient can move toward correction without the cost of conceding wrongdoing. Grievance demands; diagnosis invites; only one of those leaves the institution's self-correction mechanism intact.

## Development

The mechanism operates on doctrinal status, not emotional register. Before transmission, the institution operates under the conflicted architecture without formal knowledge of the conflict — that operational state is structurally distinct from operating with knowledge. Transmission through any documentable channel closes that gap and reclassifies every subsequent action. This reclassification is the core move; it does not depend on the institution's response.

The diagnostic form accomplishes three structural functions simultaneously:

1. It removes the face-cost of correction: reform under demand is humiliation because it concedes the adversarial framing; reform under invitation is initiative because it locates the corrective impulse inside the institution.
2. It preserves the institution's internal self-correction pathway — the very pathway that grievance tends to route around or shut down.
3. It maintains the transmitter's standing as information-source rather than opponent, which is the posture that survives escalation best.

The operative language requirement is structural, not rhetorical. The conflict must be presented in the institution's own terms — the contract clause, the regulation, the operational situation — so the institution cannot reclassify the transmission as opinion or complaint. Specificity in the institution's own doctrinal vocabulary is what locks in the status shift.

There is also a timing asymmetry. The doctrinal shift (operation-without-knowledge → operation-with-knowledge) is irreversible and immediate upon transmission. The institutional behavioral response, if any, is neither immediate nor guaranteed. These two clocks run on different mechanisms and should not be conflated.

## The standalone practitioner instrument (resolved at re-screen 2026-07-15)

Recipient-classification as a one-page practitioner tool:

1. Name the conflict in the institution's own operational vocabulary — a clause, a regulation, a stated procedure — never in emotional or adversarial language.
2. Send it through the most boring, procedural channel available (an incident log, a compliance form, a request for clarification), never through a conversation, complaint line, or grievance process.
3. Test afterward by asking what the institution now knows that it didn't before — if the answer is "a fact," diagnosis held; if the answer is "that I am upset" or "that I want something," grievance leaked through despite the intent.

This instrument is concrete enough to run without restating CWC's geometry verbatim — a checklist a practitioner could actually use, which is what earned it standing as a register-specific extension rather than folding back into [[catalysis_without_claim]].

## Failure modes

- The diagnostic form fails when the institution has no functional self-correction mechanism, because the invitation has no receiver.
- It fails when the transmitter's operational situation is so acute that waiting for institutional self-correction carries costs the transmitter cannot sustain.
- It fails when the institution reads the diagnostic form as a soft grievance and responds with token acknowledgment rather than structural review.
- The form does not compel action — it only changes the epistemic and doctrinal status of inaction. The transmitter who has diagnosed correctly and documented the transmission has changed the institutional record; they have not changed the institution.

## Open edges

- The pattern assumes a distinction between doctrinal status and practical leverage that may collapse in institutions without functioning accountability structures — diagnosis without a receiver is still just a letter in a file.
- The face-cost argument is psychologically robust but culturally variable; institutions with high shame-cultures may experience even invitation as implicit accusation.
- What happens when the transmitter needs both the doctrinal record and the institutional relationship to survive? The pattern maps the choice but does not resolve the cost when both are simultaneously at risk.
- There is an open question about what makes a channel "documentable enough" — the pattern implies a threshold but does not name it.
- The deepest unresolved tension: the diagnostic form locates corrective agency inside the institution, which is structurally correct and interpersonally expensive; the transmitter must genuinely relinquish the adversarial posture, not merely perform it, for the form to hold — and that relinquishment has a body cost the pattern does not yet account for.
