## Transferable form

An infrastructure built in lived refusal of a dominant form accumulates implicit exclusions across every instrument — each tool draws a contour against the unnamed shape without declaring it. The system remains a field rather than a form until a naming act renders the shadow explicit, at which point the instruments retroactively cohere: they were always organized around the void, not around each other. Filling the void — incorporating the refused form — would not complete the system but collapse it.

## Mechanism

The mechanism is archaeological, not legislative. The exclusion pre-exists the naming; the naming does not install a refusal but reveals one already structurally present. This distinguishes the pattern from prospective naming moves (`extend_dont_invent_name_the_wall`), where the boundary is installed by the act. Here, the act is retrospective recognition — raising the unresolved question of whether post-hoc naming carries the same structural force as prospective declaration.

The void is constitutive, not a gap awaiting repair. Every attempt to complete the infrastructure by adding the refused form's features is self-destruction, not improvement. This separates the pattern from missing-field diagnostics, which treat absence as a problem.

The naming act operates in a perceptual register, not an engineering or accounting one. It does not change the instruments; it changes what the practitioner can see. The trigger condition is the moment the infrastructure risks absorption into its field — when it begins to look like just another instance of the dominant form. The naming is a perceptual rescue operation, not a development milestone, and its timing is not arbitrary.

## Failure modes

1. **Naming collapses the shadow** — when the negative space is made fully explicit, instruments no longer define against it implicitly; the shadow becomes a labeled object and loses its generative function. Partial naming may be the operative mechanism, not full articulation.
2. **Wrong referent, plausible fit** — a practitioner names a negative space that fits the shadow's outline without being the load-bearing exclusion. The naming feels complete and produces no friction, which is the false-positive condition. Without a test distinguishing the actual constitutive negative from a plausible substitute, the pattern has no falsifier.
3. **Positional requirement** — the naming move requires a stable outside position from which the void is perceivable. When the practitioner is fully inside the infrastructure, the refused form is invisible not because it is unnamed but because it is the condition of the practitioner's own perception. Who can perform the naming is not incidental — it is the boundary condition of the pattern's applicability.

## Edges

- Whether post-hoc naming carries the same structural force as prospective naming is unresolved — resolving it determines whether this is an independent pattern or a subcategory of prospective wall-naming.
- Whether partial naming is more generative than full articulation: the shadow may need to remain partially unnamed to stay functional, which inverts the usual legibility assumption.
- Who can perform the naming — the positional requirement is silent in most infrastructure-building contexts, but the pattern only works from partial outside positions.
- What the practitioner is actually doing when they name correctly versus plausibly-but-wrongly: the absence of a falsifier is not a gap to fill with inference but a live limit that keeps the pattern honest.
