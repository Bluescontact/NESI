## Transferable form

Becoming a member of a living community is not a status granted by the container but a threshold crossed by the person: the moment some part of your survival is structurally carried by the group AND some part of the group's functioning is structurally carried by you. Dependence alone — however deep — never crosses; tenants, employees, and charity recipients depend without belonging. The crossing is bidirectional load transfer, and its concrete gesture is the first real ask that cannot be repaid in kind.

## Mechanism

The threshold is load transfer, not feeling or declaration. It flips when a bidirectional load-path splice forms: some function of the person's survival routes through the mesh, and some function of the mesh routes through the person. The moving part is where load lands when either side is removed — falsifiable by a removal test: pull the person out and see whether anything in the village fails; pull the village out and see whether anything in the person's life fails. Two nulls = no membership, regardless of sentiment. This distinguishes the crossing from `bilateral_route_formation`, which requires only a working dyad channel, not survival load.

The container's retention test (`village_container_vs_system_container`) and the person's crossing are the same mechanical event read from opposite faces: the village certifies membership when it starts routing load through the person; the person crosses when they start letting load route through the village.

**Register.** Every existing membership pattern in the library locates the crossing in the container — its legibility demands, coercions, routes. This one locates it in the member's voluntary act of accepting dependence: membership as something you do to yourself. Coupled to its source habitat (ask-as-infrastructure), the concrete crossing gesture is the first real ask: the public request that cannot be repaid in kind. The ask is not preparation for membership; it is the membership rite itself.

**Shipping condition 1 — the coercive_care discriminator.** The identical act — surrendering self-sufficiency — reads as price in the coercive_care register and as rite in the village register. The discriminator is structural, not felt: whether the container can also receive from the one who depends. Bidirectionality of dependence, not the feeling of the surrenderer, decides which register you are in.

**Shipping condition 2 — the unilateral-closure screen.** Unilateral closure never crosses: gifts complete on giving discharge cleanly and build zero interdependence — a person could gift into a village forever and remain a stranger. The threshold requires open-handled, incomplete circuits; unresolved obligation is the load-bearing material.

## Failure modes

1. **Dependence without crossing.** Someone can be deeply dependent on a village — rent, food, care — and never become a member; the reciprocal half can be permanently withheld by the container. If the threshold can be gated from the village side, "process of becoming" is exposure without guarantee.
2. **Simulated crossing.** A person can perform interdependence — visible asks, visible gifts, calibrated vulnerability — while keeping a private exit fully funded. The threshold as stated has no irreversibility clause; a reversible crossing is indistinguishable from a real one until the exit is used.

## Boundary

A dyad of mutual dependence is a marriage or partnership, not a village. The threshold plausibly exists only where dependence is distributed across enough nodes that no single defection ejects you; below some node count, "crossing into interdependence" is `bilateral_route_formation` renamed and inherits all its fragility. The boundary condition is a minimum topology, not a minimum feeling.
