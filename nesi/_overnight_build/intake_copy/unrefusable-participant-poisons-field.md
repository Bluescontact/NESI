## Transferable form

A single participant who is known to be unable to decline degrades the trust-value of every consent in the shared field, because their presence is standing proof that agreement here can be extracted rather than freely given. The damage is not contained to the coerced party; it leaks outward, because once one yes is known to be unfree, no other yes in the field can be read as clean. Consent legibility is a network property, not an individual one — it can only be as high as the weakest no.

## Mechanism

The mechanism runs through signal, not ethics. A yes carries information only against the live possibility of no. When refusal is structurally unavailable for even one participant, the field now contains a proof-of-concept that yeses here can be produced without consent. An observer reading any yes in the field must price in that possibility. The signal value of every yes drops, because the population of yeses now includes at least one known-extracted instance, and the observer cannot always tell which.

This is why the externality is bidirectional and operates at the field level rather than the dyad level. The coerced participant is harmed directly. But the uncoerced participants are harmed too — their freely-given yeses are devalued by association, sharing a signal-space with an extracted one. Legibility is set by the floor, not the average. One unfree participant caps the maximum trust any yes in the field can carry.

The poison spreads through inference, not contact. The coerced party need not interact with anyone; their mere known presence in the field is sufficient. Anyone modeling the field reasons: "if that yes can be extracted, the conditions for extraction exist here, so any yes might be." The contamination is epistemic — it lives in what observers can no longer rule out.

## Failure modes

- **Toleration-as-amenity.** Keeping the unrefusable participant because they are useful, while believing the cost is borne only by them — it is borne by the whole field's signal value, silently.
- **Invisibility of the floor.** The field measures its health by the average yes, and never sees that legibility is floor-bound.
- **The exit cannot certify itself.** Removing the unrefusable participant only restores legibility if the removal is itself visible and costless to the one removed — otherwise the exit becomes new evidence of extraction.
- **Counterfeit nos.** A field can stage visible refusals to fake legibility while the real floor stays coerced.
- **Self-recruitment.** A field that benefits from one unrefusable participant has an incentive to acquire more, since the legibility cost is a commons cost no single actor pays.

## Falsifier

A field where one known-unfree yes is correctly walled off and does not degrade the others' legibility. If that case exists, the "weakest no" invariant weakens to a tendency rather than a hard network law.

## Open edges

- The pattern assumes the unrefusable participant is *known* to be unrefusable — visibility is doing load-bearing work. An equally coerced but perfectly concealed participant produces a false-high reading, worse on discovery.
- Unclear how the floor recovers: whether a departed unrefusable participant permanently marks the field, or legibility heals once enough costless refusals accumulate.
- The threshold of "unable to decline" is not crisp — real fields are graded on a refusability gradient, not binary.
- Whether the poison requires a field that *claims* freeness; a field that never claimed it may be immune.
- The witness has no witness: the pattern relies on an observer who can read the floor and price the yeses accordingly. If no one can see the unrefusable participant, the damage is real but undetectable.
