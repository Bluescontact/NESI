## Transferable form

A grammar that appears portable may be author-anchored: the author silently supplies the contrast condition that makes the pattern visible, and the grammar has never needed to carry it. Legibility is not a property of the pattern alone but of the gap between the pattern's encoding and the reader's existing schema — a gap the author generates automatically and a stranger generates differently or not at all. The structural precondition for a self-verifying grammar is that contrast must be internalized into the encoding itself, not externalized into the author's presence.

## Mechanism

A pattern becomes visible when the named element belongs to a different ontological category than what surrounds it — figure and ground must be encoded in different registers or no edge is produced. When the author is in the relay chain, they supply this category-crossing silently through tone, emphasis, and living context. Remove the author and the contrast supply goes with them.

**Structural truth:** a grammar is self-verifying only when it contains a falsifier — a condition under which the reader can conclude the pattern is not present. Without the falsifier, recognition has no floor: the reader cannot distinguish seeing the pattern from complying with an instruction that one is there.

**The blazon analogy:** pointing requires the author present to indicate direction; describing allows the reader to reconstruct the figure from structural specification alone. The two are not different patterns — they are different encodings of the same pattern, one author-dependent, one not.

## Failure modes

1. **Figure-ground frequency match.** The pattern IS the medium it is trying to stand out from.
2. **Schema-relative zero contrast.** A pattern highly differentiated from the author's prior experience may register zero differentiation against a stranger's baseline.
3. **Misidentification at full contrast.** Sufficient contrast does not guarantee the stranger sees the intended figure — they may see a different figure with equal clarity. This is a false positive the contrast-enforcement rule cannot catch.

## Falsifier

The structural element that creates or fails to create contrast is somatic register-difference. Same-session RI tincture rule: "a recognition pattern may not be named in the same somatic register as its declared ground." RI's two tincture categories are not two fixed labels but a live pairing — whatever register the ground occupies, the pattern must be named from the other one. The three tested failing combinations (scarcity named from scarcity; gift named in a transactional frame; safety named under active threat) are the same-tincture-on-tincture violations; any pattern named from a register that differs from its ground's is the cross-tincture case the rule requires.

## Open edges

- The tincture rule requires a canonical negative space — what the pattern is NOT — to produce the contrast a stranger requires. The author cannot enforce this unilaterally because they cannot fully model what the stranger's background looks like.
- The falsifier closes the loop structurally, but building it requires knowing what the stranger's ground looks like — which is exactly the knowledge the author lacks.
