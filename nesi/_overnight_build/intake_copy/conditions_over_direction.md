# Conditions over Direction

*slug: `conditions-over-direction`*
*Promoted: 2026-06-25 · Source: Schaltegger / Metachrysalis pilot, Thaumazo*
*Screen against: catalysis-without-claim, prestress, gate-as-f4*

---

## Transferable Form

When adequate conditions exist — genuine relationships, sufficient resources, a shared picture of what thriving looks like — collaborative work emerges without direction. The convener's job is to set the conditions and step back. Direction competes with emergence.

---

## Development

The management model assumes that collaborative work requires coordination: someone must direct, assign, sequence, and track. Remove the manager and the work stops.

The Metachrysalis pilot ran on the opposite premise. A community recycling hub operator whose surplus materials spontaneously enabled a peer-led van service for unhoused individuals — no one connected them, no one assigned the task, no one managed the handoff. The conditions (proximity, visible need, available resources, relational trust) made the connection obvious. The work emerged without direction because the conditions made it emerge.

This is the distinction between direction and conditions. Direction requires continuous energy input: someone must keep directing, or the work stops. Conditions, once set, are self-sustaining: the field routes work without a manager because the conditions make the routing obvious.

**The three conditions named:**

Schaltegger identifies three: adequate funding, genuine relationships, clear vision. Each is structural, not managerial.

*Adequate funding* removes the scarcity constraint that forces competition. When resources are genuinely sufficient, participants do not need to compete for them — they can route toward what the work needs.

*Genuine relationships* replace transactional ones. Transactional relationships route through the exchange apparatus (I give this, you give that, someone tracks the ledger). They require management because the exchange must be balanced. Relational trust routes through a different channel — coordination happens because participants know and care about each other's work, not because someone is balancing the accounts.

*Clear vision* is not a plan or a set of directions. It is a shared picture of what thriving looks like — an image of the desired future that everyone in the system can see and orient toward. This is `future-image-as-pull` operating at the collective level: the vision does not tell participants what to do; it gives them something to move toward. The direction emerges from the pull, not from a manager.

**The fourth condition (inferred):**

The Metachrysalis examples share a feature not named by Schaltegger: participants could see adjacent action. The recycling hub operator could see the unhoused individuals. The connection was visible before it was made. Conditions may be necessary but not sufficient if participants cannot see each other working — visibility of adjacent action may be the fourth condition that makes the routing obvious rather than requiring discovery.

**The distinction from catalysis-without-claim:**

Catalysis-without-claim: you enable a reaction and don't claim credit for the product. You are present in the process; the move is about the claim at the end.

Conditions-over-direction: you set the conditions and step back. You are not present in the process. The process runs without you — not because you refrained from claiming, but because you were never needed in the routing.

The difference is structural presence. A catalyst is in the reaction. A conditions-setter is upstream of it.

**The disruption failure mode:**

A convener who sets conditions but then continues to direct disrupts the emergence. Direction competes with the field routing. When someone is directing, participants route toward the director's model of the work rather than toward the conditions' model. The director's model is less information-rich than the conditions' model — the director sees less of the system than the conditions do. Direction is a lower-bandwidth channel than emergence.

The move that forecloses emergence is not bad intention; it is continued presence.

**Where this is already running:**

OSG v6 as trust infrastructure: the upstream boundary that reroutes extraction before it enters is a conditions move. The condition handles the protection without Kevin managing it case by case.

The Circuit Tool: structural incapacity to represent debt is a conditions move. The tool's design makes debt unrepresentable — not as a rule Kevin enforces, but as a feature of the conditions. Participants cannot extract through the tool because the tool cannot hold extraction.

THE GOVERNOR: loaded before the runaway. The condition (instrument already present and running) prevents the failure mode without Kevin directing it each time.

## The Move

Before directing, ask: what conditions would make this work emerge without direction? Set those. Step back. The test of whether the conditions are adequate: does work route without prompting, or does it wait for a push?

If work waits for a push, the conditions are not yet set — or direction has been running long enough that participants have stopped routing through the conditions and are routing through the director instead.

---

## Edges

Conditions can be met and work still does not emerge. The pattern is not a guarantee — it is an observation from one thin dataset (one pilot, two examples). The failure mode is either: the conditions were not actually adequate (hard to know in advance, since adequacy is only visible in the routing it produces), or the pattern has a scope limit (some kinds of work require direction regardless of conditions).

The sharpest open question: what work cannot be done by emergence? The pattern comes from community-scale convening; it may not transfer to technical work, to time-critical work, or to work requiring specialist knowledge not distributed through the community. Identifying the scope limit is the most useful next move.

Second tension: the convener who sets conditions is doing work that is invisible once conditions are set. The emergence looks spontaneous. This is the invisibility failure of conditions-over-direction — the work of setting conditions is not credited because the result looks like it happened on its own. Naming this keeps the pattern honest: someone set the conditions. That work is not free.

**Pre-load failure modes** (the same mechanism that produces readiness also produces these):

*Calcification* — the more complete the pre-configuration, the more it forecloses demands outside the anticipated class. The hidden prerequisite: a correct model of the demand space. Prestressed concrete fails in directions the engineer didn't load for. When unexpected demands arrive, a fully-configured system is more brittle than an unconfigured one.

*Power installation* — when one actor holds the configuration and the demand arrives from another, the pattern functions as enclosure. The conditions, questions, and future-images are set by whoever arrived first; the second party operates inside a frame they didn't choose. The pattern offers no instrument to distinguish readiness from capture. Any application to relational or political registers requires a prior account of whose configuration counts as readiness and whose counts as enclosure.

*Inversion without discharge* — a configuration that never meets its target demand does not sit neutral. It consumes resources, bends attention toward confirming the pre-set, and generates pressure to find evidence that the anticipated demand is about to arrive. This is not non-use — it is active distortion that intensifies over time.

*Unaddressable demand class* — some demands are constituted by their arrival: grief, rupture, the body's sudden signal. Pre-configuration for these produces a system absent for the demand that has come and ready for one that hasn't. The pattern belongs to demands foreseeable-in-type and variable-in-content. It cannot cross into demands where arrival is the entire event; applied there, the rigidity failure has no recovery path.

## Falsifier

Find a community with adequate funding, genuine relationships, and clear vision where work does not emerge without direction — where participants wait for assignment regardless of conditions. If found, identify what the conditions model missed. Is it visibility of adjacent action? Is it a scope limit? Is it that the vision was cognitively held but not felt? The failure case is more informative than the success.
