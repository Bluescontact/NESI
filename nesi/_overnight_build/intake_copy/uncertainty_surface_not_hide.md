## Transferable form

A system that produces finished, single-valued output can only fail two ways at an ambiguity — silently guess through it, or halt at it. This pattern names a third move: hand the unresolved fork to whoever is positioned to collapse it, keeping the circuit open pending their answer. Its maturity axis sits one level above being right: not "did the system resolve this correctly" but "does the system have any organ for noticing it is uncertain at all" — and its whole moral charge is calibration, because surfacing everything is the same failure as surfacing nothing.

## Mechanism

The machine is two organs, not one. A producer runs a probabilistic completion; a separate observer watches it from outside, because a completion cannot flag itself while completing. The load-bearing part is the coupling: the observer must have access to something the producer leaks about its own state — competing candidates, a confidence spread, a near-tie. Without specifying what crosses from producer to observer, the pattern is a wish.

Surfacing is a third response, distinct from the two the register makes obvious. Silent completion fails open — it guesses and continues. A Governor fails closed — it halts and idles, keeping control inside the system. Surfacing does neither: it forks the decision, expels it to a witness, and idles only that one branch while the rest of the circuit stays live. The two compose: surface is the Governor's release valve, not its rival. A system with a Governor but no surfacing channel can only halt; a system with surfacing routes the uncertainty to whoever can resolve it.

What crosses the membrane must be the ambiguity itself, not an alarm. A bare "I'm unsure" is nearly useless — the receiver cannot act differently than on a silent guess. The payload is the unresolved fork: the competing readings, the specific term in question, the axis of the doubt.

The pattern lives only in a specific habitat: systems whose native register is fluent, finished, single-valued — probabilistic completion, the executive summary, the polished answer. It is invisible in any system that already outputs distributions, error bars, confidence intervals, because there doubt is on the surface by construction. It presupposes a two-party habitat: a machine that recognizes doubt and a sovereign other who holds the resolving authority. Strip the recipient and it collapses into mere self-audit.

The maturity threshold sits one level up from any particular doubt — the dangerous system is not the wrong one but the one with no organ for detecting its own wrongness. The threshold is not missing — it is a feature slot: "signal to surface" has no cutoff because the cutoff is the one knob a real deployment must set, and it cannot be set in the abstract.

## Failure modes (calibration edges, kept close per the pattern's own discipline)

- **Surfacing-flood false positive** — a probabilistic system is uncertain about nearly every output to some degree; an uncalibrated instance converts every completion into a clarifying question. Not an edge case — the default behavior of the pattern applied honestly with no threshold. It inverts into its own opposite: the delegate meant to protect the user becomes a chatterer offloading all cognitive cost back onto them.
- **Uncertainty about uncertainty** — the recognition mechanism is itself an uncertain estimate sharing the producer's probabilistic substrate. A confidently-wrong completion is exactly the case where the detector reports low uncertainty. The pattern is blind precisely where it is most needed.
- **Responsibility laundering** — flagging can be liability transfer, not honesty. "I asked" becomes cover for offloading a judgment the system was better positioned to make. The framing of the clarifying question can pre-load the answer, so a surfaced fork can be a silent resolution wearing a question mark.
- **The collapsed third response** — sometimes the gift is to eat the ambiguity so the receiver never carries it. Surfacing is correct only when the human is the better-positioned resolver — a precondition the pattern never checks and cannot check from inside.

## Edges

- The threshold is the untuned dial the pattern cannot set for itself: whether a given ambiguity is worth transporting depends entirely on context, and setting it wrong in either direction is a real failure.
- The pattern cannot verify its own precondition — that the receiver is the better-positioned resolver — from inside; it must assume what it should check.
- It is blind in the region where it matters most: false confidence is indistinguishable from inability to recognize from the outside, so the catastrophic errors are exactly the ones it never surfaces.
- The boundary between honest surfacing and framed-consent-extraction runs through the wording of the question itself — the pattern goes dark precisely there.
