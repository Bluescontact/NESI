## Transferable form

A protection, resolution mechanism, or right is formally present — it appears on paper, is cited as available, absorbs complaints about its absence — but is structured such that reaching it requires a combination of resources, knowledge, persistence, or credentials that the population it nominally serves does not possess. The mechanism functions as a pressure valve: it makes the gap between offer and access invisible by converting unreachability into procedure. Anyone who fails to reach it has, by definition, failed to navigate correctly.

## Mechanism

The mechanism has two surfaces: the nominal surface (the offer, the policy, the right, the office, the process) and the operational surface (what actually occurs when someone attempts to use it). These surfaces are structurally misaligned in a way that is deniable at every individual step — each gate is reasonable in isolation; the combination is prohibitive in aggregate.

Six structural components:

1. **Complexity threshold.** The mechanism requires navigational knowledge — forms, timelines, terminology, escalation paths — distributed asymmetrically. Those with institutional familiarity, legal literacy, or organizational backing can navigate it. Those without cannot. This asymmetry is not acknowledged as a design feature; it is presented as neutral procedure.
2. **Record suppression.** The mechanism works partly by not generating records of failure. If no record is created, there is no evidence of systemic unreachability, only individual failures to navigate. The absence of record is itself the cover.
3. **Procedural exhaustion as termination.** The process contains enough steps, delays, and conditional branches that energy and persistence are the limiting resources, not eligibility. The mechanism outlasts most attempts. Each exhausted attempt disappears without trace.
4. **Absorption without resolution.** The mechanism ingests complaints, filings, or attempts — creating the appearance of due process — while producing no substantive outcome. The intake is real; the resolution pathway is not. This is the key structural move: the intake absorbs the pressure that would otherwise accumulate outside the mechanism, reducing external visibility of the gap.
5. **Plausible deniability at each node.** No individual actor in the chain needs to intend the unreachability. Each step is defensible. The pattern is emergent from the structure, not from individual bad faith.
6. **Self-immunizing framing.** Failure to reach the mechanism is categorized as procedural error by the user, not structural failure of the mechanism. The mechanism's defenders are structurally correct at every point they make.

## Falsifier

If the target population's navigational combination becomes widely distributed — through legal aid, institutional advocates, or organized collective navigation — and access genuinely opens (outcomes shift, not just attempts), the mechanism was nominal availability under a fixable complexity threshold, not a self-adjusting lock. If instead the threshold rises to compensate and unreachability persists, the mechanism is confirmed as adaptive.

## Edges

Where does nominal availability end and deliberate design begin? The pattern can emerge from accumulated procedural drift or from intentional architecture. The structural outcome is identical; the moral and legal weight is not.

The witness problem: if no records are generated, the pattern is only visible to those who attempted navigation. Their accounts are typically discredited by the same framing that makes the unreachability deniable. Who counts as a credible witness to a pattern that operates by not being witnessed?
