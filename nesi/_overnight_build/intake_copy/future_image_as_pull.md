## Transferable Form

Human systems move in the direction of their images of the future. The image is not a prediction — it is a gravitational field. A vivid, felt picture of a desired state draws present decisions toward it without requiring deliberate strategy.

---

## Development

Conventional planning runs from problem to solution: identify what's wrong, develop a fix, implement, achieve. The future is a target to aim at. The person or system does the work of reaching it.

The anticipatory principle runs the other direction. A vivid image of a desired future acts as an attractor: present decisions organize toward it, the path reveals itself in the reorganization, the system does the work. The person does not push toward the future — the future pulls.

This is not motivational language. It is a structural observation: systems orient toward what they can picture. What cannot be pictured cannot pull. What can be pictured in sufficient detail begins generating movement before any strategy is formed.

**The Practical Utopias constraint:**

Margaret Atwood's course required fictional futures to use only existing or near-future technologies and to account for human nature. The constraint was what made the images powerful. An image that requires magical technology cannot be felt — it stays conceptual. An image built on what already exists, accounting for how people actually behave, can be inhabited. A habitable image has pull; a conceptual image has none.

**The distinction that matters:**

A picture held in the mind is not pull. Pull requires the image to be somatic — felt in the body, not just held in the head. A cognitively held vision generates a goal (something to aim at deliberately). A felt image generates an attractor (something the system moves toward without being pushed). The test is effortfulness: if reaching toward the future requires constant deliberate push, the image has not landed in the body. If the system moves toward it without effort — if decisions that serve it feel obvious rather than dutiful — the image is operating as pull.

This connects to body-as-zero: the future-image has to pass through the somatic instrument to become a gravitational field. An image that the body hasn't registered is not yet pull.

**Where this is already running:**

**The default future:**

A system with no vivid image of a desired future is not imageless — it is moving toward whatever image is already present, usually the inherited one. The default future is not neutral. It is the future that the existing configuration is already generating. Not picturing where you want to go does not prevent movement; it just means you are moving toward the default.

This is why envisioning is not optional. The question is not whether you have an image of the future — you do. The question is whether it's the one you chose.

## The Move

Build the image before building the strategy. Make it felt, not just held. The test of whether the image is operating as pull: do present decisions organize toward it without effort, or does reaching toward it require constant deliberate push?

When a decision is hard — when the right move isn't clear — the question is not "what should I do?" but "which of these futures can I feel?" The felt future is the attractor. The answer is downstream of the picture.

## Edges

The image that cannot be updated becomes a trap. A future-image built under one set of conditions may pull toward a configuration that no longer fits the ground. The gravitational field persists after the conditions that generated it have changed. This is the false-CONTINUE failure mode at the attractor level: the system is still moving toward a picture that was true once.

The second tension: a vivid image of the wrong future is more dangerous than no image. It generates strong pull in a direction that damages. This is not an argument against image-building — it is an argument for the body-check: a future the body cannot inhabit is not safe to move toward, regardless of how compelling it looks on paper.

**Trap phenomenology:**

*Four named phenomena at the seam between a live and an obsolete attractor:*

**Stale Attractor No Alarm** — At the seam where conditions have genuinely shifted but the image has not yet registered the change, the pull continues — fully operational, somatically real, phenomenologically identical to a live attractor. The person is not failing to have a felt image. They have one. It is vivid, embodied, and obsolete. The gravitational field is still active, still generating movement, and the system has no internal alarm that the target has moved.

**Trap Requires No Effort** — The effortfulness test — this pattern's primary diagnostic — fails here. An obsolete image produces a genuine pull; following it is effortless. The instrument is blind to this case because the somatic signal is identical to a live attractor. The standard tell (what feels hard?) returns a false negative. The trap requires no effort and triggers no alarm.

**The Lag Has a Body** — This is the lag interval: the gravitational field persists after conditions shift. The lag is not cognitive delay — the body is still moving toward a target that has moved. The somatic stake in the image's currency is indistinguishable from the somatic stake in a live image; the body is not hedging, it is genuinely oriented. The lag has a body because the pull is embodied, not merely believed.

**Visibility at the Seam** — The seam is the one diagnostic moment: a decision that previously felt obvious begins to feel slightly off before it can be explained. That friction — before it becomes articulable — is the earliest visible signal that the image and the ground have diverged. The seam closes quickly. Once it closes, the pull resumes as before, now re-confirmed rather than questioned. [[attractor_currency]] is the instrument for visiting the seam before it closes.

## Falsifier

Find a system moving consistently toward a future it cannot picture — where no image is present and movement toward a desired state happens anyway. If found, the pattern is not universal. More useful: find a desired future held as a cognitive goal for years without movement, while a less-desired future generated strong pull with minimal effort. What distinguished them? The felt quality of the image, or something else?
