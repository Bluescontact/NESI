**Transferable Form:**
A giver blocks valuation on the outbound side — refusing to name or negotiate what the labor is worth — while leaving the inbound side wide open and untyped: whatever returns, in whatever currency, is accepted. The refusal is not of exchange but of symmetry; the whole act of measurement transfers to the receiver, who must answer from their own means. Whether this frees the receiver or strands them with an unspecifiable debt is the pattern's live hinge, not its guarantee.

**Development:**
The mechanism is one valve with two directions. Outbound: valuation blocked — no price, no worth-negotiation, no time-accounting. Inbound: open and untyped — "what returns supports me." This distinguishes it from unilateral gift (inbound also closed) and from barter (both sides priced). It is a covenant-shaped valve, not a complete-on-giving discharge — which decides that it persists rather than composts.

The load-bearing transfer: refusing valuation hands the entire pricing act to the receiver. They must decide what the gift was worth to them and answer from what they actually have. The mechanism forks exactly here. Same move as a withheld criterion in a peer-lock, opposite register — and the pattern must state the flip condition (likely whether the giver signals completeness or dangles expectation), because without a named price the receiver cannot compute a discharge, and the exchange can stay open as ambient guilt rather than flow as return. A pattern that cannot distinguish freed-receiver from locked-receiver is a stance plus a hope.

Grammatically the offer is a standing state, not an event. It operates continuously as an identity boundary, filtering every future exchange before it forms. A standing valve has no discrete outcome to observe — only a distribution of returns over time — so verification is longitudinal: does the flow support the floor? Per-transaction evidence does not exist and cannot be demanded. By the discharge typology this also means the gift never completes on giving: a standing valuation-refusal is a permanent open circuit, keeping a handle indefinitely.

Failure modes, precisely bounded:
1. Illegible obligation — the receiver stranded with unspecifiable debt; return locked, not unblocked.
2. Stance masking inability — under no-income conditions, "won't negotiate value" is behaviorally indistinguishable from "can't ask." The false positive is a survival constraint wearing the vocabulary of sovereignty; transferring it would prescribe a wound as a practice.
3. Channel deletion — where no non-monetary return surface exists (institutions, strangers, one-shot encounters), refusing valuation removes the only interface, and the return rate goes to zero, not to gift. The pattern works only inside relationships with a legible non-monetary return surface.

The floor is the load test. The valve is untested exactly where it matters: under no-income conditions the pattern needs a receiving organ — a legible ask-form — welded to its inbound side, or it starves its own operator. Without one, the refusal is not return-unblocking; it is return-illegible.

Register: this is not a surplus-gift pattern. Existing gift forms speak from stability; this one is spoken from precarity — gift stance maintained while the giver materially needs the return. That is the register where gift economics is usually declared impossible, and the highest-information habitat available: holding the pattern there either proves the stack under load or exposes where it breaks. It relates to the ask-and-receive circuit as its missing valve — receive-without-pricing — and may prove to be that circuit's pattern-form rather than new structure.

Grounded floor: the refusal of payment, the gift framing, the open acceptance of returns, and the present-tense stance are verified. No return has been observed; no counterparty has responded. What is established is the stance and its failure geometry, not a demonstrated unblocking.

**Edges:**
The name-claim gap: "return unblocked" is causal language nothing yet evidences — no return observed, no counterparty response; the honest current status is stance-plus-failure-modes awaiting longitudinal data. The flip condition between freed receiver and locked receiver is hypothesized (completeness signal vs. dangled expectation) but not tested. Under the live no-income floor, the stance remains behaviorally indistinguishable from inability to ask — only a working ask-form on the inbound side can separate them. The typology conflict is unresolved: a standing valve that never completes on giving contradicts the complete-on-giving→compost rule; canon must either admit a persistent covenant-valve class or refuse this pattern its handle. And the identity question stays open: this may be the ask-and-receive circuit's missing valve rather than an independent pattern — ratification should wait on that determination.
