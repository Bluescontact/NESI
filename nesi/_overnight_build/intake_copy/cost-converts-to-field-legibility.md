## Transferable form

A clean refusal is supposed to send a cost back to the one being refused — they wanted something, they do not get it, the lack lands on them. But when the counterparty has enough power to route around that lack, the cost never settles where you aimed it. It does not disappear. It changes form: the refusal becomes a public reading of the relationship, legible to everyone watching, even though no bill ever arrived at the refuser's door.

## Mechanism

The dyadic model of refusal assumes a two-body system: A refuses, B absorbs the cost of being refused, the cost reflects back to B at the point of contact. This is the barter logic of the no. The model has a hidden dependency: it requires B to be unable to route around the imposed lack. Against a peer this holds. Against power it fails — power is precisely the capacity to not let a refused thing become a lack. The institution finds another supplier, another body, another path; the cost A tried to reflect never settles at origin because origin has the resources to keep it from settling.

The failure of dyadic reflection does not zero the refusal's value. The cost is conserved, not destroyed. It undergoes a phase change — from *dyadic* (a debt owed by B) to *field-level* (a fact now readable by every third party in the surrounding field). What B did under refusal — how it routed around, what it was willing to do to avoid absorbing the no — becomes legible information about B's character, its constraints, its relationship to consent. That legibility is the converted value, and it accrues to the field, not to A.

Two distinct claims, both load-bearing:
- **Never wasted.** There is no configuration of power in which a clean refusal produces zero value. If dyadic reflection lands, A is paid directly. If it fails, the field is informed.
- **Does not pay where the refuser stands.** When conversion to field-legibility occurs, the refuser personally eats the cost of refusing while the value lands in the commons as legibility others can use. A subsidizes the field's knowledge with A's own exposure — a genuine externality, not fair to A.

## Failure modes / boundary conditions

- **No witnesses, no conversion.** A refusal against power with zero third parties present converts into nothing — the cost is absorbed by power and the information dies unread.
- **Legibility requires the refusal to have been clean.** A muddy, hedged, or transactional no publishes ambiguity, which power can recast in its own favor.
- **The refuser cannot direct where the value lands.** The moment A refuses *in order to* generate field-legibility for personal gain, the no is no longer clean and the conversion degrades.
- **Legibility is slow and non-fungible.** Field-legibility is diffuse, delayed, and cannot be cashed by the refuser — it does not pay rent, restore the unmet want, or shield A from retaliation.

## Open edges

- Who owns the converted value — commons are not always able to *use* what they can now read; legibility without leverage is conserved value that still cannot be spent.
- The externality is named but not resolved: is there any structure that returns field-level value to the refuser without corrupting the cleanness of the no, or is the asymmetry (refuser pays, commons receives) irreducible?
- What is the minimum field required for conversion — one witness? A witness who later forgets? Latent legibility that converts only when a future field picks it up?
- Whether the conversion fails the same way against fully opaque power that routes around a refusal invisibly, publishing nothing — this points toward a second axis: not just whether a field is present, but whether power's evasion is itself observable.
