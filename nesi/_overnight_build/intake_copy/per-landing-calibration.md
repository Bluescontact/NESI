## Transferable form

Some systems have no global setting — each entry point must be leveled by hand, one at a time, and a correct setting on one carries zero information about the next. The one that gets skipped is always the least-trafficked, because attention is allocated by demand and emptiness removes a node from the queue. Two people can hold the identical status stamped by the same container and stand on entirely different floors; the shared credential is read as proof the floor beneath it was set, which is precisely how a mis-set landing hides.

## Mechanism

**Calibration is non-transitive.** A building that looks like one continuous shaft is actually N independent instruments that share only a status label. Nothing propagates — leveling on one landing must be re-established by hand at the next. There is no master panel: no dashboard, no config file, no single lever, because correctness is irreducibly distributed handwork.

**The agent.** Nothing sets the basement wrong. The failure agent is the calibration schedule, which allocates attention by ridership. Traffic gets inspected; the basement's emptiness is exactly what removes it from the queue, so low use compounds into low scrutiny. The accountable party is not a person who erred but a routing rule working correctly by its own logic.

**The mask.** The divergence lives entirely in the invisible layer. The status field is uniform, container-issued, portable; the precondition field is per-landing, hand-set, non-portable. Because the visible layer certifies sameness, there is no trigger signature. Status functions as alibi: identical status is taken as evidence of identical reality, not a neutral label but active epistemic cover that certifies the basement was calibrated when it never was.

**The sensor.** The difference is real, invisible at rest, readable only in motion. It cannot be measured at the destination (same endpoint) but is fully encoded in the arrival vector: one enters on a floor of their own, one enters through the basement. A real instrument would read entry-history, not current status.

## Failure modes

1. **False positive — universal basement.** The pattern flags any two-same-status-different-reality gap as an uncalibrated landing, but some gaps are irreducible: the two people brought incommensurable floors no per-landing setting could equalize. Boundary test: is the divergence PRODUCED by the landing, or merely REVEALED there?
2. **Un-set vs. mis-set.** With no named holder of the schedule, "basement never inspected" and "basement deliberately mis-set by whoever benefits" produce identical surface readings. An unowned schedule fails toward "nobody's fault" — exactly the reading a bad-faith calibrator wants.
3. **The self-calibrating rider (domain boundary).** The pattern assumes calibration is done TO the landing by the building. Some riders walk into the uncalibrated basement and set their own floor; for them the schedule's blindness is inert. The pattern bites only where the rider's reality is downstream of the building's calibration.

## Habitat

Native to any system with a single admission gate and no per-arrival calibration: hiring, immigration, membership — any container that stamps one status on everyone but sets each person's actual floor by hand, unrecorded. It lives in the intake event, not steady-state operation. "We treat everyone the same" is the sound of one setting claimed for every floor; equal-status onboarding is the precise site where floors silently diverge.

## Falsifier

If the least-visited node is inspected FIRST, the pattern is not operating.

## Open edges

- The holder is named as absent, not supplied — the pattern can locate WHERE it can be captured (the unowned schedule) but cannot yet name WHO should hold it.
- The cost of a skipped basement is asserted structurally but never priced: who bears it, how much, on what horizon.
- The boundary test (produced-by vs. revealed-at the landing) is stated but not operationalized — no procedure exists for telling the two apart at the moment of reading.
- The arrival-vector sensor points toward reading entry-history rather than current status, but the pattern does not specify what such an instrument would record or how it would survive a container built to erase trajectory into uniform status.
