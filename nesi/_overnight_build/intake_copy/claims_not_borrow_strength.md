# Claims must not borrow strength

## Transferable form

A position holds two claims about the same object — one total and glamorous, one scoped and defensible — joined by a hinge word that means differently in each. The listener hears the shared predicate, not the difference in scope, so the strong claim rides on the weak claim's defensibility while the defense, under pressure, quietly retreats to the weak one. The rule: each claim must carry its own load path — delete either, and the other must still stand on its stated support.

## Mechanism

Scope-slippage under a shared predicate. Two claims share one surface predicate ("no debt" / "no return") while differing only in quantifier width — economic debt vs. any address whatsoever. The transfer requires a hinge word ("gift", "return", "name") that is load-bearing in both claims but means differently in each: name-as-invoice versus name-as-trace. The hinge is the physical joint through which force passes; no shared ambiguous term, no borrowing.

**Topology — intra-position, no external host.** Both claims live inside one body of thought and lend to each other reciprocally: a closed loop with no outside creditor, which is why the position appears self-supporting — the circular loan never shows an external ledger entry. This distinguishes it from authority-harvest patterns (expertise-conscription, provenance-stripping, false-height), which are cross-artifact or cross-person transfers with a visible donor. Here the audit must be internal; there is no host to check the loan against.

**Habitat.** Impossible-standard discourse — pure gift, absolute security, total privacy — anywhere a maximal claim is kept alive as regulative ideal alongside a workable scoped claim. The slippage happens before any handoff, inside one thinker or one document, and the cost lands first on the claim-holder's own coherence. Register: the difference between a vow (total, unfalsifiable-by-instance, identity-bearing) and a policy (scoped, satisfiable, auditable) sharing one name — constitutions and statutes, "do no harm" and dosage guidelines, a standing law and any given session's satisfaction of it.

## Detection

Invisible at rest — in ordinary use the two claims present as one; no provenance tag or authority-altitude check will show it. The load appears only under stress, like a hairline weld: apply the strong claim's own falsifier to the weak claim's instance and watch which one flinches. Equivalently, force explicit quantification of each claim and see whether the position survives fully scoped.

**Test for illicit transfer (against false positives):** borrowing exists only when the defense retreats to the weak claim while the advertised force stays with the strong one. A speaker who stages the strong claim as aspiration and the weak claim as commitment is scaffolding, not borrowing — direction-of-reliance under pressure is the discriminator.

## The deletion test (constructive demand)

Every stated claim, at every scope, must have an independent mechanism that would hold if the sibling claim were deleted. Remove the strong claim; does the weak one still stand on stated support? This makes it a build-time instrument — authoring specs, drafting doctrine — where the neighboring patterns are review-time fraud detectors.

## Boundary clause (failure mode)

Some positions are constitutively ambiguous — the aporia is the content, and forcing a split into weak/strong claims destroys what is being said. Derrida's gift is not two conflated claims but the impossibility of holding either alone. Applied to genuinely aporetic material, the repair becomes a debt-foreclosure move itself. The split is only safe where the position was always secretly two claims.

## Edges

- Jurisdiction boundary with the authority-harvest family: at the edge case — a claim borrowing strength from its author's other famous claim — the two mechanisms are indistinguishable; if every real borrowing routes through a person's standing, this is a special case of that family, not a peer.
- No settled procedure yet for distinguishing constitutively aporetic positions from secretly-two-claims positions before applying the split.
- The direction-of-reliance test requires adversarial pressure; there is no at-rest instrument.
- Generalization predicted, unproven: any hinge term spanning a legal/economic register and an ontological/absolute register (consent, ownership, autonomy, zero-cost) should host the pattern.
- The vow/policy register reads back into the library's own standing laws — several are vows whose session-level satisfactions are policies wearing the vow's name. An internal audit this pattern licenses but has not yet run.
