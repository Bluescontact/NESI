## Transferable form

When a resource feels perpetually insufficient, the felt insufficiency usually lives not in the resource but in the absence of a named floor beneath it. A ceiling marks a limit overhead and leaves everything below it uncertain, generating endless comparison; a floor is a load-bearing surface you can put weight on, and naming one converts the governing question from "how much / can I afford this?" (an unbounded scalar that can never be enough) into "does this serve what stands above the floor?" (a boolean gate). The move only holds when the floor is set from your own lived minimum, by you, under something that actually has a bottom.

## Mechanism

Two geometries wear similar language but do opposite work. A ceiling is referential: it exists only relative to what is below it, marks a limit overhead, and transmits no load — it generates infinite subordinate uncertainty because every point below it is a comparison. A floor is load-bearing: its whole function is that weight rests ON it, so it produces standing rather than measuring. The differentiator is bearing-vs-referring, not height.

The core operation is a type-change, not an addition of resource. Before the floor, the resource is read as a continuous scalar — more/less, never enough, because "enough" is a threshold word and a scalar has no natural stopping point. Naming the floor installs a partition: below (the field does not exist yet) and above (every unit is now free to be evaluated for fit rather than survival). This is the exact remedy-move of the manufactured-deficit loop: the deficit loop is a scalar with no gate that can never discharge; the floor is the gate installed. Same axis, opposite operation.

**Setting.** A floor is not chosen, it is found — by subtraction to bedrock, descending until you hit the lowest configuration you have already survived on, then naming that as a surface rather than a fear. A floor set aspirationally — from above, as a target — inverts into a ceiling, because nothing has verified it can bear weight.

**Authorship.** A floor set by the one who must stand on it is load-bearing; a floor set by another and imposed downward is a ceiling in disguise. The same line liberates when self-authored and subordinates when other-authored.

**Register.** The geometry is resource-agnostic — it arrives dressed in money but recurs wherever a bounded resource feels perpetually insufficient: attention, time, energy, social capacity, trust. Money is its most legible costume; the floor is a topological move.

## Failure modes

1. **The ceiling wearing floor-clothes.** "I will never spend more than X" becomes "X is my floor" by rhetorical inversion alone. Behavioral test: does the named line CLOSE options above it or OPEN them?
2. **The floor set at the ceiling.** The move only produces a field when the floor sits LOW relative to resource, leaving headroom. Set near or above available resource, everything is "below the floor," and the anxiety returns wearing the floor's name.
3. **No genuine minimum exists.** Some resources have no floor to find — a progressive illness, a collapsing income, a grief with no bottom. Applying floor-setting here draws a comforting line under something still falling; it breaks and takes the standing-place with it.
4. **Naming without building.** A floor named but not materially secured is a floor drawn on air — curing the feeling can leave the ground unbuilt.

## Somatic boundary

The floor operates entirely in the framing register. It is adjacent to but distinct from body-gatekeeping: a floor can be structurally correct and somatically unratified. The frame sets the gate; felt-safety must occupy it. A floor nobody can somatically stand on is a diagram of a floor, not a floor.

## Falsifier

The behavioral test named in failure mode 1 (does the line open or close what's above it) is named but not operationalized — no clean procedure exists for running it on a line you have already told yourself is a floor.

## Open edges

- No test yet distinguishes case 3 (real scarcity) from case 4 (unbuilt floor) from the inside — both present as accurate insufficiency-signals.
- The somatic-ratification gap has no bridge — nothing here converts a named floor into a stood-on floor.
- The authorship axis predicts that imposed minimums (benefits floor, minimum wage, a rule) function as floor or ceiling depending on whether the one beneath experiences ground or a cap — but the pattern does not yet say what makes an imposed line inhabitable as ground rather than felt as a cap.
