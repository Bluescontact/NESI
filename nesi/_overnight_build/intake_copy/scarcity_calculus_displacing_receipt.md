## Transferable form

When a nervous system organized by scarcity contacts a gift, it reads the gift's surplus as an asymmetry signal — not abundance but leverage the giver now holds. Before reception can form, a calculus fires: "what do they want from me?" This calculation is not a response to the gift; it is a substitute for receiving it. The gift arrives into a room already occupied by debt-ledger logic, and receipt never occurs.

## Mechanism

Gift contact triggers surplus-detection. For a scarcity-tuned nervous system, surplus reads as anomaly — an asymmetry requiring explanation before it can be inhabited. The question "what do they want from me?" is the instantiation of a liability ledger. The moment that ledger opens, the gift has been converted into a contract with unknown terms, and the only safe move is to discover those terms before they are called. Receipt requires an unoccupied window — a moment before meaning is assigned — and the calculus is pre-loaded, closing that window before it opens. The substitution is pre-emptive, not reactive: calculation does not follow failed receipt, it forecloses the receptor site before receipt can begin.

Once the ledger is open, the interaction continues under debt-management protocol. The calculus is a closed epistemic system — internally coherent indefinitely, unable to self-correct toward receipt, because receipt was never the frame. This is why the pattern is stable and self-reinforcing rather than episodic.

**Activation condition:** surplus, not gift-ness. A small, bounded, low-stakes transfer may not fire the calculus even in a fully scarcity-tuned receiver. The Extractor-calculus is a threshold phenomenon, not a stable orientation — the activation gate is the degree to which surplus reads as leverage.

## Falsifier (grounded, re-screen 2026-07-15)

**Functional receipt:** the receiver's next action is drawn from the space of "what to do with this now that I have it" (use, enjoyment, passing it forward) rather than from the space of "what does the giver want in return" (accounting, anticipated obligation, reading their expectations).

**Observable test:** in the receiver's very next substantive response to the gift, does the subject of any future-tense verb belong to the gift's own possibilities, or to the giver's intent/feelings/expectations? The first is receipt; the second is the calculus firing in receipt's place.

This also sorts the self-defense edge: high-socialization "care" calculus and scarcity-driven calculus produce the same observable (giver-directed accounting), but the pattern doesn't need to tell them apart to detect displacement — it only needs to detect whether the gift itself, not the giver, ever became the object of the receiver's attention.

## Failure modes

- **Cannot distinguish scarcity-driven calculation from high-socialization attunement** by mechanism alone — someone trained that receiving without reciprocity harms the giver runs the same calculus as a form of care, not threat-mapping. Discriminant: not the calculation itself but what it forecloses.
- **No downstream behavioral signature required** — a receiver who runs the calculus may still enact the functionally appropriate response, making the displacement invisible from outside without the falsifier above.
- **Boundary condition:** in dependency relationships, economic subordination, or coercive contexts, the calculus is structural self-defense — the only form of receipt that preserves autonomy. The pattern pathologizes a survival instrument if it fails to account for contexts where calculation before receipt is rational.

## Edges

- The activation-threshold finding (surplus, not gift-ness) may redraw the pattern's boundary — if the actual mechanism is "ambiguous obligation detection" rather than "gift contact," the pattern may describe a subset of a larger social-contract-reading apparatus, not a gift-specific phenomenon.
- The self-defense edge: the pattern needs a lever for distinguishing calculus-as-displacement from calculus-as-the-only-safe-receipt — resolved in part by the functional-receipt falsifier above, but the boundary in coercive contexts remains a live design question.
