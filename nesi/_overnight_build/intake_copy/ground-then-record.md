## Transferable form

Admitting someone to a floor is a two-part act: leveling the ground so it can hold their weight, and writing the record that says the ground is set. These are two outputs of one event, but they emit separately — the record fires on a word or a write, near-free; the calibration fires only on performed labor. Where nothing wires the two levers together, any pressure to produce landings quickly optimizes toward the cheap half, and the record gets published over ground that was never leveled.

## Mechanism

Two emitters, one event. Calibration and record are not first-step and second-step of a single act; they are two outputs of one admission that can be emitted independently. The record has its own emitter — a signature, a database write, a spoken "you're in" — that fires on command. Calibration's emitter is bound to physical labor and fires only when the work is done. A physical elevator won't run misset — the ground interlocks the record. A title, a membership, a seat has no such interlock; the levers are free.

The cost asymmetry drives the skip. The record is near-free to emit; the calibration carries the full cost of physical leveling. Under any pressure to produce landings quickly, the system optimizes toward the cheap half and the costly half silently drops out — not by decision but by gradient. This is emergent from the gradient, not authored, which is the seam that separates a misset floor from a deliberately harvested one.

The gap is a latency, not a hiddenness. The record is legible by construction. The calibration is legible only at the moment of load, when weight comes onto the floor. Between grant and load, a properly set floor and a misset one show the same green record — the failure surfaces exactly when someone steps on. This is why misset floors are discovered by the person who falls through them.

The record is load-bearing in the wrong way. A granted record does structural work whether or not the ground was leveled — people route weight onto it, plan against it, stand where it says to stand. So the record actively converts an absent floor into a trap by inviting the load the ground can't carry. Calibration-without-record fails safe (a level floor nobody trusts, so nobody steps — inert). Record-without-calibration fails dangerous (an untrusted-but-published landing that draws weight).

The boundary against a stated-condition gate: calibration is a performance, not a specification. A gate is satisfied by naming its condition; a floor cannot be set by naming flatness. Any register where granting is speech and grounding is labor instantiates this pattern. Where both halves are speech, it collapses into a stated-condition gate and this pattern does not apply.

**Symmetry of the two deletions.** The reverse defect — ground genuinely leveled, no record written — is the same class of break, often the more corrosive one: the person who did the work and stands on level ground that the ledger never wrote a landing for falls through a floor that IS set, because the system does not know it is there.

**Negligence versus harvest.** The state (record present, calibration absent) has two metabolisms behind it. Negligence: ground is invisible, status is visible, the cheap visible half gets done. Harvest: the ungrounded status is deliberately kept ungrounded for positional leverage. The distinction is not taxonomy — it is remedy. You fix negligence by making the ground visible; you cannot fix harvest that way, because the harvester's incentive is precisely to keep it invisible.

## Habitat

The onboarding moment — the instant a system admits a newcomer: the welcome email, the added-to-Slack, keys handed over, the seat named. The load-bearing name for inclusion-without-precondition: membership without accommodation, the title without the ramp, the scholarship without the housing. In any measured system the two-part structure is inverted: the record becomes the reward surface (membership count, placement number), so the incentive gradient actively pulls toward records without calibrations.

## Failure modes

Invisibility sits in position, not artifact. The calibration is felt only from the newcomer's standing weight. The grantor experiences only the record-side; only the grantee's body is the calibration instrument.

## Open edges

- The pattern presumes a material substrate that pre-exists the record. It false-positives on every speech-act container (legal recognition, a name spoken) where the declaration IS the leveling. The boundary between the two ontologies is not yet drawn from the inside — it currently reads only by outcome.
- Negligence vs. harvest gives no in-situ test to tell them apart before remedy is attempted — the footprint is identical, and the discriminator (whose interest keeps the ground invisible) is exactly what a harvester conceals.
- If only the grantee's body is the calibration instrument, the pattern points toward but does not build the feedback path by which a felt read from the standing position reaches the grantor who holds the leveling authority.
